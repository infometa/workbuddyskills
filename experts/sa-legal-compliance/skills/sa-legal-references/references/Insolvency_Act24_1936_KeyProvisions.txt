KEY PROVISIONS - QUICK REFERENCE
================================================================================

VOLUNTARY SURRENDER (ss2-8):
  - s2: Applicant may petition court for acceptance of surrender
  - s3: Contents of surrender petition (statement of affairs, cause of insolvency)
  - s4: Notice of surrender (Gazette + creditor notice 14-30 days before hearing)
  - s5: Consequences of publication of notice (stay of sales in execution)
  - s6: Meeting of creditors, election of trustee
  - s7: Court order accepting surrender
  - s8: Setting aside of surrender order

COMPULSORY SEQUESTRATION (ss8-12):
  - s8: Acts of insolvency (8 categories):
    (a) Absence from Republic with intent to evade debts
    (b) Failure to satisfy judgment debt after court order/notice
    (c) Disposition of property prejudicing creditors
    (d) Removal of property with intent to prejudice creditors
    (e) Arrangement with creditors for release of debts
    (f) Failure to apply for surrender when estate is insolvent
    (g) Notice of inability to pay debts (de facto insolvency)
    (h) Being a trader, failure to deliver statement of affairs upon application
  - s9: Application for sequestration (creditor petition)
  - s10: Provisional sequestration order
  - s11: Final sequestration order (court may grant after nisi order)
  - s12: Security for costs

EFFECTS OF SEQUESTRATION (ss13-25):
  - s13: Master to appoint curator bonis (pending trustee appointment)
  - s14: Stay of proceedings against estate
  - s15: Property attachments become void on insolvency
  - s16: Insolvent to deliver books and records
  - s17: Curator to take estate into custody
  - s18: Meetings of creditors
  - s19: Rights of employees and workers
  - s20: Effects on contracts (continuation, termination, damages claims)
  - s21: Property vesting in trustee
  - s22: Insolvent's personal earnings (portion protected)
  - s23: Property excluded from insolvent estate (personal items, tools of trade up to limit)
  - s24: Insolvent's right to maintenance
  - s25: Insolvent may not contract debts without disclosing insolvency status

IMPEDABLE DISPOSITIONS — SETTING ASIDE (ss26-34):
  - s26: Dispositions WITHOUT VALUE (Disposition not made for value) — 
    * 2 YEARS before sequestration: court may set aside
    * Onus on person claiming under disposition
  - s27: Dispositions having effect of preferring creditors — voidable
  - s28: Dispositions as fraudem creditorum (intention to prefer)
  - s29: VOIDABLE PREFERENCES (Undue preference):
    * 6 MONTHS before sequestration: if disposition gave creditor preference
    * Debtor's liabilities exceeded assets at time of disposition
    * Creditor to return what was received UNLESS bona fide
  - s30: Collusive dealings voidable (collusion between debtor and creditor)
  - s31: Undue preference to creditors by trader
  - s32: Proceedings to set aside dispositions
  - s33: Protection of bona fide purchasers for value
  - s34: Transfers of business or goodwill before sequestration

TRUSTEE APPOINTMENT AND ADMINISTRATION (ss35-79):
  - ss35-55: Trustee appointment, election, removal, security, remuneration
  - s56: Trustee to take possession of estate property
  - ss57-64: Realisation of estate assets (sale of property, auction procedures)
  - ss65-79: Accounts, distributions, meetings, trustee duties and reports

PROOF OF CLAIMS AND SET-OFF (ss80-94):
  - ss80-85: Procedure for proving claims against estate
  - s86: Claims provable (all debts due at sequestration + contingent claims)
  - ss87-94: Admission, rejection, review of claims

PRIORITY OF CLAIMS / DISTRIBUTION ORDER (ss95-103):
  - s95: Priority ranking (first claim category to last):
    1. Funeral and death-bed expenses (R300 limit)
    2. Costs of sequestration (Master's fees, curator/trustee costs)
    3. Costs of execution levied within 1 month before sequestration
    4. Salary/wages of employees (S98A: up to R12,000 per employee, for 3 months prior)
    5. PAYE, UIF, SDL deductions from employees
    6. VAT payable (s99)
    7. Workmen's compensation assessments
    8. Taxes (income tax) and other state claims
    9. Secured creditors (to extent of security)
    10. Preferent creditors under any statute
    11. Concurrent creditors (pari passu - pro rata)
  - s96: Secured creditors (realisation of security, proof for deficiency)
  - s97: Interest on claims stops running at date of sequestration
  - s98: Costs of creditors proving claims
  - s98A: EMPLOYEE CLAIMS PRIORITY:
    * Wages/salary up to R12,000 per employee
    * Leave pay, severance pay, notice pay
    * For services rendered during 3 months before sequestration
    * Also: amounts due in terms of pension/provident/medical aid fund
    * Employee can claim directly from estate (not just via UIF/CCMA)
  - s99: VAT preference
  - s100: Distribution of surplus (after all debts paid, surplus to insolvent)
  - s101: Contribution by creditors
  - s102: Second and subsequent accounts
  - s103: Creditors may prove after second account (late claims)

COMPOSITION AND REHABILITATION (ss119-129):
  - s119: Offer of composition (debtor may offer composition to creditors)
  - s120: Meeting to consider composition
  - s121: Confirmation by court
  - s122: Failure to comply = automatic sequestration
  - ss124-129: REHABILITATION (applicable after certain period):
    * Automatic after 10 years
    * Court application after 4 years (if authorised by Master)
    * Earlier if creditors paid in full, or composition accepted
    * s127: Court order setting aside rehabilitation

MISCELLANEOUS AND PENALTIES (ss130-158):
  - ss130-131: Partnership and marriage in community of property
  - ss132-143: OFFENCES AND PENALTIES:
    * Fraudulent concealment of property
    * Failure to keep proper books of account (traders)
    * Obtaining credit without disclosing insolvency
    * Disposing of property after sequestration
    * Destroying/falsifying books
    * Concealing property from trustee
    * Penalties: fines and imprisonment (varying by offence)
  - ss144-158: General provisions, regulations, legal proceedings

(see also section 158ter  of Act, inserted by Act 99 of 1965 and  
deemed to have come into force on 1 July 1943)  
 
APPLICABILITY TO SOUTH WEST AFRICA: The Act was initially applied to South West Africa by the 
Insolvency Amendment Act 16 of 1943, which stated in section 37: “ The principal Act as amended by 
this Act, shall apply to the mandated territory of South- West Africa and the port and settlement of Walvis 
Bay, and for the purposes of such application the said port and settlement shall be deemed to be a portion of the said mandated territory.” Act 16 of 1943 also made various amendments to Act 24 of 1936 to 
make it appropriate to South West Africa, in cluding defining “ Union ” to include “ the mandated territory 
of South -West Africa ”. Section 39 of Act 16 of 1943 stated “ This Act shall be called the Insolvency Law 
Amendment Act, 1943, and shall come into operation on a date to be fixed by the Governor -General by 
proclamation in the Gazette , in so far as its application to the mandated territory of South- West Africa and 
the port and settlement of Walvis Bay is concerned, but shall otherwise be in force as from the date of 
promulgation [16 April 1943] .” Act 16 of 1943  was brought into force in South West Africa on 1 July 
1943 by SA Proc. 112/1943  (SA GG 3209).  Although Act 16 of 1943  did not refer to the application of 
subsequent amendment s of the Act to South West Africa, they appear to have applied by virtue of 
the definition of “Union”  (and later “ Republic ”) to include South West Africa . Act 99 of 1965  
subsequently made this clear by inserting s ection 158ter , which was  deemed to have come into 
operation on 1 July 1943  and which  states “ This Act and any amendment thereof shall apply also in the 
Territory, including that portion of the Territory known as the Eastern Caprivi Zipfel and referred to in sub-section (3) of section three of the South West Africa Affairs Amendment Act, 1951 (Act No. 55 of 1951).”  
(Act 99 of 1965 also repealed section 37 of Act 16 of 1943.)  
 TRANSFER TO SOUTH WEST AFRICA:  The administration of this Act was transferred to South West 
Africa by the Executive Powers (Justice) Transfer Proclamation (AG 33/1979), dated 12 November 
1979.  One amendment to the Act in South Af rica after the date of transfer, the Insolvency Amendment 
Act 78 of 1980  (RSA GG 7083) , was made expressly applicable to S outh West Africa by the South 
West Africa Insolvency Amendment Act 18  of 1980.  None of the other South African amendments 
after the date of transfer and prior to Namibian independence were applicable to South West Africa because none were made expressly so applicable.  
 Section 3(1)(a) of the transfer proclamation excluded the references to the Republic in the Act from the operation of section 3(1) of the Executive Powers Transfer (General Provisions) Proclamation, AG 7 of 1977,  except where this word occurs for the second and third times in section 148 of the 
Act. This means that , prior to Namibian independence, the designated references to the Republic in 
section 148 were to be construed as references to South West Africa only, while elsewhere the term retained the meaning given to it in the definition section of the Act (South Africa and South West Africa ).

Acts of insolvency  
 
8. A debtor  commits  an act of insolvency  - 
 
(a) if he leaves the Republic  or being out of the Republic  remains absent therefrom, or  
departs  from  his dwelling or otherwise absents himself, with intent by so doing to 
evade or delay the payment  of his debts ; 
 
(b) if a Court has given judgment against him and he fails, upon the demand of the 
officer whose duty  it is to execute  that judgment, to satisf y it or to indicate to that 
officer disposabl e property sufficient to satisf y it, or if it appears from the return 
made by that officer that he has  not found sufficient  disposable property to  satisfy  
the judgment ; 
 
(c) if he makes or attempts to  make  any disposition  of any of his property  which  has or 
would have  the eff ect of prejudicing  his creditors  or of preferring one  creditor  above  
another ; 
 
(d)  if he removes or attempts to remove any of his property with intent to pre judice his 
creditors or to pref er one creditor  above  another ; 
 
(e)  if he makes or off ers to make any arrangement with any of  his creditors for releasing 
him wholly or partially  from  his debts ; 
 (f) if, after having published a notice of surrender of his estate which has not lapsed or 
been withdrawn  in terms of section six or seven, he fails to comply with the 
requirements of sub -section (3) of section four or lodges, in terms of that sub- section, 
a statement which is incorrect  or incomplete  in any material respect or fails to apply 
for the acceptance  of the surrender of his estate on the date mentioned in the aforesaid 
notice as the date on which such application is to be made ; 
 
(g) if he gives notice  in writing to any one of his creditors that he is unable to pay any 
of his debts ;
