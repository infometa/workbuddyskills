SOUTH AFRICAN COMPETITION ACT, NO. 89 OF 1998
KEY PROVISIONS SUMMARY
============================================================

OVERVIEW
--------
The Competition Act 89 of 1998 establishes the legal framework for competition
regulation in South Africa. It creates three institutions:
  - Competition Commission (investigative and enforcement)
  - Competition Tribunal (adjudicative)
  - Competition Appeal Court (appellate review)

Assented to: 20 October 1998
Published: 30 October 1998 (Government Gazette No. 19412)

PURPOSE (Section 2)
-------------------
To promote and maintain competition in SA to:
  - Promote efficiency, adaptability and development of the economy
  - Provide consumers with competitive prices and product choices
  - Promote employment and advance social and economic welfare
  - Expand opportunities for SA participation in world markets
  - Ensure SMMEs have equitable opportunity to participate
  - Promote greater spread of ownership, particularly for HDPs

CHAPTER 1: DEFINITIONS (Sections 1-3)
--------------------------------------
Key definitions:
  "Prohibited practice": practice prohibited under Chapter 2
  "Merger": direct/indirect acquisition or establishment of control
  "Dominant firm": >= 45% market share (irrebuttable)
    or >= 35% but < 45% (rebuttable presumption)
  "Small merger": below threshold set by Minister
  "Intermediate merger": between small and large thresholds
  "Large merger": above threshold set by Minister

CHAPTER 2: PROHIBITED PRACTICES (Sections 4-10)
-------------------------------------------------
Section 4: Restrictive Horizontal Practices (PER SE prohibited):
  - Price fixing between competitors
  - Market division/allocation between competitors
  - Collusive tendering (bid rigging)
  - Fixing of trading conditions

Section 5: Restrictive Vertical Practices:
  - Minimum resale price maintenance (per se prohibited)
  - Other vertical agreements subject to rule of reason

Section 7: Dominant firm thresholds:
  - Irrebuttable: market share >= 45%
  - Rebuttable: market share >= 35% but < 45%

Section 8: Abuse of Dominance (prohibited):
  - Excessive pricing to the detriment of consumers
  - Refusal to give access to essential facility
  - Exclusionary acts: refusal to supply, tying/bundling,
    predatory pricing, margin squeeze, etc.

Section 9: Price discrimination by dominant seller (prohibited)

Section 10: Exemptions - apply to Commission; agreement must
  contribute to specific objectives

CHAPTER 3: MERGER CONTROL (Sections 11-18)
--------------------------------------------
Section 12: Merger = acquisition/establishment of control
Section 13: Intermediate/large mergers MUST notify Commission
  - Small mergers: voluntary notification; prior implementation OK
  - Prior implementation prohibited pending approval for others
Section 14: Intermediate merger - Commission decides within 30 days
Section 15: Large merger - Commission investigates, Tribunal decides
Section 16: Public interest factors:
  - Effect on industrial sector/region
  - Effect on employment
  - Effect on SMMEs and HDP-controlled firms
  - International competitiveness of national industries

CHAPTER 4: COMPETITION INSTITUTIONS (Sections 19-43)
------------------------------------------------------
PART A - Competition Commission (Sec 19-24):
  Independent body; Commissioner + Deputy appointed by Minister
  Functions: investigate, evaluate mergers, grant exemptions

PART B - Competition Tribunal (Sec 25-35):
  Chairperson + 6-10 members appointed by President
  Adjudicates prohibited practices and large mergers

PART C - Competition Appeal Court (Sec 36-38):
  Division of High Court; Judge President + judges
  Hears appeals/reviews of Tribunal decisions

PART D - Financing (Sec 40-43): parliamentary appropriation, fees

CHAPTER 5: INVESTIGATION & ADJUDICATION (Sections 44-54)
---------------------------------------------------------
Section 44: Complaint by any person or Commission own initiative
Section 49B-50: Commission must refer to Tribunal or non-refer
Section 52-54: Tribunal hearings; summons powers; document production

CHAPTER 6: PENALTIES & REMEDIES (Sections 55-68)
--------------------------------------------------
Section 59: Tribunal orders - interdict, divestiture, void conduct
Section 60: Administrative penalties for Chapter 2 violations:
  - MAX: 10% of annual turnover in SA
  - Repeat offence: up to 25%
Section 62: Merger contravention penalty - up to 10% of turnover
Section 63: Consent orders (Commission + respondent, confirmed by Tribunal)

CHAPTER 7: OFFENCES (Sections 69-74)
-------------------------------------
Section 69: Confidentiality breach
Section 70: Hindering/obstructing investigation
Section 71: Failure to attend when summoned
Section 72: Perjury (false statements under oath)
Section 73: Contravention of Tribunal order
Section 74: Penalties - fine up to R500,000 or imprisonment up to
  10 years (or both); directors personally liable

CHAPTER 8: GENERAL PROVISIONS (Sections 75-84)
-----------------------------------------------
Section 79: Commission may publish guidelines
Section 82: President may assign duties to Commission

KEY COMPLIANCE CHECKPOINTS
---------------------------
1. NO cartel conduct (price fixing, market division, bid rigging)
   -> PER SE prohibited; criminal sanctions apply
2. If market share > 35%: assess dominance risk
3. Notify Commission before intermediate/large mergers
4. Consider public interest in M&A (employment, HDP, SMMEs)
5. Maintain antitrust compliance program
6. Respond promptly to Commission info requests
7. Max penalty: 10% turnover (25% repeat); criminal liability
8. Corporate Leniency Policy available for cartel whistle-blowers

============================================================
END OF SUMMARY

ACT
To provide for the establishment of a Competition Commission responsible for the
investigation, control and evaluation of restrictive practices, abuse of dominant
position, and mergers; and for the establishment of a Competition Tribunal
responsible to adjudicate such matters; and for the establishment of a Competition
Appeal Court; and for related matters.
PREAMBLE
The people of South Africa recognise:
That apartheid and other discriminatory laws and practices of the past resulted
in excessive concentrations of ownership and control within the national
economy, weak enforcement of anti-competitive trade practices. and unjust
restrictions on full and free participation in the economy by all South Africans.
That the economy must be open to greater ownership by a greater number of
South Africans.
That credible competition law, and effective structures to administer that law.
are necessary for an efficient functioning economy.
That an eflicient, competitive economic environment. balancing the interests
of workers, owners and consumers and focussed on development. will benefit
all South Africans.
IN ORDER TO—
provide all South Africans equal opportunity to participate fairly in the
national economy;
achieve a more effective and efficient economy in South Africa:
provide for markets in which consumers have access to, and can freely select.
the quality and variety of goods and services they desire:
create greater capability and an environment for South Africans to compete
effectively in international markets:
restrain particular trade practices which undermine a competitive economy:
regulate the transfer of economic ownership  in keeping  with the public
interest:
establish independent institutions to monitor economic competition: and
~ive etiect to the international law obligations of the Republic.
