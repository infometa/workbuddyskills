============================================================
SOUTH AFRICAN PREVENTION AND COMBATING OF CORRUPT ACTIVITIES ACT
(PRECCA) NO. 12 OF 2004
KEY PROVISIONS SUMMARY
============================================================

OVERVIEW
--------
The Prevention and Combating of Corrupt Activities Act (PRECCA) is the primary
anti-corruption legislation in South Africa. It creates a comprehensive framework
of corruption offences, investigative measures, and penalties.

Published: 28 April 2004 (Government Gazette No. 26311)
Assented to: 27 April 2004
Also referred to as: Act No. 12 of 2004, Prevention and Combating of Corrupt
  Activities Act, 2003 (year of adoption)

CHAPTER 1: DEFINITIONS AND INTERPRETATION (Sections 1-2)
--------------------------------------------------------
Key definitions (Section 1):
  - "Gratification": VERY broadly defined to include:
    (a) Money (cash or otherwise)
    (b) Any donation, gift, loan, fee, reward, valuable security, property or 
        interest in property (movable or immovable)
    (c) Avoidance of loss, liability, penalty, forfeiture, punishment, disadvantage
    (d) Any office, status, honour, employment, contract, residential accommodation
    (e) Payment, release, discharge or liquidation of any loan or obligation
    (f) Forbearance to demand money or valuable thing
    (g) Any service, favour or advantage of any description, including protection 
        from penalty or proceedings (disciplinary, civil, criminal)
    (h) Any right or privilege
    (i) Any real or pretended aid, vote, consent, influence or abstention from voting
    (j) Any valuable consideration or benefit, including discount, commission, 
        rebate, bonus, deduction or percentage
  
  - "Public officer": any person who is a member, officer, employee or servant of
    any public body
  - "Foreign public official": holder of legislative/administrative/judicial office 
    of foreign state; person performing public functions for foreign state; 
    official/agent of public international organisation
  - "Agent": authorised representative acting on behalf of principal, including 
    directors, officers, employees
  - "Judicial officer": Constitutional Court judges, High Court judges, Labour Court
    judges, Land Claims Court judges, Competition Appeal Court judges, magistrates
  - "Member of legislative authority": Parliament, provincial legislatures, 
    municipal councils

CHAPTER 2: OFFENCES IN RESPECT OF CORRUPT ACTIVITIES (Sections 3-21)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Part 1: General offence of corruption (Section 3)
  - General, all-encompassing corruption offence
  - Any person who directly or indirectly accepts/gives/offers/agrees to accept 
    any gratification from/to any other person, in order to act in a manner that 
    amounts to illegal, dishonest, unauthorised, incomplete or biased conduct, 
    or that is designed to achieve an unjustified result
  - "Giver" and "receiver" BOTH guilty
  - Covers: abuse of position, breach of trust, violation of duty

Part 2: Offences relating to SPECIFIC PERSONS (Sections 4-9)
  - Section 4: Public officers — giving/accepting gratification to act/influence
  - Section 5: Foreign public officials — bribery of foreign officials criminalised
    (aligns with OECD Anti-Bribery Convention and UNCAC)
  - Section 6: Agents — corrupt activities involving agents/principals
  - Section 7: Members of legislative authority — National Assembly, NCOP, 
    provincial legislatures, municipal councils
  - Section 8: Judicial officers — judges and magistrates
  - Section 9: Members of prosecuting authority — NPA prosecutors

Part 3: Unauthorised gratification in employment (Section 10)
  - Receiving or offering unauthorised gratification by/to party in employment
    relationship

Part 4: Offences relating to SPECIFIC MATTERS (Sections 11-16)
  - Section 11: Witnesses and evidential material during proceedings
  - Section 12: Contracts — corrupt activities in procurement/contracting
  - Section 13: Procuring and withdrawal of tenders
  - Section 14: Auctions — corrupt activities at auctions
  - Section 15: Sporting events — match-fixing, corrupt manipulation
  - Section 16: Gambling games or games of chance

Part 5: Conflict of interest and unacceptable conduct (Sections 17-19)
  - Section 17: Public officer acquiring private interest in contract/agreement/
    investment of a public body (CONFLICT OF INTEREST OFFENCE)
    * Must be authorised in writing by NDPP before prosecution
  - Section 18: Unacceptable conduct relating to witnesses
  - Section 19: Intentional interference with, hindering or obstructing 
    investigation of offence

Part 6: Other offences (Sections 20-21)
  - Section 20: Accessory to or after an offence — dealing with proceeds of 
    corruption, knowing them to be gratification from an offence
  - Section 21: Attempt, conspiracy and inducing another person to commit 
    an offence under PRECCA

CHAPTER 3: INVESTIGATIONS REGARDING POSSESSION OF PROPERTY (Sections 22-23)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Section 22: Investigation regarding property relating to corrupt activities
    * National Director may institute investigation under Chapter 5 of NPA Act
    * Applies to property connected to commission of Chapter 2 offence, facilitated
      commission, or that is proceeds of such offence
  - Section 23: Investigation direction for property disproportionate to known 
    sources of income/assets
    * Judge may issue investigation direction on application
    * Must be satisfied investigation will reveal relevant information
    * Powers include: summon suspects, require documents, search premises

CHAPTER 4: PRESUMPTIONS AND DEFENCES (Sections 24-25)
-----------------------------------------------------
  - Section 24: Presumptions — proof of gratification given/received creates 
    presumption of corrupt intent, rebuttable by accused
  - Section 25: Defences — accused may raise specific defences including 
    that gratification was not offered/accepted corruptly

CHAPTER 5: PENALTIES AND RELATED MATTERS (Sections 26-28)
----------------------------------------------------------
Section 26: Penalties
  (1)(a) Conviction under Part 1, 2, 3, 4 or section 18:
    - High Court: fine OR imprisonment FOR LIFE
    - Regional Court: fine OR imprisonment up to 18 YEARS
    - Magistrate's Court: fine OR imprisonment up to 5 YEARS
  
  (1)(b) Conviction under s17(1), 19, 20, 23(7), 34(2):
    - High Court/Regional Court: fine OR imprisonment up to 10 YEARS
    - Magistrate's Court: fine OR imprisonment up to 3 YEARS
  
  (1)(c) Conviction under s28(6)(b):
    - Fine of R250,000 OR imprisonment up to 3 YEARS
  
  (3) ADDITIONAL FINE: The court may impose a fine equal to FIVE TIMES the 
      value of the gratification involved in the offence

Section 27: Authorisation to prosecute
  - Prosecution for s17(1), 23(7)(b) or 34(2) requires WRITTEN AUTHORISATION 
    by National Director, Deputy National Director, or DPP
  - Accused must first be given reasonable opportunity to explain

Section 28: Endorsement of Register (Tender Defaulters)
  - Court convicting under s12 or 13 (contract/tender offences) may order 
    endorsement on Register for Tender Defaulters
  - Endorsement applies to convicted person AND enterprise involved, 
    as well as enterprises that knew or ought to have known of offence
  - Register endorsement remains indefinitely / for court-determined period
  - Person must disclose endorsement when dealing with state entities

CHAPTER 6: REGISTER FOR TENDER DEFAULTERS (Sections 29-33)
-----------------------------------------------------------
  - Section 29: Minister of Finance must establish Register within 6 months
  - Section 30: Designation of Registrar by Minister of Finance
  - Section 31: Powers, duties and functions of Registrar (maintain Register, 
    record court endorsements)
  - Section 32: Register open to the PUBLIC as prescribed
  - Section 33: Minister may make regulations re: maintenance, access, 
    safe-keeping; penalty for contravention: fine or imprisonment up to 12 months

CHAPTER 7: MISCELLANEOUS MATTERS (Sections 34-37)
--------------------------------------------------
Section 34: DUTY TO REPORT CORRUPT TRANSACTIONS (CRITICAL)
  (1) ANY PERSON holding a POSITION OF AUTHORITY who knows or ought 
      reasonably to know/suspect that another person has committed:
    (a) Offence under Part 1-4, s20 or s21 of Chapter 2; OR
    (b) Theft, fraud, extortion, forgery or uttering involving R100,000 OR MORE
    MUST REPORT such knowledge/suspicion to any police official.
  
  (2) FAILURE TO REPORT = criminal offence.
  
  (4) "Position of authority" includes:
    - DG/head of national or provincial department
    - Municipal manager
    - Any public officer in Senior Management Service
    - Head/rector/principal of tertiary institution
    - Manager, secretary or director of a company (including CC members)
    - Executive manager of bank or financial institution
    - Partner in a partnership
    - CEO or equivalent of any agency, board, commission, etc.
    - Any person responsible for overall management of employer's business
    - Persons acting in above capacities

Section 35: EXTRATERRITORIAL JURISDICTION
  (1) South African courts have jurisdiction over PRECCA offences committed 
      OUTSIDE the Republic if the accused is:
    (a) SA citizen
    (b) Ordinarily resident in SA
    (c) Arrested in SA territory/waters/registered ship or aircraft
    (d) Company incorporated in SA
    (e) Body of persons (corporate or unincorporate) in SA
  
  (2) Non-SA persons can also be prosecuted if:
    (a) Act affects or is intended to affect a public body, business or person 
        in South Africa; AND
    (b) Person is found in South Africa; AND
    (c) Person is not extradited
  
  (3) Offence deemed committed in SA if any part of the offence occurred in SA

Section 36: Repeal and amendment of laws, transitional provisions
  - Repeals the Corruption Act 94/1992
  - Amends Schedule 2 to various Acts (POCA, RICA, etc.)
  
Section 37: Short title and commencement
  - "Prevention and Combating of Corrupt Activities Act, 2004"
  - Commences on date determined by Presidential proclamation

SCHEDULE: LAWS AMENDED
-----------------------
  Amends 18+ statutes including:
  - Prevention of Organised Crime Act (POCA)
  - Regulation of Interception of Communications Act (RICA)
  - National Prosecuting Authority Act
  - Special Investigating Units and Special Tribunals Act
  - Witness Protection Act
  - Criminal Procedure Act (competent verdicts)

KEY COMPLIANCE CHECKPOINTS
--------------------------
1. "Gratification" is defined EXTREMELY broadly — gifts, hospitality, 
   employment, loans, discounts, forbearance, even avoidance of penalty
2. Both GIVER and RECEIVER commit an offence under s3
3. Mandatory duty to report: persons in authority MUST report known/suspected 
   corruption or fraud/theft of R100,000+ to police — failure = criminal offence
4. Foreign bribery criminalised (s5) — aligns with OECD Convention and UNCAC
5. Extra-territorial reach: SA courts have jurisdiction even if act occurs abroad
6. Maximum penalty: LIFE IMPRISONMENT (High Court) for core corruption offences
7. Additional fine: up to 5x the value of gratification
8. Register for Tender Defaulters: conviction under s12/s13 → public blacklisting
9. No limitation on prosecution — extremely serious offences
10. Companies and enterprises can be endorsed on Tender Defaulter Register 
    even if only individual convicted (vicarious liability)

ACT 
To provide for the strengthening of measures to prevent and combat corruption 
and  corrupt activities; to provide for the offence of corruption  and offences relating 
to corrupt activities; to provide for investigative measures in respect of corruption 
and related corrupt activities; to provide for the establishment and endorsement of 
a Register in order to place certain restrictions on persons and  enterprises 
convicted of corrupt activities relating to  tenders  and  contracts;  to place a  duty on 
certain persons holding a position of authority to report  certain  corrupt 
transactions; to provide for  extraterritorial  jurisdiction in respect of the offence of 
corruption  and offences relating to corrupt activities; and  to provide for  matters 
connected therewith. 
PREAMBLE 
WHEREAS the Constitution enshrines the  rights of all people  in  the  Republic and 
affirms the democratic  values of human dignity, equality  and  freedom; 
AND  WHEREAS the Constitution places a duty  on  the  State  to respect,  protect, 
promote and fulfil all the  rights  as  enshrined  in  the  Bill of Rights; 
AND  WHEREAS  corruption and  related corrupt activities  undermine the said  rights, 
endanger the  stability  and  security of societies, undermine  the institutions  and  values of 
democracy  and ethical  values and morality, jeopardise sustainable development, the  rule 
of law  and  the  credibility of governments,  and  provide a  breeding  ground for organised 
crime; 
AND  WHEREAS  the illicit  acquisition of personal  wealth can be particularly 
damaging  to  democratic institutions,  national economies, ethical  values and the rule of 
law; 
AND  WHEREAS there are  links  between  corrupt activities and  other  forms of crime, 
in particular  organised crime  and  economic  crime, including  money-laundering; 
AND  WHEREAS  corruption  is a transnational phenomenon that crosses national 
borders  and  affects all societies  and  economies, and is equally destructive  and 
reprehensible within both  the  public  and  private  spheres of life, so that  regional and 
international  cooperation is essential to prevent  and control corruption and related 
corrupt activities; 
AND  WHEREAS a comprehensive, integrated and multidisciplinary  approach is 
required  to  prevent  and  combat  corruption  and related corrupt activities efficiently and 
effectively; 
AND  WHEREAS the  availability of technical  assistance can play an important  role in 
enhancing the  ability of States, including by strengthening capacity and by institution- 
building,  to prevent and  combat corruption and related corrupt activities efficiently and 
effectively;
