KEY POINTS FOR BUSINESS:


Employment Equity Act 55/1998

PURPOSE: Eliminate unfair discrimination + implement affirmative action.

TWO PILLARS:

1. UNFAIR DISCRIMINATION (Chapter II, ss5-11)
   Prohibited grounds: race, gender, sex, pregnancy, marital status, family
   responsibility, ethnic/social origin, colour, sexual orientation, age,
   disability, religion, HIV status, conscience, belief, political opinion,
   culture, language, birth, or ANY other arbitrary ground.
   s7  — Medical testing prohibited (unless legislation requires or justifiable)
   s8  — Psychological testing restricted (must be scientifically valid + fair)
   s11 — Burden of proof on employer once discrimination shown

2. AFFIRMATIVE ACTION (Chapter III, ss12-27)
   Applies to DESIGNATED EMPLOYERS:
   - >50 employees, OR
   - Above industry turnover threshold (Schedule 4):
     Agriculture R6M, Mining R22.5M, Manufacturing R30M, Construction R15M,
     Retail R45M, Wholesale R75M, Transport R30M, Finance R30M
   s19 — Workforce analysis (profile, policies, practices)
   s20 — Employment Equity Plan (1-5 years, with numerical goals)
   s21 — Annual EE Report to Dept of Employment and Labour
   s22 — MUST publish report
   s24 — Assign senior manager responsible for EE
   s27 — Address income differentials (progressive elimination)

3. STATE CONTRACTS (s53)
   - Organs of state MUST include EE compliance as contract criterion
   - Must request EE Compliance Certificate (s42) from bidders
   - Non-compliant employers can be RESTRICTED from state contracts

4. ENFORCEMENT
   s35 — Labour inspectors: enter, inspect, question, copy
   s37 — Compliance order
   s42 — D-G reviews compliance → issues EE Compliance Certificate
   s50 — Labour Court: fines Schedule 1 (R1.5M first offence, R2.7M repeat)

ACT 
To provide for employment equity; and to provide for matters incidental thereto. 
PREAMBLE 
Recognising-
that as a result of apartheid and other discriminatory laws and practices, there 
are disparities in employment, occupation and income within the national 
labour market; and 
that those disparities create such pronounced disadvantages for certain 
categories of people that they cannot be redressed simply by repealing 
discriminatory laws, 
Therefore, in order to-
promote the constitutional right of equality and the exercise of true 
democracy; 
eliminate unfair discrimination in employment; 
ensure the implementation of employment equity to redress the effects of 
discrimination; 
achieve a diverse workforce broadly representative of our people; 
promote economic development and efficiency in the workforce; and 
give effect to the obligations of the Republic as a member of the International 
Labour Organisation, 
(English text signed by the President.) 
(Assented to 12 October 1998.)

SCHEDULE 3 
Transitional arrangements 
Definitions Schedule 7 
1. In this Schedule, unless the context indicates otherwise-
"pending" means existing immediately before this Act came into operation; and 
"repealed provisions of the Labour Relations Act" means the provisions of the 
Labour Relations Act repealed by Schedule 2. 
Disputes arising before commencement of this Act 
2. Any dispute contemplated in item (2)(1 )(a) of Schedule 7 of the Labour Relations 
Act that arose before the commencement of this Act, must be dealt with as if the repealed 
provisions of the Labour Relations Act had not been repealed.

Act No. 55, 1998 EMPLOYMENT EQUITY ACT. 1998 
Courts 
3. (1) In any pending dispute contemplated in item (2)(1 )( a) of Schedule 7 of the 
Labour Relations Act in respect of which the Labour Court or the Labour Appeal Court 
had jurisdiction and in respect of which proceedings had not been instituted before the 
commencement of this Act, proceedings must be instituted in the Labour Court or 
Labour Appeal Court (as the case may be) and dealt with as if the repealed provisions of 
the Labour Relations Act had not been repealed. 
(2) Any dispute contemplated in item (2)( 1 )( a) of Schedule 7 of the Labour Relations 
Act in respect of which proceedings were pending in the Labour Court or Labour Appeal 
Court must be proceeded with as if the repealed provisions of the Labour Relations Act 
had not been repealed. 
(3) Any pending appeal before the Labour Appeal Court must be dealt with by the 
Labour Appeal Court as if the repealed provisions of the Labour Relations Act had not 
been repealed. 
(4) When acting in terms of subitems (I) to (3), the Labour Court or Labour Appeal 
Court may perform or exercise any function or power that it had in terms of the repealed 
provisions of the Labour Relations Act.
