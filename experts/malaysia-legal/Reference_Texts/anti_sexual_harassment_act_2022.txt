MALAYSIA ANTI-SEXUAL HARASSMENT ACT 2022 (Act 840)
Comprehensive Summary for HR & Employer Compliance
================================================================================
Source: AGC, Herbert Smith Freehills Kramer, LPP Law
Gazetted: 18 October 2022; Phased Implementation from March 2023
================================================================================

1. OVERVIEW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Anti-Sexual Harassment Act 2022 (ASHA) provides a legal right of redress
for any person who has been sexually harassed. It establishes a dedicated
Tribunal for Anti-Sexual Harassment. Unlike the Employment Act 1955 which
covers only workplace-related complaints, ASHA applies BEYOND the workplace.

2. DEFINITION OF SEXUAL HARASSMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
"Sexual harassment" means any unwanted conduct of a sexual nature, in ANY form:
- Verbal
- Non-verbal
- Visual
- Gestural
- Physical

...directed at a person which is REASONABLY OFFENSIVE or HUMILIATING or
is a threat to their WELL-BEING.

3. IMPLEMENTATION TIMELINE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
28 March 2023: Sections 1, 2, 24, 25, 26 came into force
  - Definition of sexual harassment
  - Ministry policy formulation powers
  - Regulation-making powers for the Tribunal

8 March 2024: Sections 3-23 came into force
  - Full Tribunal operations
  - Complaint and hearing procedures
  - Compensation powers

4. TRIBUNAL FOR ANTI-SEXUAL HARASSMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.1 Composition
- Legally trained AND non-legally trained members
- Current/past Judicial and Legal Service members
- Advocates/solicitors with 7+ years' standing
- Individuals with knowledge/practical experience in sexual harassment

4.2 Tribunal Powers
The Tribunal may ORDER the respondent to:
a) Issue a statement of APOLOGY (including public apology if act was public)
b) Pay COMPENSATION or damages NOT EXCEEDING RM 250,000
c) Attend any PROGRAMME as the Tribunal deems necessary
d) Dismiss complaint if frivolous or vexatious

4.3 Hearing Rules
- ALL hearings are CLOSED to the public
- Parties MAY NOT be represented by lawyer UNLESS complex legal issues
- Tribunal can: order preservation of evidence, summon any person to
  give evidence or produce documents

5. IMPLICATIONS FOR EMPLOYERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Even though ASHA is not limited to employment context, it has MAJOR
implications for employers:

5.1 Evidence and Summons
- Tribunal may order EMPLOYER to preserve evidence
- Tribunal may SUMMON employer or other employees to give evidence
- Employer records of grievances and investigations may be subpoenaed

5.2 Internal vs Tribunal Complaints
- Employee CAN file Tribunal complaint even if they already filed
  an internal grievance with the employer
- Outcome of INTERNAL investigation does NOT prevent Tribunal claim
- Employer internal process does NOT preempt ASHA jurisdiction

5.3 Employer Obligations
- MUST have robust anti-sexual harassment POLICIES
- MUST maintain proper records of grievances and investigations
- MUST display anti-sexual harassment notice in workplace
- Must consider governance implications of external Tribunal complaints

6. AWARDS AND ENFORCEMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Maximum compensation: RM 250,000
- Apology orders (including public apologies)
- Mandatory attendance at programmes
- Tribunal awards enforceable through court system
- Non-compliance with Tribunal orders may result in further penalties

7. PRACTICAL STEPS FOR EMPLOYERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. DRAFT/UPDATE anti-sexual harassment policy
2. DISPLAY policy prominently in workplace
3. TRAIN all employees (including management) on ASHA obligations
4. ESTABLISH clear complaint and investigation procedures
5. APPOINT designated harassment complaint handlers
6. DOCUMENT all complaints, investigations, and outcomes
7. REVIEW employment contracts and handbooks
