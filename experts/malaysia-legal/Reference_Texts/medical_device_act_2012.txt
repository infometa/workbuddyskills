1. LEGAL FRAMEWORK
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Medical Device Act 2012 (Act 737): Principal legislation
- Medical Device Regulations 2012: Subsidiary legislation
- Medical Device Authority (MDA): Regulatory body under Ministry of Health
- Effective date: 30 June 2013 [P.U. (B)126/2013]
- Act 737 consists of 80 Sections divided into 6 Parts

PART I: PRELIMINARY (Sections 1-4)
- Section 2: Definitions — "medical device", "establishment", "manufacturer",
  "authorized representative", "conformity assessment", "import", "export",
  "place in the market"
- "Medical device" defined as: any instrument, apparatus, implement, machine,
  appliance, software, material or other similar or related article intended
  for use in healthcare for diagnosis, prevention, monitoring, treatment or
  alleviation of disease or handicap (excludes drugs)

PART II: MEDICAL DEVICE AUTHORITY (Sections 5-14)
- Establishment of MDA as regulatory body
- Functions include: regulating medical devices, industry oversight,
  enforcement, advising Minister

PART III: REGISTRATION OF MEDICAL DEVICES (Sections 15-31)
- Section 15(1): No establishment shall import, export, or place in the market
  any registered medical device unless it holds an Establishment Licence
- Section 5(1): No medical device shall be imported, exported or placed in
  the market unless the medical device is registered with the Authority

2. DEVICE CLASSIFICATION (4 Classes)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Based on risk level per ASEAN harmonized classification:

  Class A: Low risk (e.g., bandages, tongue depressors, non-sterile gloves)
  Class B: Low-moderate risk (e.g., infusion devices, diagnostic ultrasound)
  Class C: Moderate-high risk (e.g., lung ventilator, bone fixation plate)
  Class D: High risk (e.g., pacemakers, heart valves, drug-eluting stents)

Classification based on: duration of contact, invasiveness, local vs systemic
effect, active vs non-active, whether delivering medicines/energy

3. ESTABLISHMENT LICENCE (EL)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Required under Section 15(1) of Act 737:
- ANY establishment that imports, exports, or places medical devices in the
  Malaysian market MUST hold an Establishment Licence
- Types of EL:
  a) Manufacturer's Licence — for local manufacturers
  b) Importer's Licence — for importing devices
  c) Distributor's Licence — for local distribution
  d) Authorised Representative Licence — for foreign manufacturers' agents

Key requirements for EL application:
- Must be a company registered under Companies Act 2016 in Malaysia
- Appoint a Person-In-Charge (PIC) with relevant qualifications
- Establish Quality Management System (QMS)
- Maintain proper records of all devices handled
- Appoint a registered Conformity Assessment Body (CAB) for Class C/D devices

4. PRODUCT REGISTRATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Process through MeDC@St (Medical Device Centralized Online Application System):

Step 1: Determine if product is a medical device (per Section 2 definition)
Step 2: Classify device (Class A/B/C/D)
Step 3: Group similar models/components under single application
Step 4: Conformity Assessment via registered CAB
Step 5: Compile Common Submission Dossier Template (CSDT)
Step 6: Submit registration via MeDC@St
Step 7: MDA review (90 days to correct if returned; auto-withdrawal if exceed)

Required in CSDT:
- General device information & classification justification
- Manufacturer information (name, address, website)
- Grouping details (list of all models/components)
- Design description, risk analysis, clinical evidence
- Manufacturing process documentation
- Labelling and instructions for use
- Declaration of Conformity (from manufacturer's letterhead)
- Post-market vigilance history (recalls, adverse events, bans)

Key notes:
- MDA recognizes CE Marking and US FDA clearances as conformity evidence
- Expired EC certificates may be accepted under specific conditions
- Registration renewal typically required when changes are made

5. AUTHORIZED REPRESENTATIVE (AR)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Foreign manufacturers MUST appoint an AR in Malaysia:
- AR must hold an Establishment Licence
- AR is responsible for device registration, post-market surveillance,
  adverse event reporting
- AR liable for regulatory compliance on behalf of overseas manufacturer
- AR agreement must be in writing and filed with MDA

6. QUALITY MANAGEMENT SYSTEM REQUIREMENTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Local manufacturers: MUST implement ISO 13485 QMS
- Importers/distributors: MUST obtain GDPMD certification
  (Good Distribution Practice for Medical Devices)
- Conformity Assessment Body (CAB) must verify compliance

7. PART IV: OFFENCES AND PENALTIES (Sections 32-46)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Key offences under Act 737:

- Section 32: Import/export without device registration
  Fine up to RM 200,000 or imprisonment up to 3 years, or both

- Section 33: Placing unregistered device in market
  Fine up to RM 200,000 or imprisonment up to 3 years, or both

- Section 34: Operating without Establishment Licence
  Fine up to RM 200,000 or imprisonment up to 3 years, or both

- Section 35: Failure to maintain QMS / non-compliance with conditions
  Fine up to RM 100,000 or imprisonment up to 2 years, or both

- Section 36: False or misleading information in application
  Fine up to RM 100,000 or imprisonment up to 2 years, or both

- Section 37: Obstruction of authorized officer
  Fine up to RM 50,000 or imprisonment up to 1 year, or both

- Section 38: Offences by body corporate
  Directors/officers also liable if offence committed with consent/neglect

- Section 39-46: Additional enforcement powers
  - Search, seizure, arrest without warrant
  - Suspension or cancellation of registration
  - Recall orders for non-compliant devices
  - Compound of offences (MDA can offer compound)

8. PART V: APPEALS (Sections 47-56)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Appeal against MDA decisions to Minister of Health
- Timeframe for appeal submission
- Minister's decision is final

9. PART VI: GENERAL (Sections 57-80)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Regulation-making powers of Minister
- Fees and charges
- Transitional provisions for devices already on market pre-Act
- Exemptions (customized devices, special access)
- Reporting requirements for adverse events and field safety corrective
  actions (FSCA)

10. POST-MARKET OBLIGATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Adverse event reporting to MDA
- Field Safety Corrective Actions (FSCA) notification
- Recall management
- Record keeping (minimum retention period)
- Renewal of registration upon significant changes
- Post-market surveillance studies for higher-risk devices

11. RELATED REGULATIONS AND STANDARDS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Medical Device Regulations 2012
- Medical Device (Advertisement) Regulations
- Medical Device (Export) Regulations
- ISO 13485: Medical devices — Quality management systems
- ISO 14971: Medical devices — Risk management
- ASEAN Medical Device Directive (AMDD) harmonized requirements
- GHTF (Global Harmonization Task Force) guidance documents

12. MDA REGULATORY FEES (Indicative)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Establishment Licence application: ~RM 1,000 - 5,000 (depending on type)
- Product registration (Class A): ~RM 500 per product
- Product registration (Class B): ~RM 1,000 per product  
- Product registration (Class C/D): ~RM 2,000+ per product
- Annual retention fee: applicable
(Note: fees subject to change - check MDA latest Fee Schedule)
