================================================================================
MALAYSIA INDUSTRIAL COURT — LANDMARK PRECEDENT CASES
HR Reference Guide for Unfair Dismissal Principles
================================================================================
Source: Industrial Court of Malaysia (Mahkamah Perusahaan), NST, Mondaq, LinkedIn
Scope: Key principles from published awards
================================================================================

1. CONSTRUCTIVE DISMISSAL — KEY TEST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DEFINITION: Employee resigns due to employer's conduct that fundamentally
breaches the employment contract, making continued employment INTOLERABLE.

Four-Part Test (established in Wong Chee Hong v Cathay Organisation):
i.  Employer breached fundamental TERM of employment contract
ii.  Breach must be SIGNIFICANT, going to ROOT of the contract
iii. Employee resigned IN RESPONSE to that breach
iv.  Employee did NOT WAIVE or condone the breach

COMMON SCENARIOS:
- Unilateral demotion without justification
- Significant salary reduction without consultation
- Forced relocation without contractual basis
- Hostile work environment not addressed by management
- Removal of job functions/authority (constructive redundancy)

2. PROBATIONERS' RIGHTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Khaliah Abbas v Pesaka Astana (M) Sdn Bhd (Industrial Court Award 106/97):
- Probationer has same right as confirmed employee to challenge dismissal
- Employer MUST give probationer opportunity to improve
- Cannot dismiss probationer without JUST CAUSE
- "Unsatisfactory performance" requires EVIDENCE of counselling and
  opportunity to improve

3. RETRENCHMENT — LIFO PRINCIPLE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Stephen Bong v Borneo Development Corp (Industrial Court Award):
- Selection for retrenchment MUST follow "Last In, First Out" (LIFO) unless
  employer can justify deviations
- Employer must show FAIR and OBJECTIVE criteria for selection
- Foreign employees SHOULD be retrenched FIRST (before locals doing
  same work) — National Employment Policy principle

See also: Penang & Seberang Prai Textile & Garment Workers Union v
Penfabric Sdn Bhd (Award 310/2009):
- Employer must CONSULT workers/union BEFORE implementing retrenchment
- Must demonstrate retrenchment is GENUINE (not disguised dismissal)
- Must prove business necessity (financial records, declining orders)

4. DISMISSAL FOR MISCONDUCT — STANDARD OF PROOF
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Telekom Malaysia v Ahmad Nizam (Award 123/2004):
- Standard: BALANCE OF PROBABILITIES (not criminal "beyond reasonable doubt")
- Employer must prove misconduct "MORE LIKELY THAN NOT" occurred
- Evidence must be CREDIBLE and RELIABLE (not hearsay or suspicion)

5. DISMISSAL FOR POOR PERFORMANCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Key principle from IE Products v Industrial Court:
- Employer must show employee was AWARE of performance standards
- Employer must give REASONABLE TIME and SUPPORT to improve
- At least ONE warning/appraisal indicating unsatisfactory performance
- Performance improvement plan (PIP) strongly recommended
- Dismissal for a SINGLE missed target is disproportionate

6. COMPENSATION CALCULATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Formula established by Industrial Court practice:
Back wages = Monthly salary x months from dismissal to award (MAX 24 months)
Compensation in lieu = 1 month salary per YEAR of service

2025 HIGH-VALUE AWARD (Ang Chin Gaik v Former Employer, NST March 2025):
- Industrial Court awarded RM 1,380,722.20 for wrongful dismissal
- Demonstrates increasing quantum for senior executive dismissals
- Employer's procedural failures heavily penalised

7. VICTIMISATION / WHISTLEBLOWER DISMISSAL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Whistleblower Protection Act 2010 read with Industrial Relations Act:
- Dismissal of whistleblower = AUTOMATICALLY UNFAIR
- Burden shifts to EMPLOYER to prove dismissal was for valid,
  non-retaliatory reasons
- Employees reporting safety violations, fraud, corruption protected

8. PREGNANCY DISCRIMINATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Beatrice Fernandez v Sistem Penerbangan Malaysia (FC 2005):
- Termination during pregnancy must NOT be related to pregnancy
- If termination coincides with pregnancy, REVERSE BURDEN on employer
  to prove valid, non-discriminatory reason
- Maternity protection is constitutional (Article 8 — gender equality)

9. PRACTICAL LESSONS FOR HR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. ALWAYS document performance issues with specific dates and facts
2. Follow PROCEDURE before SUSPECT (not the other way around)
3. DI is INVESTMENT, not COST — RM 5,000 on DI now saves RM 100,000+ later
4. "At-will employment" does NOT exist in Malaysia
5. Every dismissal must have BOTH a valid reason AND fair procedure
6. When in doubt, consult lawyer BEFORE acting (not after)
7. Settlement agreements must be in writing with independent legal advice
