================================================================================
MALAYSIA DOMESTIC INQUIRY — COMPLETE PROCEDURAL GUIDE
Comprehensive Summary for HR Practitioners
================================================================================
Source: Malaysia HR Forum, Worksy, One Asia Legal, Industrial Court precedents
Legal Basis: Employment Act 1955, Section 14(1) — "due inquiry" requirement
================================================================================

1. WHAT IS A DOMESTIC INQUIRY (DI)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A Domestic Inquiry is an INTERNAL investigation conducted by an employer to
determine whether an employee has committed misconduct. It is NOT a court
proceeding but must follow principles of NATURAL JUSTICE. A properly conducted
DI is the employer's strongest defence against unfair dismissal claims.

Legal requirement: Section 14(1) Employment Act 1955 requires employer to
conduct a "due inquiry" before dismissing an employee for misconduct.

2. WHEN IS A DI REQUIRED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Conduct a DI when:
- Allegation of SERIOUS misconduct (theft, fraud, insubordination, fighting)
- Repeated minor misconduct after warnings
- Breach of company policy that may warrant dismissal
- Employee disputes the allegation
- Show-cause response is unsatisfactory

Skip DI when:
- Employee ADMITS guilt in show-cause response
- Misconduct is CONFIRMED by clear video/audio evidence and admitted
- Minor infractions (verbal warning sufficient)

3. PRE-DI STEPS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Step 1: PRELIMINARY INVESTIGATION
- Gather all evidence (documents, CCTV, emails, witness statements)
- Interview relevant witnesses informally
- Verify facts and timeline
- Determine if misconduct warrants formal DI

Step 2: SUSPENSION (Optional but recommended)
- May suspend employee on HALF PAY (max 2 weeks under EA Section 14(1))
- Purpose: prevent evidence tampering, witness intimidation
- Suspension is NOT punishment — it is administrative
- Must restore full back-pay if employee found NOT guilty

Step 3: SHOW-CAUSE LETTER
- MUST be in WRITING
- State EACH allegation separately with:
  - Exact date, time, and place of each alleged misconduct
  - Specific company rule/policy violated
  - Potential consequence (dismissal/demotion/etc.)
- Give REASONABLE TIME to reply (typically 7-14 days)
- Inform employee of right to bring WITNESS and/or REPRESENTATIVE

Step 4: EVALUATE SHOW-CAUSE RESPONSE
- If employee admits: proceed to disciplinary decision
- If employee denies or explanation unsatisfactory: proceed to DI

4. DI PANEL COMPOSITION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Minimum 3 members (odd number to avoid deadlock)
- Panel MUST be IMPARTIAL:
  - Investigating officer CANNOT sit on panel
  - Direct supervisor of the accused CANNOT sit on panel
  - Panel chairperson should be senior management or external consultant
- Appoint PROSECUTOR from management
- Accused employee has RIGHT to representation:
  - Colleague or union representative
  - NOT a lawyer (unless company policy allows)

5. DI PROCEDURE (Step-by-Step)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.1 Notice of Domestic Inquiry
- Give at least 7 DAYS written notice
- State: date, time, venue, charge(s), witnesses to be called
- List documentary evidence to be presented
- Remind employee of right to bring witness/representative

5.2 Opening
- Panel introduces itself
- Prosecutor reads charges
- Employee enters plea for each charge

5.3 Prosecution Case (Employer)
- Present documentary evidence (marked as exhibits)
- Call prosecution witnesses (examination-in-chief)
- Employee may cross-examine each witness
- Panel may ask clarification questions

5.4 Defence Case (Accused Employee)
- Present defence evidence and documents
- Call defence witnesses
- Prosecutor may cross-examine defence witnesses
- Panel may ask clarification questions

5.5 Submissions
- Prosecutor makes closing submission
- Accused/representative makes closing submission

5.6 Deliberation
- Panel deliberates in PRIVATE
- Standard of proof: BALANCE OF PROBABILITIES (not beyond reasonable doubt)
- Must DOCUMENT reasoning for decision

6. BURDEN OF PROOF
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The employer (prosecution) bears the burden of proving misconduct on a
BALANCE OF PROBABILITIES — meaning it must be MORE LIKELY THAN NOT that the
employee committed the misconduct. This is LOWER than the criminal standard
("beyond reasonable doubt").

7. POSSIBLE OUTCOMES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
After DI, the panel may recommend:
a) NO CASE — employee exonerated, restore full back-pay if suspended
b) GUILTY with WARNING — written warning, no other action
c) GUILTY with DEMOTION — reduction in rank/responsibilities
d) GUILTY with FINE — deduction from wages (max amounts under EA)
e) GUILTY with DISMISSAL — termination for misconduct
f) INCONCLUSIVE — further investigation needed

Panel's recommendation goes to ULTIMATE DECISION-MAKER (MD/CEO/Board).
Decision-maker is NOT bound by panel recommendation but should give
strong reasons for deviating.

8. POST-DI DOCUMENTATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Minutes of proceedings (contemporaneous, signed)
- Panel findings report
- Ultimate decision letter to employee
- Employee's right of appeal (if company policy provides)
- KEEP ALL RECORDS for minimum 7 YEARS

9. COMMON MISTAKES (that lead to Industrial Court losses)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Skipping show-cause letter → automatic unfair dismissal finding
2. Biased panel (investigator sitting on panel) → DI VOID
3. Denying employee right to representation → procedural flaw
4. Vague charges ("poor performance" without specifics) → impossible to defend
5. No documentary evidence → employer's word vs employee's word
6. Pre-judging ("we already decided you're guilty") → breach of natural justice
7. Excessive penalty (dismissal for first-time minor offence) → disproportionate
8. Reaching conclusion based on HEARSAY (not firsthand evidence)

10. INDUSTRIAL COURT REVIEW STANDARD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
When a dismissed employee challenges through Industrial Court, the Court will
examine:
i.   Was there a VALID REASON for dismissal? (substantive fairness)
ii.  Was there a PROPER PROCEDURE? (procedural fairness)
iii. Was the PUNISHMENT PROPORTIONATE? (proportionality)

A properly conducted DI helps employer on ALL three tests.

11. COST OF GETTING IT WRONG
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Typical Industrial Court awards for unfair dismissal:
- Reinstatement with back wages (up to 24 months)
- Compensation in lieu: 1 month per year of service
- Recent cases: awards exceeding RM 1.38 million for senior executives
- Legal costs: typically RM 20,000-80,000
