# Malaysia Customs Duties & Tariff Guide
# 马来西亚海关关税指南
# Source: Royal Malaysian Customs Department (JKDM/RMCD), Customs Duties Order 2025, Paul Hype Page
# Last updated: November 2025

## Part 1: Legal Framework

### 1.1 Key Legislation
- Customs Act 1967 (Act 235) — Primary customs legislation
- Customs Duties Order 2025 — Effective 1 November 2025, tariff code updates and duty rate adjustments
- Sales Tax Act 2018 — SST treatment tied to HS classification
- Excise Act 1976 — Excise duties on specific goods

### 1.2 Administering Authority
Royal Malaysian Customs Department (JKDM/RMCD)
- Portal: www.customs.gov.my
- HS Explorer: ezhs.customs.gov.my (tariff code lookup tool)
- uCustoms: Digital customs clearance system

## Part 2: Customs Duties Structure

### 2.1 Types of Duties

Import Duty (Duti Import):
- Levied on goods imported into Malaysia
- Based on HS Code classification
- Rates: 0% to 60% (varies by product category)
- Most manufactured goods: 0% to 30%

Export Duty (Duti Eksport):
- Levied on selected goods exported from Malaysia
- Applicable to: palm oil, crude petroleum, rubber, timber
- Rates vary by commodity and price levels

Excise Duty (Duti Eksais):
- Levied on specific manufactured goods
- Products: alcohol, tobacco, motor vehicles, playing cards, mahjong tiles
- Rates vary by product type and value

### 2.2 Customs Duties Order 2025 Updates
- Implementation: 1 November 2025
- New HS code structures aligned with WCO 2025 updates
- Tariff reclassification for selected goods
- Duty rate increases or reductions for certain categories
- Removal of certain exemptions
- New product definitions and subcategories

## Part 3: HS Code System

### 3.1 Structure
- Asean Harmonised Tariff Nomenclature (AHTN)
- Based on Harmonised System (HS) of WCO
- Malaysia uses AHTN 2025 (9-digit codes)
- First 6 digits: International HS code
- Next 2 digits: ASEAN specific
- Last digit: Malaysia specific

### 3.2 Key Sectors Affected (2025 Reclassification)
- Electronics & components
- Plastics, rubber and polymers
- Food & beverage (processed)
- Machinery & tools
- Automotive parts
- Chemicals and industrial supplies
- Textile and garment
- Beauty, personal care & health products

## Part 4: Duty Rates by Category

### 4.1 Typical Duty Rate Ranges

Raw materials: 0% - 5%
Machinery and equipment: 0% - 10%
Manufactured goods: 5% - 30%
Automotive (CBU): 30% - 60%
Processed food: 0% - 30%
Chemicals: 0% - 20%
Textiles: 10% - 30%

### 4.2 Excise Duty Rates
Alcohol: RM150 - RM500 per litre of pure alcohol
Tobacco: RM0.40 per cigarette + ad valorem 40%
Motor vehicles: 60% - 105% (depending on engine capacity)

## Part 5: Free Trade Agreements (FTAs)

Malaysia has comprehensive FTA network providing preferential tariff rates:

ASEAN FTAs:
- ATIGA (ASEAN Trade in Goods Agreement) — 0% for most goods within ASEAN
- Regional: RCEP (Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership)

Bilateral FTAs:
- ACFTA (ASEAN-China)
- AJCEP (ASEAN-Japan)
- AKFTA (ASEAN-Korea)
- AANZFTA (ASEAN-Australia-New Zealand)
- MJEPA (Malaysia-Japan)
- MPCEPA (Malaysia-Pakistan)
- MICECA (Malaysia-India)
- MTFTA (Malaysia-Turkey)
- MCFTA (Malaysia-Chile)
- CPTPP (Comprehensive and Progressive Trans-Pacific Partnership)

## Part 6: Duty Exemptions and Reliefs

### 6.1 Types of Relief

1. Temporary Importation (ATA Carnet): Goods imported temporarily for exhibitions, professional equipment
2. Re-importation: Malaysian goods returned within 3 years, duty relief available
3. Drawback: Duty refund for imported materials used in manufactured exports
4. Warehousing: Goods stored in Licensed Warehouse without duty payment until removal
5. Free Zones: No duty payable on goods in Free Industrial Zones / Free Commercial Zones
6. Approved Trader Scheme: Fast-track clearance for compliant traders
7. Sales Tax Exemption (STeP): For registered manufacturers importing raw materials directly

### 6.2 Conditional Exemptions
- Goods for government departments
- Diplomatic goods
- Gifts and samples (low value)
- Personal effects and household goods

## Part 7: Landed Cost Calculation

### 7.1 Components
Landed Cost = CIF Value (Cost + Insurance + Freight) + Import Duty + SST + Handling Charges

### 7.2 SST on Imports
- Imported goods subject to sales tax at 5% or 10% (depending on HS classification)
- Basic necessities: 0%
- Service tax does not apply to goods

### 7.3 Calculation Example
CIF Value: RM100,000
Import Duty (5%): RM5,000
SST (10% on CIF + duty): RM10,500
Total Duty & Tax: RM15,500
Total Landed Cost: RM115,500

## Part 8: Customs Compliance

### 8.1 Import/Export Declarations
- K1 Form: Import declaration
- K2 Form: Export declaration
- K3 Form: Transit declaration
- K8 Form: Warehouse declaration
- Must be submitted via uCustoms (digital system)

### 8.2 Key Procedures
1. Customs clearance within 14 days of arrival for imports
2. Declaration must include correct HS Code, value, origin, quantity
3. Supporting documents: Commercial invoice, Bill of Lading/Air Waybill, Packing List, Certificate of Origin (for preferential rates)

### 8.3 Penalties for Non-Compliance
- Misclassification: Penalty up to RM500,000 or 3x the duty undercharged
- Late payment: 10% per annum
- Smuggling: Fine up to RM500,000, imprisonment up to 5 years, forfeiture of goods
- Incorrect declaration: Fine up to RM300,000

### 8.4 Customs Modernisation (2026)
- More digital verification of tariff codes
- Increased RMCD audits on SST and import classification
- Automated cross-checking in uCustoms
- Greater scrutiny of FTA documents (RCEP/CPTPP)
- Stricter penalties for misclassification
