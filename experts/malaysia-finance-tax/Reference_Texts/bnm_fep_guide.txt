# Malaysia Foreign Exchange Policy (FEP) Guide
# 马来西亚外汇政策指南
# Source: Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM), Financial Markets Information Portal, RHB Bank
# Last updated: October 2025

## Part 1: Overview

### 1.1 Legal Framework
- Financial Services Act 2013 (FSA)
- Islamic Financial Services Act 2013 (IFSA)
- Administered by Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM)

### 1.2 Core Principles
Malaysia's Foreign Exchange Policy (FEP) remains liberal, designed to promote financial stability while supporting a competitive economy. The policy is committed to facilitating cross-border economic activities.

### 1.3 Definitions

Resident:
- Malaysia citizen
- Malaysia citizen with PR status of another country but resides in Malaysia
- Non-Malaysian citizen with PR status in Malaysia and residing in Malaysia
- Business enterprises/societies established and operating in Malaysia
- The Government or any State Government

Non-Resident:
- Non-Malaysian citizen
- Malaysian citizen with PR status abroad and resides abroad
- Embassies, Consulates, High Commissions, supranational or International organisations
- Business entities established abroad
- An overseas branch, subsidiary, regional office, sales office or representative office of a resident company

## Part 3: Rules for Non-Residents

### 3.1 External Account
An account opened by non-residents (individually or jointly) or by residents operating in trust for non-residents.
Transaction limit: MYR 10,000 accumulated per account daily (standard)
No limit for Non-Resident Exempted Group (NEG):
- Consulates, High Commissions, Embassies
- MM2H (Malaysia My Second Home) participants
- Individuals working/studying in Malaysia (including spouse, child, parent)

### 3.2 Investing in Malaysia
Non-residents may freely invest in:
- Malaysian equities (listed and unlisted)
- Malaysian bonds (government and corporate)
- Real property (subject to state-level approval thresholds)
- Foreign Direct Investment (FDI)

### 3.3 Borrowing in Malaysia
Non-resident borrowing in ringgit is subject to FEP rules under Notice 2.
Specific limits apply based on purpose and type of facility.

### 3.4 Ringgit Payments to Non-Residents
- Payments for trade in goods and services: Permitted
- Payments for investments: Permitted
- Other ringgit payments: Subject to External Account limits

## Part 4: Rules for Residents

### 4.1 Domestic Borrowing
Resident individuals: Up to RM1 million equivalent per calendar year for foreign currency asset investment
Resident entities (group): Up to RM50 million equivalent per calendar year

### 4.2 Foreign Currency Accounts
Residents may maintain foreign currency accounts with licensed onshore banks.
Limits apply based on the source of funds and purpose.

### 4.3 Investment Abroad
Resident individuals: Up to RM1 million per calendar year through conversion of ringgit
Resident entities: Up to RM50 million per calendar year (group aggregate)

### 4.4 Foreign Currency Borrowing
Resident individuals: Up to RM10 million equivalent total through foreign currency borrowing from licensed onshore banks or non-residents

## Part 5: Repatriation of Funds

### 5.1 Repatriation by Non-Residents
- Dividends, profits, and capital gains: Freely repatriable (subject to withholding tax where applicable)
- Proceeds from sale of investments: Freely repatriable
- Loan repayments: Permitted subject to FEP rules

### 5.2 Repatriation by Residents
- Foreign investment returns: Freely repatriable
- Subject to reporting requirements for amounts exceeding certain thresholds

## Part 6: Hedging

- Onshore FX hedging permitted for residents with underlying exposures
- Non-residents may hedge ringgit exposures with licensed onshore banks
- Dynamic hedging framework available through BNM

## Part 7: Compliance and Reporting

- All transactions must be conducted through licensed onshore banks
- Banks are required to conduct due diligence under MDD (Minimum Due Diligence) guidelines
- Reporting thresholds apply for large transactions
- ABM (Association of Banks Malaysia) publishes consolidated MDD guide

## Part 8: Recent Developments
