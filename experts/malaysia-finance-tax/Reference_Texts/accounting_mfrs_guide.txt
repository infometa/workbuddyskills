# Malaysia Financial Reporting Standards (MFRS & MPERS) Guide
# 马来西亚财务报告准则指南
# Source: Malaysian Accounting Standards Board (MASB), IFRS Foundation
# Last updated: 2025

## Part 1: Overview

The Malaysian Accounting Standards Board (MASB) was established under the Financial Reporting Act 1997 as an independent authority to develop and issue accounting and financial reporting standards in Malaysia.

## Part 2: Two-Tier Reporting Framework

### 2.1 MFRS (Malaysian Financial Reporting Standards)
- Full IFRS-compliant standards
- Mandatory for:
  - Listed companies on Bursa Malaysia
  - Companies with public accountability
  - Financial institutions regulated by BNM
  - Large private companies (by revenue, assets, or employee thresholds)
- Essentially identical to IFRS as issued by the IASB

### 2.2 MPERS (Malaysian Private Entities Reporting Standard)
- Based on IFRS for SMEs
- Available for private entities that do not have public accountability
- Simplified reporting requirements compared to full MFRS
- Recently revised and relaunched by MASB (2025)
- Applies to small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) that are not required to use MFRS

### 2.3 Key Differences Between MFRS and MPERS

Area | MFRS | MPERS
Financial instruments | MFRS 9 (complex classification) | Simplified approach
Revenue recognition | MFRS 15 (5-step model) | Simplified guidance
Leases | MFRS 16 (lessees recognize ROU asset) | Simplified lease accounting
Consolidation | Full consolidation for all subsidiaries | Option to use cost method for certain subsidiaries
Disclosure | Extensive IFRS-level disclosures | Reduced disclosure requirements

## Part 4: Statutory Audit Requirements

### 4.1 Auditor Appointment
- Every company must appoint an auditor within 3 months of incorporation
- Auditor must be approved by SSM and be a member of MIA (Malaysian Institute of Accountants)

### 4.2 Audit Report Deadline
- Audited financial statements must be prepared within 6 months of financial year-end
- Filed with SSM together with annual return

### 4.3 Audit Exemption
- Dormant companies (no accounting transactions)
- Zero-revenue companies meeting certain thresholds
- Small private companies: Revenue <= RM300k, Total assets <= RM300k, <= 5 employees

## Part 5: Director's Report Requirements

Every company's annual report must include a directors' report containing:
- Profit/loss for the financial year
- Dividends recommended
- Directors' names and shareholdings
- Directors' benefits during the year
- Issue of shares or debentures
- Options exercised over shares
- Significant events after the reporting period
- Likely future developments
- Indemnity given to directors/officers
- Approval and signature by a director
