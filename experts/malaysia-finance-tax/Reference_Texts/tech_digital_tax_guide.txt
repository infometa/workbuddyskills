# Part 1: Malaysia Digital (MD) Status — Overview

Replacing the legacy MSC Malaysia status (2022), MD Status is the primary incentive framework for technology and digital economy companies.

## 1.1 Key Benefits of MD Status

| Benefit | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Income Tax Incentive** | 0% / 10% / 15% concessionary rate on qualifying income, OR Investment Tax Allowance |
| **Duration** | 5–10 years (depending on tier and MIDA assessment) |
| **Expat Hiring** | Streamlined Employment Pass route through MDEC; Knowledge Worker pass |
| **Ownership** | 100% foreign equity ownership allowed |
| **Capital Movement** | Freedom to source capital globally |
| **Digital Hub Access** | Cyberjaya and other recognised digital hub locations |

## 1.2 Eligible Activities

MD Status applies to digital-economy activities. Promoted areas include:

| Category | Examples | MSIC Code |
|----------|----------|-----------|
| Software Development | Enterprise SaaS, mobile apps, platform software | 62 (Computer programming) |
| Digital Content | Animation, gaming, creative media | 59 (Motion picture, video, TV) |
| IoT & Embedded Systems | Device connectivity, sensors, industrial IoT | 62 |
| Big Data & AI | Model development, data labelling, MLOps | 62 / 63 |
| Cybersecurity | Products, managed security services | 62 |
| Cloud Computing | Cloud infrastructure, SaaS, PaaS, IaaS | 63 (Information service activities) |
| Blockchain | DLT infrastructure, smart contracts | 62 / 63 |
| Data Centres | Colocation, cloud data centre operations | 63 |
| E-Fulfilment Hubs | Digital logistics platforms | 52 (Warehousing, support) |
| Fintech Infrastructure | Payment gateway, digital banking tech | 62 / 64 |

## 1.3 Tier Structure

MD Status itself is uniform — differentiation happens at the **tax incentive stage** (via MIDA scorecard):

| Assessment Axis | Weight | High Tier Target | Medium Tier |
|----------------|--------|-----------------|-------------|
| Investment quantum | High | > RM 50 million | RM 10–50 million |
| Knowledge worker headcount | High | > 100 skilled employees | 30–100 |
| Average salary | High | > RM 120,000/year | RM 60–120,000 |
| Domestic spending | Medium | > 30% local procurement | 15–30% |
| R&D intensity | High | > 10% of revenue | 5–10% |
| IP development | High | Patents, proprietary tech | Licensed technology |

### Tax Incentive Matrix

| Tier Assessment | Tax Rate on Qualifying Income | Period | ITA Alternative |
|----------------|------------------------------|--------|-----------------|
| High | 0% | Up to 10 years | 100% ITA, offset 100% SI |
| Medium (Upper) | 10% | Up to 5 years | 80% ITA, offset 70% SI |
| Medium (Lower) | 15% | Up to 5 years | 60% ITA, offset 70% SI |

# Part 2: Application Process

## 2.1 Step-by-Step

1. **Form Sdn Bhd**: Applicant must be a Malaysian incorporated company
2. **Apply for MD Status**: Through MDEC's online portal
   - Required: Business plan, founder/team profile, project narrative, financial projections
3. **Apply for Tax Incentive (separately to MIDA)**: MIDA issues the Letter of Authorisation
4. **LHDN gives effect**: Tax incentive applied in the year of assessment
5. **Annual Reporting**: To MDEC and MIDA to maintain status (KPIs on headcount, R&D, local spending)

### Common Pitfalls
| Pitfall | How to Avoid |
|---------|-------------|
| Applying too early | MDEC wants traction (business plan, early revenue, credible team) |
| Mis-classifying activity | "Digital marketing agency" without tech platform doesn't qualify |
| Ignoring KPI conditions | Annual KPIs must be met; failure triggers withdrawal |
| Forgetting separate tax incentive | MD Status alone = Bill of Guarantees only, no tax reduction |

## 2.2 Timeline

| Step | Duration |
|------|----------|
| MD Status application | 4–8 weeks |
| MIDA tax incentive application | 8–12 weeks (after MD Status) |
| Letter of Authorisation | 6–8 weeks |
| **Total** | **4–7 months** (plan accordingly) |

# Part 3: MSC Malaysia (Legacy) — Migration

Existing MSC-status holders have been migrated to MD Status. Key legacy provisions maintained:

### MSC Bill of Guarantees (Legacy)
- Unrestricted employment of local and foreign knowledge workers
- Freedom of ownership (100% foreign equity)
- Freedom to source capital globally for MSC-status activities
- Exemption from local sourcing requirements
- 10-year tax exemption under old MSC framework (now replaced by MD tax incentive)

# Part 4: Technology-Specific R&D Tax Incentives

## 4.1 R&D Double Deduction

| Expenditure | Deduction |
|-------------|-----------|
| In-house R&D | 200% deduction on qualifying expenditure |
| Contracted R&D (to approved research institute) | 200% deduction |
| Approved Research Institute | Additional incentives for R&D service providers |

### Qualifying R&D Expenditure
| Category | Examples |
|----------|----------|
| Staff costs | R&D personnel salaries, benefits |
| Materials | Prototype materials, test samples |
| Equipment | Lab/IT equipment for R&D (CA also available) |
| Subcontracting | Up to 30% of total R&D (may be capped) |
| Patent costs | Registration, legal fees for patents |

## 4.2 R&D Company Status

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| Incentive | Pioneer Status (100% exemption) for contract R&D companies |
| Period | 5 years (extendable) |
| Qualifying | Company must be established primarily for R&D services |

## 4.3 Automation Capital Allowance (Tech Manufacturing)

For tech companies that also manufacture hardware/equipment:
| Year | Allowance |
|------|-----------|
| Year 1 (initial) | 40% |
| Years 2–4 (annual) | 20% each year |
| **Total** | **100%** over 4 years |

# Part 5: Digital Services & SaaS Taxation

## 5.1 Service Tax on Digital Services

### Foreign Digital Service Provider (FDSP) SST
| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| Rate | 8% service tax |
| Threshold | Annual revenue from Malaysian consumers > RM 500,000 |
| Registration | Via LHDN (customs) |
| Scope | Digital services delivered to Malaysian consumers |

### Domestic Digital Services
| Service | Service Tax Rate |
|---------|-----------------|
| SaaS (software as a service) | 8% (from 2024 expansion) |
| IT consultancy (management) | 8% |
| Professional software development (B2B) | 0% (if custom development, not standard product) |
| Cloud infrastructure services | 8% |
| Digital advertising | 8% (if charged to Malaysian clients) |

> Note: B2B services provided by registered SST vendors are subject to SST unless specific exemption applies.

## 5.2 Income Tax on Digital Income

### SaaS / Digital Products
| Scenario | Tax Treatment |
|----------|--------------|
| Malaysian company selling SaaS to local clients | Normal income tax (24%) — reduced via MD Status if eligible |
| Malaysian company exporting SaaS | Normal income tax — Export deduction available |
| Foreign company with Malaysian clients | May trigger e-SST or digital service provider tax |
| Royalties (software licensing) | 10% WHT (if non-resident; reduced by DTA) |

# Part 6: Technology IP Monetisation

## 6.1 IP Tax Incentives

| Activity | Incentive |
|----------|-----------|
| IP commercialisation | Income may qualify for MD Status concessionary rate (0-15%) |
| IP licensing income | Taxable as ordinary income (but reduced under MD if qualifying) |
| IP sale (capital gains) | Not subject to income tax (unless trading) |
| Patent box | No specific patent box regime in Malaysia (contrary to Singapore) |

## 6.2 IP Protection Costs

| Cost | Tax Deductibility |
|------|------------------|
| Patent registration | Capital in nature (not deductible) — but CA available |
| Trademark registration | Capital in nature |
| IP legal defence | Deductible if related to income generation |
| IP amortisation | Deductible if acquired (not self-created) |

# Part 7: Tech Industry-Specific Considerations

## 7.1 Startup Taxation

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| SME rate (≤ RM 150K profit) | 15% (on first RM 150,000) |
| Standard rate (> RM 150K profit) | 24% |
| Unabsorbed losses | Carry forward 7 years (since 2019) |
| Capital allowance unutilised | Carry forward indefinitely |
| Angel investor tax deduction | Up to RM 500,000 deduction (qualifying startups) |

## 7.2 Gig Economy & Platform Workers

- Platform workers (e.g., e-hailing, freelance) are self-employed
- Required to register for income tax if income exceeds threshold
- Voluntary EPF/SOCSO contributions available
- Socso protection extended to gig workers (2024+)

## 7.3 E-commerce Tax

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| Online marketplace/aggregator | May be taxed as e-commerce income |
| Cross-border e-commerce | Low-value goods (≤ RM 500) exempt from import duty; SST on imported goods |
| Dropshipping | Taxable as trading income in Malaysia (if sourced in Malaysia) |

# Part 9: Key Reminders

- **MD Status** is the primary incentive for technology companies — replaces old MSC status
- **Tax incentive part is separate** from MD Status — must apply to MIDA separately
- **Tier assessment** determines the tax rate (0% for high tier, up to 15% for lower)
- **R&D Double Deduction (200%)** — available regardless of MD Status
- **SST on digital services** — 8% for most digital services (B2B and B2C)
- **Startup tax**: 15% on first RM 150K profit (SME rate) + carry forward losses 7 years
- **Data centres** qualify for MD Status with significant infrastructure investment
- **Common pitfall**: Mis-classifying activity as MD-eligible (need genuine tech platform)
- **Annual KPI reporting** to MDEC required — failure triggers penalty/withdrawal
