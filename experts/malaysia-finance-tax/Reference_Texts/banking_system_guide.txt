## Part 1: Dual Banking System
Malaysia operates a dual banking system: conventional banking in tandem with Islamic banking. Both are regulated and supervised by BNM.

- Islamic financing share: ~46% of total banking loans/financing (2025)
- Malaysia ranks #1 globally on Islamic Finance Development Index (IFDI) 2025
- Total banking system assets: ~RM 3.8 trillion (2025)
- Number of licensed banks: 27 commercial banks, 17 Islamic banks (including Islamic banking windows)

## Part 3: Payment Systems

### National Payment Infrastructure
- **PayNet** (Payments Network Malaysia Sdn Bhd) — National payment system operator and central infrastructure provider
- **FPX** (Financial Process Exchange) — Online real-time bill payments and e-commerce
- **DuitNow** — Real-time P2P fund transfers 24/7 (by mobile number or NRIC)
- **DuitNow QR** — National QR payment standard, cross-bank merchant payments
- **MyDebit** — National debit card network (ATM/Eftpos)
- **MyDebit Online** — Secure e-commerce debit card payments
- **IBG** (Interbank GIRO) — Batch transfer (being phased out in favour of Instant Transfer)
- **Instant Transfer** — Real-time fund transfers between banks (processing fee: RM 0.50)

## Part 4: Banking Regulation

### Capital Adequacy (Basel III)
| Ratio | Requirement | Status |
|-------|-------------|--------|
| Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) | ≥ 4.5% | System average: ~14% (well above minimum) |
| Capital Conservation Buffer (CCB) | 2.5% | Included in CET1 target |
| Total Capital Adequacy Ratio (CAR) | ≥ 10.5% | System average: ~18% |
| Countercyclical Buffer (CCyB) | 0% (current) | Can be raised by BNM |
| Leverage Ratio | ≥ 3% | Met by all banks |

### Liquidity
| Ratio | Requirement |
|-------|-------------|
| Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR) | ≥ 100% |
| Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR) | ≥ 100% |
| Statutory Reserve Requirement (SRR) | 2.00% (effective 2025, reduced from 3%) |

### Deposit Insurance (PIDM)
- Coverage: Up to RM 250,000 per depositor per bank
- Coverage also applies to Islamic deposit accounts (same limit)
- Takaful and insurance benefits covered by PIDM (up to RM 500,000 per life policy)
- Premium: Risk-based, paid by member institutions

### Selected Banking Regulations
| Regulation | Purpose |
|------------|---------|
| FSA 2013 / IFSA 2013 | Primary banking legislation |
| BNM Credit Risk Policy | Minimum credit underwriting standards |
| BNM Responsible Lending Guidelines | Affordability assessment, DSR cap |
| Anti-Money Laundering (AMLA) | AML/CFT requirements for banks |
| BNM Technology Risk Management | IT/cyber risk for banks (2025 update) |
| BNM Outsourcing Policy | Third-party and cloud outsourcing rules |

### Regulatory Transition
Digital banks operate under a phased transition period (3-5 years):
- **Phase 1**: Simplified regulatory requirements, capped exposures
- **Phase 2 (post-transition)**: Full FSA/IFSA compliance
- Capital requirement: RM 100M (Phase 1) → RM 300M (steady-state)

## Part 8: Key Reminders

- **Dual banking system** is unique — Islamic banking ≈ 46% of total financing
- **DFIs** serve niche segments (SMEs, agriculture, exports, development) — often with subsidised rates
- **DuitNow** is Malaysia's instant payment backbone — 24/7 P2P transfers
- **PIDM** covers up to RM 250,000 per depositor per bank — full coverage for most retail depositors
- **OPR at 2.75%** as of 2026 — rate environment supports moderate loan growth
- **Basel III capital** buffers are well above minimum (CET1 ~14%, CAR ~18%)
- **Digital banks** in transition phase — caps and simplified rules until steady-state (2028-2030)
- **Banking system** remains well-capitalised and liquid despite global uncertainties
- **OPR directional bias**: BNM remains data-dependent — monitoring inflation and growth balance
