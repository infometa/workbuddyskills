# Malaysia Sales and Service Tax (SST) Guide
# 马来西亚销售与服务税指南
# Source: Compiled from Royal Malaysian Customs Department (RMCD) / LHDNM guidelines as of 2025
# Last Updated: 2025-07

## Overview of SST

Sales and Service Tax (SST) is a single-stage consumption tax implemented by the Royal Malaysian Customs Department (RMCD/JKDM). It replaced the Goods and Services Tax (GST) on September 1, 2018. SST consists of two components: Sales Tax and Service Tax.

## Part 1: Sales Tax

### 1.1 Legal Framework
- Sales Tax Act 2018 (Act 806)
- Sales Tax (Exempted Persons) Order 2018
- Sales Tax (Exempted Goods) Order 2018
- Enforced: September 1, 2018

### 1.2 Tax Rates Structure (effective July 1, 2025)

Category A - Basic Necessities: 0%
Examples: Unprocessed food (chicken, fish, vegetables, local fruits), processed staples (flour, sugar, salt, cooking oil), medicines, books, newspapers, fertilizers

Category B - Non-essential Goods: 5%
Examples: Abalone, lobster, cheese, king crab, salmon, cod, truffles, essential oils, silk

Category C - Luxury Goods: 10%
Examples: Shark fin, alcoholic beverages, leather goods, racing bicycles, antique hand-paintings

### 1.3 Low Value Goods (LVG) Tax
- Effective: January 1, 2024
- 10% sales tax on imported LVG valued at RM500 and below
- Applicable to online sales and imported via air, sea, or land

### 1.4 Registration Threshold
- Manufacturers: Must register if annual taxable sales exceed RM500,000 in past 12 months
- Importers: No separate registration; tax is collected at customs clearance

### 1.5 Exemptions
Exempted Persons - Federal/State Governments, Local Authorities, Armed Forces, Public Higher Education Institutions, Free Zone operators
Exempted Goods - Live animals, fish, meat, eggs, milk, vegetables, fruits, bread, books, magazines, newspapers, medicines & medical supplies, agricultural fertilizers & pesticides, bicycle parts, precious metals (gold, platinum, silver), raw materials for exempted goods production
Export goods are generally exempt from sales tax.

## Part 2: Service Tax

### 2.1 Legal Framework
- Service Tax Act 2018 (Act 807)
- Enforced: September 1, 2018

### 2.2 Tax Rates (effective March 1, 2024)

Group A - 8%: Hotels, serviced apartments, homestays
Group B - 6%: Restaurants, bars, coffee shops (except F&B at hotels where service charge applies)
Group C - 8%: Night clubs, dance halls, cabaret, health centres, massage parlours, karaoke
Group D - 8%: Private clubs
Group E - 8%: Golf courses and driving ranges
Group F - 8%: Betting, lotteries, slot machines, gambling machines
Group G - 8%: Professional and management services (legal, accounting, engineering, architecture, consultancy, IT, recruitment, security, warehousing, parking, sports, secretarial)
Group H - 8%: Credit card services (RM25/year)
Group I - 8%: Insurance/takaful, telecommunications, customs agents, vehicle parking, vehicle service, courier, rental & hire car, advertising, domestic electricity (>600kWh), cleaning, brokerage, real estate, ships/aircraft, commodities
Group J - 6%: Logistics services (supply chain management, warehousing, freight forwarding, port/airport services, cargo transport, e-commerce delivery, customs agents)

### 2.3 New Taxable Service Categories (effective July 1, 2025)

Group C - Health Centres (RM500k threshold, 8%):
Facials, hair salons, barber shops, spas, massage, body therapy, slimming centres

Group H - Financial Services (RM1M threshold, 8%):
Financial service providers earning through fees, commissions or similar payments. Excludes basic banking, Shariah-compliant financing, and life insurance/takaful related services.

Group I - Healthcare for Non-Citizens (RM1.5M threshold, 6%):
Private hospitals, clinics, and allied health professionals (physiotherapists, nutritionists) serving foreign patients

Group K - Rental Services (RM500k threshold, 8%):
Commercial property landlords, machinery or equipment leasing companies. Excludes residential properties, reading materials, or assets outside Malaysia.

Group L - Construction (RM1.5M threshold, 6%):
Construction companies undertaking commercial/industrial buildings, infrastructure projects (roads, tunnels, bridges)

Group M - Education Services (no threshold, fee >RM60k/student/year, 6%):
Private schools, colleges, and language centres offering courses to non-citizen students

### 2.4 Service Tax Registration Thresholds

General taxable services: RM500,000
Food & beverage: RM1,500,000
Rental services (from Jul 2025): RM500,000 (or RM1,000,000)
Financial services (from Jul 2025): RM1,000,000
Construction (from Jul 2025): RM1,500,000
Private healthcare for non-citizens (from Jul 2025): RM1,500,000
Private education (from Jul 2025): RM60,000 per student per year
Credit/charge card providers: No threshold
Approved customs agents: No threshold

### 2.5 Digital Services Tax (SToDS)
- 8% tax on digital services provided by foreign service providers (FSPs) to Malaysian consumers
- Registration threshold: RM500,000 in a 12-month period
- Taxable services: SaaS, cloud storage, online advertising, streaming services

## Part 3: Compliance and Filing

### 3.1 Registration
- Online via MySST portal (https://mysst.customs.gov.my/)
- Sales tax and service tax registration are separate
- For new service categories (July 2025): registration grace period until December 31, 2025

### 3.2 Filing and Payment
- Local businesses: Bimonthly filing
- Foreign registered persons (SToDS): Quarterly filing
- Payment deadline: Within 30 days after the end of the taxable period
- Zero returns must still be submitted

### 3.3 Penalties

Failure to submit SST return: Fine up to RM50,000, imprisonment up to 3 years, or both
Failure to pay SST tax: Fine up to RM50,000, imprisonment up to 3 years, or both

Late payment penalties (updated):
- 1-30 days: 10%
- 31-60 days: 15%
- 61-90 days: 15%
- 91+ days: Up to 40%

Grace period for new compliance (2025 changes): Late payment penalty exemption until December 31, 2025
