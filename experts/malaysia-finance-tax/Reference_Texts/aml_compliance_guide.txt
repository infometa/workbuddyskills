# Malaysia Anti-Money Laundering (AML) & Financial Crime Compliance Guide
# 马来西亚反洗钱与金融犯罪合规指南
# Source: Bank Negara Malaysia (AML/CFT Policy Documents), FATF, AMLA 2001
# Last updated: February 2025

## Part 1: Legal Framework
- Anti-Money Laundering, Anti-Terrorism Financing and Proceeds of Unlawful Activities Act 2001 (AMLA)
- Financial Services Act 2013 (FSA)
- Islamic Financial Services Act 2013 (IFSA)
- BNM: Primary regulator for AML/CFT compliance
- FATF 40 Recommendations (Malaysia is a FATF member)

## Part 2: Reporting Institutions
Financial Institutions: Banks, investment banks, Islamic banks, insurance/takaful operators, money services businesses, payment system operators, DFIs, capital market intermediaries
DNFBPs: Lawyers, accountants, company secretaries, real estate agents/developers, dealers in precious metals/stones, gaming operators, trust and company service providers

## Part 3: Core Compliance Obligations
CDD: Identify/verify customer and beneficial owner, understand purpose of business relationship, ongoing monitoring
SDD: Low-risk customers (government entities, listed companies)
EDD: PEPs (domestic and foreign), high-risk FATF jurisdictions, complex/unusually large transactions, non-face-to-face relationships
Beneficial Ownership: Identify natural person(s) owning/controlling >25%, register maintained and updated, reported to SSM

## Part 4: Reporting Requirements
STR: Submit within 14 days of suspicion determination, protected from liability for good faith reporting
CTR: Transactions exceeding RM50,000 must be reported to FIU/BNM
Cross-border: Cash/bearer instruments >=RM10,000 must be declared to Customs
Tipping-off is a criminal offence

## Part 5: Record Keeping
Minimum retention: 6-7 years from transaction date or account closure
Includes: Transaction documents, identification, account files, correspondence
Must be readily retrievable for regulators

## Part 6: Risk-Based Approach
Institutions must implement RBA at enterprise, business unit, and customer level
Risk factors: Country/geographic, customer, product/service, delivery channel
Mitigation measures proportionate to identified risks

## Part 7: Sanctions Screening
Screen against: UN Security Council sanctions, BNM TFS list
Real-time screening for new customers and transactions
Periodic screening of existing customers

## Part 8: FATF Mutual Evaluation
Malaysia is a FATF member and APG member
Regular mutual evaluation assesses Technical Compliance and Effectiveness

## Part 9: Penalties
Non-compliance with AMLA: Fine up to RM1M, imprisonment up to 1 year, or both
Money laundering: Fine up to RM5M, imprisonment up to 5 years
Terrorist financing: Fine up to RM5M, imprisonment up to 10 years
Tipping-off: Fine up to RM500K, imprisonment up to 3 years
Regulatory actions: Licence revocation, prohibition orders, public reprimand
