# Malaysia Corporate & Personal Income Tax Guide
# 马来西亚所得税指南
# Source: PwC Tax Summaries, LHDN (Inland Revenue Board), iMoney.my
# Last reviewed: June 2026

## Part 1: Corporate Income Tax

### 1.1 Legal Framework
- Income Tax Act 1967 (Act 53)
- Administered by Lembaga Hasil Dalam Negeri Malaysia (LHDN / IRBM)

### 1.2 Corporate Tax Rates (YA 2026)

Standard resident company: 24%
SME resident company (paid-up capital <= RM2.5M AND gross business income <= RM50M):
  - First RM150,000 chargeable income: 15%
  - Next RM450,000 (RM150k-RM600k): 17%
  - Above RM600,000: 24%

SME conditions:
- Paid-up capital of RM2.5 million or less
- Gross income from business not more than RM50 million
- Does not control another company with paid-up capital > RM2.5M
- Not controlled by another company with paid-up capital > RM2.5M
- No more than 20% of paid-up capital owned by foreign company or non-Malaysian citizen

Non-resident company: 24%
Petroleum income tax: 38% (25% for marginal fields)

### 1.3 Taxation Basis
- Resident companies: Taxed on income accruing in or derived from Malaysia PLUS foreign-sourced income received in Malaysia
- Non-resident companies: Taxed only on income accruing in or derived from Malaysia

### 1.4 Residence Status
A company is resident in Malaysia if the control and management of its business is exercised in Malaysia (i.e., where board meetings are held).

### 1.5 Withholding Tax Rates

Interest: 15% (or lower under tax treaty)
Royalties: 10% (or lower under tax treaty)
Technical fees: 10% (or lower under tax treaty)
Contract payments (to non-resident contractors): 10% (or lower under tax treaty)
Branch profits: Taxed at corporate rate (no specific branch profits remittance tax)

### 1.6 Capital Gains Tax
Malaysia introduced Capital Gains Tax (CGT) effective January 1, 2024:
- Disposal of unlisted shares in a Malaysian company: 10% on chargeable gain (or 2% on gross disposal value, at option of taxpayer)
- Certain exemptions apply (e.g., venture capital companies, approved restructuring)

Real Property Gains Tax (RPGT) applies separately on disposal of real property:
- Disposal within 3 years: 30%
- Disposal in 4th year: 20%
- Disposal in 5th year: 15%
- Disposal in 6th year and beyond: 10% (companies) / 5% (individuals, Malaysian citizens)

### 1.7 Transfer Pricing
Malaysia adheres to OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines:
- Arm's length range: 37.5th to 62.5th percentile
- Documentation required for related party transactions exceeding certain thresholds
- Advance Pricing Arrangement (APA) available

### 1.8 Multinational Top-Up Tax (MTT) / QDMTT
- Effective from financial years commencing on or after January 1, 2025
- GloBE Rules under OECD Two-Pillar Solution
- MTT: Applied to Malaysian parent entities of MNE groups operating in low-tax jurisdictions (ETR < 15%)
- QDMTT: 15% minimum tax on Malaysian operations of MNE groups
- UTPR not adopted at this stage

## Part 2: Personal Income Tax

### 2.1 Tax Rates for YA 2025 (filing in 2026)

Progressive rates from 0% to 30%:

Chargeable Income (RM) | Rate | Tax on First Band | Tax on Next Band
0 - 5,000 | 0% | 0 | 0
5,001 - 20,000 | 1% | 0 | 150
20,001 - 35,000 | 3% | 150 | 450
35,001 - 50,000 | 6% | 600 | 900
50,001 - 70,000 | 11% | 1,500 | 2,200
70,001 - 100,000 | 19% | 3,700 | 5,700
100,001 - 400,000 | 25% | 9,400 | 75,000
400,001 - 600,000 | 26% | 84,400 | 52,000
600,001 - 2,000,000 | 28% | 136,400 | 392,000
Exceeding 2,000,000 | 30% | 528,400 | -

### 2.2 Personal Reliefs (YA 2025)

Individual (self): RM9,000
EPF contributions: Up to RM4,000
Life insurance premiums: Up to RM3,000 (combined with EPF total RM7,000)
Education/medical insurance: Up to RM3,000
Medical expenses (serious illness): Up to RM8,000
Medical expenses (fertility treatment): Up to RM1,000
Medical expenses (vaccination): Up to RM1,000
Medical expenses (full medical checkup): Up to RM1,000
Education fees (professional courses/degree): Up to RM7,000
Childcare: Up to RM3,000
Lifestyle (books, internet, gym, sports equipment): Up to RM2,500
SSPN (education savings): Up to RM8,000
Housing loan interest: Up to RM10,000 (for 3 consecutive YA from first interest payment)
EV charging facility: Up to RM2,500
Alimony/ex-spouse: Up to RM5,000

### 2.3 Rebates
Tax rebate for chargeable income <= RM35,000: RM400
Zakat/fitrah: Full rebate on zakat paid

## Part 3: Tax Administration

### 3.1 Filing Deadline
Companies: Within 7 months after financial year end
Individuals: April 30 (e-filing) / April 15 (paper filing) following the year of assessment

### 3.2 Tax Payment
Companies: Monthly instalments (CP 204/CP 204A)
Individuals: Direct payment or monthly salary deduction (PCB/schedular tax deduction)

### 3.3 Penalties
Late filing: Fine up to RM1,000 (individuals) / RM2,000 (companies)
Late payment: 15% compounded (10% initial + 5% after 60 days)
Tax evasion: Fine up to RM20,000, imprisonment up to 3 years, penalty up to 300% of tax undercharged

## Part 4: Tax Incentives

### 4.1 Pioneer Status
- 70% exemption on statutory income for 5 years (10 years for high-tech/strategic industries)
- Unabsorbed capital allowances and losses carried forward post-pioneer period

### 4.2 Investment Tax Allowance (ITA)
- 60% (for general manufacturing) to 100% (for strategic sectors) on qualifying capital expenditure
- Offset against 70% of statutory income (unused balance carried forward)

### 4.3 Reinvestment Allowance
- 60% on qualifying capital expenditure for expansion/modernization/diversification
- Offset against 70% of statutory income
- Available for companies that have been in operation for at least 36 months

### 4.4 Special Incentives
- Green technology (GITA/GITE): Tax exemption or investment allowance
- Digital economy (MSC/PDE status): Various incentives
- Halal industry incentives
- R&D incentives (double deduction or investment allowance)
- Approved Service Projects (ASPs)
