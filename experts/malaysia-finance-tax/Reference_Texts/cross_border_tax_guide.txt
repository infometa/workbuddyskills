# Part 1: Double Taxation Agreements (DTAs)

## 1.1 Overview

Malaysia has concluded Double Taxation Agreements (DTAs) with over 70 countries. DTAs typically reduce or eliminate double taxation of cross-border income and provide reduced withholding tax rates.

### Key DTA Partners & Reduced Withholding Tax Rates

| Country | Dividends (portfolio) | Dividends (substantial shareholding) | Interest | Royalties |
|---------|---------------------|--------------------------------------|----------|-----------|
| Singapore | 10% | 5% (≥25% holding) | 10% | 8% |
| China | 10% | 5% (≥25% holding) | 10% | 10% |
| Japan | 10% | 5% (≥25% holding) | 10% | 10% |
| UK | 10% | 5% (≥10% holding) | 10% | 8% |
| Australia | 15% | 15% | 15% | 15% |
| India | 10% | 5% (≥25% holding) | 10% | 10% |
| Indonesia | 10% | 10% | 10% | 10% |
| Thailand | 10% | — | 10% | 10% |
| UAE | 5% | 0% (≥20% holding) | 5% | 5% |
| South Korea | 10% | 10% | 10% | 10% |
| Germany | 10% | 5% (≥10% holding) | 10% | 10% |
| Netherlands | 10% | 0% (≥10% holding) | 10% | 8% |
| Switzerland | 10% | 5% (≥25% holding) | 10% | 8% |
| USA | No DTA | — | — | — |

> **Note**: Malaysia does NOT have a DTA with the United States. US-sourced income is subject to Malaysian domestic law rates (or US tax treaty rates if applicable from the US side).

### DTA Application Procedure
- Claim DTAs via Form P (or equivalent) in tax return
- Supporting documents: Certificate of Residence (COR) from treaty partner tax authority
- COR must be current and authenticated
- Taxpayers must self-assess DTA eligibility

## 1.2 Non-DTA Countries

For income from non-DTA countries:
- Withholding tax rates apply at domestic law rates (no treaty reduction)
- Foreign tax credit available unilaterally under domestic law
- Double taxation relief limited to the lower of Malaysian tax or foreign tax paid

# Part 2: OECD Base Erosion & Profit Shifting (BEPS)

## 2.1 BEPS Minimum Standards

Malaysia committed to BEPS Inclusive Framework. Implementation status:

| BEPS Action | Malaysia Status | Effective Date |
|------------|----------------|----------------|
| Action 5 (Harmful Tax Practices) | Implemented | Various |
| Action 6 (Treaty Abuse) | MLI implemented | 2019 |
| Action 13 (CbCR) | Implemented | FY 2017+ |
| Action 14 (Dispute Resolution) | Implemented | 2020+ |
| Action 15 (MLI) | Signed & ratified | MLI in force 2019 |

## 2.2 Multilateral Instrument (MLI)

Malaysia ratified the MLI, which modifies existing DTAs to implement BEPS treaty-related measures:

- **Principal Purpose Test (PPT)**: Anti-avoidance provision in covered DTAs
- **Dual Resident Tie-breaker**: Based on mutual agreement procedure (MAP)
- **Treaty Abuse Prevention**: LOB or PPT provisions
- **Covered DTAs**: 60+ DTAs modified by MLI

# Part 3: Pillar Two — Global Minimum Tax

## 3.1 Overview

Malaysia implemented OECD Pillar Two via **Finance (No. 2) Act 2023**, effective for financial years beginning on or after 1 January 2025.

### Scope

| Condition | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| Target | Multinational Enterprise (MNE) groups |
| Revenue threshold | Consolidated annual revenue ≥ EUR 750 million |
| Effective tax rate floor | 15% minimum in each jurisdiction |
| Applies to | Foreign subsidiaries and Malaysian-headquartered MNEs |

### Three-Component Structure

| Component | Short Name | Function | Effective Date |
|-----------|-----------|----------|----------------|
| Qualified Domestic Minimum Top-Up Tax | QDMTT (DTT) | Allows Malaysia to collect top-up tax when local ETR < 15% | FY 2025 |
| Income Inclusion Rule | IIR | Parent entity-level top-up taxation of low-taxed subsidiaries | FY 2025 |
| Undertaxed Profit Rule | UTPR | Back-up allocation mechanism when IIR not fully applied | FY 2025 |

## 3.2 QDMTT / Domestic Top-Up Tax (DTT)

### How It Works
- When the effective tax rate of Malaysian constituent entities falls below 15%
- Malaysia collects the top-up tax domestically (rather than allowing other countries to claim under IIR)
- Prevents revenue leakage to other jurisdictions

### Impact on Tax Incentives
Previously attractive tax holidays (e.g., Pioneer Status 0-10% rate) may result in ETR below 15%, triggering QDMTT:
| Pre-Pillar Two Incentive | Nominal Rate | ETR Impact | QDMTT Exposure |
|------------------------|-------------|------------|----------------|
| Pioneer Status (10-year 0%) | 0% | Potentially way below 15% | High — will trigger top-up |
| Pioneer Status (5-year 70% exempt) | ~7.2% | Below 15% | Moderate — may trigger top-up |
| ITA (60% on capex) | Varies | Depends on capex level | May need modelling |
| MD Tax Incentive (0-15% on qualifying income) | 0-15% | Varies | May trigger partial top-up if < 15% |

## 3.3 Compliance Requirements

### GloBE Information Return (GIR)
- Required from in-scope MNEs
- Must include: ETR calculations per jurisdiction, profit breakdowns, deferred tax adjustments, top-up tax computation, safe harbour disclosures
- **Filing deadline**: 15 months after the end of the relevant financial year (GIR TTR filing)
  - For FY ending 31 Dec 2025: Due by ~31 Mar 2027

### Designated Local Entity (DLE)
- In-scope MNEs must appoint a Designated Local Entity in Malaysia
- Coordinates local filing compliance
- Liaises with LHDN on Pillar Two matters
- Manages reporting submissions and top-up tax administration

### Safe Harbours
- Transitional CbCR safe harbour (first 3 years)
- Simplified ETR calculations available for transitional period
- De minimis safe harbour (ETR ≥ 15% in all jurisdictions)

## 3.4 G7 Coexistence Framework (June 2025)

The G7 June 2025 statement outlined a side-by-side approach:
- US tax regime and BEPS Pillar Two rules can coexist
- Ongoing evolution of implementation approaches
- Malaysia monitors global developments for local adjustments

# Part 4: Country-by-Country Reporting (CbCR)

## 4.1 Requirements

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| Threshold | Consolidated group revenue ≥ EUR 750 million (≈ RM 3.8 billion) |
| Filing obligation | Ultimate parent entity OR surrogate parent entity |
| Notification | Must notify LHDN of filing entity by specified deadline |
| Deadline | 12 months after the end of the financial year |
| Penalty | Non-compliance: Up to RM 10,000 per violation |

### CbCR Content
- Revenue, profit/loss, income tax paid, income tax accrued, stated capital
- Accumulated earnings, number of employees, tangible assets per jurisdiction
- List of constituent entities, business activities per entity

## 4.2 Malaysia CbCR Filing Flow

| Scenario | Filing Entity | Filing Location |
|----------|--------------|-----------------|
| Malaysian-parented MNE | Malaysian parent | LHDN Malaysia |
| Foreign-parented MNE with CbCR exchange | Foreign parent | Parent jurisdiction + notification to LHDN |
| Foreign-parented MNE (no exchange) | Malaysian local entity | Must file in Malaysia (secondary mechanism) |

# Part 5: Foreign Tax Credit

## 5.1 Unilateral Tax Relief

Malaysia provides unilateral foreign tax credit (FTC) under Section 132 of ITA 1967 when no DTA applies:

| Income Type | FTC Limitation |
|------------|----------------|
| Business income (foreign branch) | Lower of: foreign tax paid OR Malaysian tax on same income |
| Dividends from foreign companies | Not taxable in Malaysia (single-tier system) |
| Interest | Lower of foreign WHT or Malaysian tax |
| Royalties | Lower of foreign WHT or Malaysian tax |
| Employment income | Lower of foreign tax or Malaysian tax |

### FTC Calculation
```
Maximum FTC = (Foreign source income ÷ Total chargeable income) × Malaysian tax before credit
```

# Part 6: Controlled Foreign Company (CFC) Rules

## 6.1 Malaysia CFC Regime

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| Legislation | ITA 1967 (Section 138A) |
| Definition | Foreign company controlled by Malaysian residents |
| Control test | ≥ 50% ownership or control by ≤ 5 Malaysian residents |
| Apportionment | Malaysian resident's share of CFC income is taxable proportionally |
| Exemption | CFC established in a specified jurisdiction with substantial business presence |

### When CFC Rules Apply
- CFC resident in a low-tax jurisdiction (tax rate < 15%)
- CFC mainly derives passive income (rents, royalties, dividends, interest)
- CFC has no substantial economic substance in its residence country

# Part 7: Transfer Pricing & Cross-Border Documentation

## 7.1 Related Party Transactions

Transfer pricing documentation requirements under ITA 1967 (covered in detail in transfer_pricing_guide.txt):

| Document | Threshold (annual value of related party transactions) |
|---------|------------------------------------------------------|
| Contemporaneous TP documentation | > RM 15 million (goods) or > RM 1.5 million (services/others) |
| Master File | Group revenue > EUR 750 million |
| Local File | As above + related party transactions exceeding threshold |
| CbCR | Group revenue > EUR 750 million |

## 7.2 Advance Pricing Agreement (APA)

Malaysia APA program:
- Unilateral, bilateral, and multilateral APA available
- APA covers: Method, comparables, arm's length range
- Validity: Typically 3-5 years (can be renewed)
- Application fee: RM 30,000 (large), RM 10,000 (SMEs)

# Part 8: Withholding Tax on Cross-Border Payments

## 8.1 Domestic Withholding Tax Rates (non-DTA)

| Payment Type | WHT Rate | Exemptions |
|-------------|----------|------------|
| Interest to non-resident | 15% | Approved loans, listed bonds, banks |
| Royalties to non-resident | 10% | Approved technical services |
| Technical fees | 10% + 3% (CP38) | None (unless DTA applies) |
| Contract payments (non-resident contractor) | 10% + 3% (CP37) | DTA relief available |
| Distributions from Labuan entities | 3% | Tax-exempt under Labuan regime |
| Branch profit remittance | 0% | Not taxed (single-tier) |

## 8.2 Filing Requirements

| Form | Description | Due Date |
|------|-------------|----------|
| CP37 | Contractor payments to non-resident | Within 1 month of payment |
| CP37A | Payment of WHT to LHDN | Within 1 month of payment |
| P-Form / Section 107 | WHT on interest, royalties | At time of payment |

# Part 9: Special Cross-Border Regimes

## 9.1 Labuan International Business and Financial Centre (IBFC)

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| Corporate tax rate | 3% of net audited profits OR RM 20,000 flat |
| Stamp duty | Exemption for Labuan-licensed entities |
| WHT | 3% on distributions (can be 0% with specific exemptions) |
| Qualifying activities | Banking, insurance, leasing, fund management, captive insurance |
| Substance requirement | Physical office + at least 2 full-time employees |

## 9.2 Principal Hub Incentive

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| Tax rate | 8% (reduced from 24%) for 5 years + 5 years extension |
| Qualifying criteria | Regional operations hub with minimum operational expenditure |
| Qualifying activities | Regional treasury, procurement, strategic business services |
| Location requirement | Must be based in Malaysia |

# Part 10: Key Reminders

- **No DTA with USA** — US-sourced income taxed at domestic rates
- **Pillar Two effective from FY 2025** — MNEs with EUR 750M+ revenue must comply
- **QDMTT (DTT)** — Malaysia collects top-up tax domestically; tax incentives may need restructuring
- **CbCR filing** — 12-month deadline; notification requirement; non-compliance penalty up to RM 10,000
- **Foreign tax credit** available for non-DTA income (unilateral relief)
- **Withholding tax rates** significantly lower under DTAs — ensure COR is current
- **Labuan IBFC** offers 3% rate for international businesses (substance required)
- **Principal Hub** incentive at 8% for regional operations
