# Part 1: Real Property Gains Tax (RPGT)

RPGT is governed by the Real Property Gains Tax Act 1976, administered by LHDN.

## 1.1 RPGT Rates — Budget 2026 (No Rate Change)

| Disposer Category | Year 1–3 | Year 4 | Year 5 | Year 6+ |
|------------------|---------|--------|--------|---------|
| Citizen / PR (individual) | 30% | 20% | 15% | **0%** |
| Company (Malaysian incorporated) | 30% | 20% | 15% | **10%** |
| Non-citizen / Non-PR / Foreign company | 30% | 30% | 30% | **10%** |
| Trust / Co-operative / Society | 30% | 20% | 15% | 10% |

## 1.2 Key Exemptions

| Exemption | Condition |
|-----------|-----------|
| Once-in-a-lifetime exemption | Individual citizen/PR: Full exemption on ONE disposal of residential property |
| Low-value property exemption | Disposal price ≤ RM 200,000 (all categories) |
| Gift between family members | Spouse, parent-child, grandparent-grandchild |
| Transfer of property between spouses | Full exemption |
| Compulsory acquisition | By government or state authority |
| Transfer to/from REIT | Subject to conditions |

## 1.3 Budget 2026 RPGT Changes — Retention Sum

**Effective 1 January 2026** — Retention Sum can now be based on seller's self-assessed RPGT liability, replacing the fixed percentage:

| Method | Old Rule (Pre-2026) | New Rule (2026+) |
|--------|-------------------|-----------------|
| Fixed percentage | Citizen/PR: 3%, Company: 5%, Foreigner: 7% | Still available as default |
| Self-assessment option | Not available | Buyer may use seller's computed RPGT as retention sum |

### Withholding Procedure
1. Seller computes RPGT liability
2. Buyer chooses to withhold based on seller's computed tax (instead of fixed %)
3. Buyer must submit and remit via e-CKHT (Form CKHT 2A) within 60 days of SPA
4. Penalty for non-remittance: 10% per annum

## 1.4 RPGT Self-Assessment System (From 2025)

- **Mandatory self-assessment** from 1 Jan 2025
- Seller must compute RPGT liability and submit via e-CKHT
- Submitted return is treated as final assessment (LHDN no longer issues assessment notices)
- All RPGT filings must be **electronic** via MyTax e-CKHT portal
- Calculation errors: Full liability and penalty risk on taxpayer

## 1.5 RPGT Calculation Example

```
Disposal price: RM 1,500,000
Acquisition price: RM 800,000
Incidental costs (legal, valuation, broker): RM 50,000
Chargeable gain: RM 1,500,000 - RM 800,000 - RM 50,000 = RM 650,000
Holding period: 4 years (citizen)
RPGT rate: 20%
RPGT payable: 20% × RM 650,000 = RM 130,000

Less: Once-in-a-lifetime exemption (if not yet claimed): RM 10,000 or 10% of gain
Net RPGT payable: RM 130,000 - RM 10,000 = RM 120,000
```

# Part 2: Stamp Duty on Property Transactions

## 2.1 Transfer of Property — Stamp Duty Rates (Instruments of Transfer)

| Consideration / Market Value | Rate |
|---------------------------|------|
| First RM 100,000 | 1% |
| RM 100,001 – RM 500,000 | 2% |
| RM 500,001 – RM 1,000,000 | 3% |
| RM 1,000,001 – RM 2,000,000 | 4% |
| > RM 2,000,001 | 5% |

### Stamp Duty Exemptions / Relief

| Category | Relief | Period |
|----------|--------|--------|
| First-time home buyer (up to RM 500,000) | Full exemption on transfer and loan agreement | Budget 2026 extended |
| Property transfer between related parties | 50% remission | Ongoing |
| SPA cancellation/adjustment | Nominal duty (RM 10) | Ongoing |
| REIT property transfer | 50% remission subject to conditions | Ongoing |

## 2.2 Loan Agreement Stamp Duty

| Loan Amount | Duty Rate |
|------------|-----------|
| ≤ RM 200,000 | 0.5% |
| RM 200,001 – RM 500,000 | 0.5% |
| > RM 500,000 | 0.5% (capped at RM 5,000 for residential properties) |

# Part 3: Real Estate Financing

## 3.1 Mortgage Products

| Type | Typical Rate (2026) | LTV | Tenure |
|------|-------------------|-----|--------|
| Flexi Home Loan | OPR + 0.50–1.00% | Up to 90% | Up to 35 years |
| Fixed Rate Loan | 3.5–4.5% p.a. | Up to 80% | Up to 30 years |
| Islamic Home Financing (BBA/Musharakah) | 3.5–5.0% p.a. (profit rate) | Up to 90% | Up to 35 years |
| Developer Interest Bearing Scheme (DIBS) | Not permitted | — | — |
| Bridging Loan | 5–7% p.a. | Up to 80% of project value | 12–36 months |

### Key BNM Lending Guidelines
- **Maximum LTV for 3rd property onward**: 70% (cooling measure)
- **Debt Service Ratio (DSR)**: Usually capped at 60-70% of net income (varies by bank)
- **Net Debt Service Ratio (NSDR)**: Applies to borrowers with multiple financing
- **Tenure cap**: Maximum 35 years for residential property; 25 years for commercial

## 3.2 Developer Financing / Progressive Billing

### Schedule of Payment (SOP) — Standard
| Construction Milestone | Payment % |
|----------------------|-----------|
| Foundation | 10% |
| Concrete frame (floor/roof) | 15% |
| Wall/building envelope | 10% |
| Door/window installation | 10% |
| Plumbing/sanitary | 10% |
| Electrical | 10% |
| Completion of common areas | 5% |
| Certificate of Completion & Compliance (CCC) | 30% (balance) |

# Part 4: REITs (Real Estate Investment Trusts)

## 4.1 Regulatory Framework

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| Regulator | Securities Commission Malaysia |
| Legislation | CMSA 2007, SC Guidelines on REITs |
| Structure | Trust-based (manager + trustee) |
| Minimum distribution | 90% of total distributable income |
| Tax transparency | REIT is taxable at 24% (corporate level), but distributions to unit holders are deductible |

## 4.2 REIT Taxation

| Entity | Tax Treatment |
|--------|--------------|
| REIT itself | Taxable at 24% on non-distributed income; distributions are tax-deductible |
| Distributions — Individual unit holders | **Exempt** from income tax |
| Distributions — Corporate unit holders | Taxable at 24% (with credit for tax paid by REIT) |
| Distributions — Foreign unit holders | Withholding tax at: 10% (treaty), 24% (non-treaty) |
| Foreign sourced income of REIT | Exempt from Malaysian tax (if economic substance in Malaysia) |

# Part 5: Real Estate Development — Accounting & Compliance

## 5.1 MFRS 15 — Revenue from Contracts with Customers

Property developers must recognise revenue based on the transfer of control:

| Approach | Method | Application |
|----------|--------|-------------|
| Full legal title transfer | Point in time | Completed property sales |
| Progressive transfer | Over time | Long-term projects with continuous construction |
| Input method | Cost-based | Recognise revenue based on costs incurred relative to total costs |
| Output method | Milestone-based | Recognise revenue based on physical completion certified by architect |

### Key MFRS 15 Considerations
- **Variable consideration**: Discounts, rebates, incentive payments must be estimated
- **Contract costs**: Capitalise costs to obtain/fulfil a contract
- **Significant financing component**: If SPA period > 12 months
- **Warranty obligations**: Separate performance obligation (if beyond statutory defect liability)

## 5.2 Developer Licensing & Compliance

| Requirement | Authority | Details |
|------------|-----------|---------|
| Developer license (APDL) | Ministry of Housing (KPKT) | Annual renewal; minimum paid-up capital |
| Housing Development Act 1966 (HDA) | KPKT | Standard SPA, defect liability period (24 months) |
| HDA accounts | BNM/KPKT | Developer account regulation; retention sum requirements |
| Building plan approval | Local council (PBT) | MUST obtain before construction |
| CCC (Certificate of Completion & Compliance) | Architect/Principal Submitter | Required for vacant possession |
| Strata title application | JPPH / Land Office | Must apply within 6 months of CCC |

# Part 6: Property Developer Tax Incentives

## 6.1 Affordability Housing Incentives

| Incentive | Condition | Benefit |
|-----------|-----------|---------|
| Affordable housing exemption | Price ≤ RM 300,000 (vary by state) | Stamp duty exemption on transfer |
| B40 housing (Rumah Mampu Milik) | Government-subsidised | Income tax exemption on developer profit for qualifying projects |
| PPR (Program Perumahan Rakyat) | Low-cost flats | Full tax exemption on project profits |
| Developer reserve fund | Set up for defect liability | Not taxable (dedicated fund) |

## 6.2 Real Estate Company Tax

| Income Type | Tax Treatment |
|-------------|--------------|
| Trading gains from property sales | Ordinary income (taxable at 24%) |
| Property development profit | Ordinary income (24%) |
| Rental income | Ordinary income (24%) |
| Capital gains on investment properties | RPGT (if chargeable asset) or income (if trading) |
| Interest income | Taxable (24%) |

> **Distinction**: Whether proceeds from property sale are capital gains (subject to RPGT) or trading income (subject to income tax at 24%) depends on badges of trade — frequency, purpose of acquisition, holding period, development activity, etc.

# Part 7: Key Reminders

- **RPGT 0% from year 6** for citizens/PRs (but 10% for companies/foreigners)
- **Budget 2026** introduced flexible Retention Sum (self-assessment option) from 1 Jan 2026
- **Self-assessment system** from 2025 — all RPGT via e-CKHT; seller must self-compute
- **Stamp duty** on property: 1-5% sliding scale; first-time buyer exemptions available
- **REITs**: 90% minimum distribution; distributions to individuals are tax-exempt
- **MFRS 15** governs property development revenue recognition (over time vs point in time)
- **Developer licensing**: APDL + HDA compliance mandatory; 24-month defect liability period
- **Bridging loans** and mezzanine financing available for development projects
