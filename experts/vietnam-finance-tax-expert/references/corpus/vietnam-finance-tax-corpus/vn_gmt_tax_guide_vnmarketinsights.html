Vietnam Business Tax Guide for Foreign Companies (2026) — CIT, VAT, FCT & Global Minimum Tax

Tax is where many foreign businesses in Vietnam get caught out — not by the headline corporate rate, but by withholding taxes on cross-border payments and fast-moving reform. This guide covers the taxes that matter in 2026: CIT, VAT, foreign contractor tax, the new global minimum tax, and expat personal income tax. It's an orientation, not tax advice.

⚖️ Recently changed. A new Law on Corporate Income Tax (No. 67/2025/QH15) took effect 1 October 2025, and the global minimum tax applies from 2024. Figures below reflect rules as of June 2026 — confirm current rates with a licensed tax adviser.

The one most foreigners miss: FCT

When a Vietnamese company pays a foreign supplier for services, software, royalties, or interest, it usually must withhold Foreign Contractor Tax (FCT) — a blend of a CIT element (often 0.1–10%) and a VAT element (often 2–5%). FCT is easy to forget when drafting a cross-border contract, and grossing-up for it after the fact erodes margins. Price it in from the start.

Incentives — and the global-minimum-tax ceiling

Vietnam offers generous CIT incentives (rates of 10% or 17% and multi-year holidays) for high-tech, large-scale manufacturing, R&D, and disadvantaged locations. But for large multinationals, the global minimum tax now sets a floor: if incentives drop the effective rate below 15%, a top-up brings it back to 15%. The interaction means incentive planning and Pillar Two exposure have to be modeled together.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the corporate income tax rate in Vietnam?

The standard corporate income tax (CIT) rate is 20%. Under the new Law on Corporate Income Tax (No. 67/2025/QH15), in force from 1 October 2025, qualifying small and medium enterprises pay tiered rates of 15–17%, and encouraged projects can access preferential rates of 10% or 17% plus tax holidays. Oil, gas, and certain natural-resource activities are taxed at higher rates.

What is foreign contractor tax (FCT)?

FCT is a withholding tax on payments to foreign organizations that earn income in Vietnam without setting up a local entity — for example a foreign company providing services, software, or interest/royalties to a Vietnamese business. It bundles a CIT element (commonly 0.1–10% depending on the activity) and a VAT element (commonly 2–5%), withheld by the Vietnamese payer. It is one of the most commonly overlooked costs in cross-border contracts.

Does the global minimum tax apply in Vietnam?

Yes. Vietnam adopted the OECD Pillar Two global minimum tax via Resolution 107/2023/QH15 (effective 1 January 2024), with Decree 236/2025 implementing it. Multinational groups with consolidated revenue of €750 million or more must ensure their Vietnam profits are taxed at an effective rate of at least 15%; where incentives push the effective rate below 15%, a top-up applies. It mainly affects large multinationals, not typical SMEs.

How is VAT charged in Vietnam?

VAT has three positive rates — 0% (exports), 5% (essential goods and services), and 10% (the standard rate) — plus exempt categories. Foreign suppliers selling digital services or e-commerce into Vietnam without a permanent establishment register and charge VAT at 10%. Businesses generally use the credit method, offsetting input VAT against output VAT.

How are expatriate employees taxed in Vietnam?

Individuals who are Vietnam tax residents — present 183+ days in a 12-month period, or with a permanent/registered residence — are taxed on worldwide employment income at progressive rates from 5% to 35%. Non-residents are taxed at a flat 20% on Vietnam-sourced income. Many expat packages also need to account for compulsory insurance and tax-equalization arrangements.
