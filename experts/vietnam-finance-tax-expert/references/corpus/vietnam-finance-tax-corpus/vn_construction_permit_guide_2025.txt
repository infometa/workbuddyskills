# The complete guide to obtaining a construction permit in Vietnam (2025 updated)

Securing a construction permit in Vietnam remains one of the most complex and challenging steps for foreign investors launching large-scale industrial projects. Given the continuous evolution of relevant decrees and construction laws, managing legal risk and ensuring timely project commencement is paramount.

## 1. Decoding the pre-construction approval roadmap

For Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) projects in Vietnam, the construction permit (CP) process is a sequential roadmap, not a simple application. The key to successful project management is shifting the focus from the CP submission date to the strategic completion of all prerequisites.

### Why the construction permit is the final milestone, not the first step

The CP is the formal governmental certification that all essential investment, land, and technical requirements have been met. It is the end of the approval journey, not the start.

Investors must secure three prerequisites before the CP dossier is accepted:
- Investment Registration Certificate (IRC): Confirms the legal authority to execute the project.
- Legal Land Use Rights (LUR): Proof of secured land use rights, officially ratified by the Land Handover Minute.
- Basic Design Appraisals: Confirmation that the technical design complies with national building codes and safety standards (FPP and Environmental).

### Key legal framework
The approval process is primarily governed by the Law on Construction 2014 (as amended) and the detailed procedural guidance found in Decree 15/2021/ND-CP.

### Project classification: how project grade determines the licensing authority
- Grade I projects: Large, complex structures often require approval from the Ministry of Construction (MOC).
- Grade II and III projects: The majority of standard manufacturing factories and warehouses are handled by the Provincial People's Committee (PPC) or the Industrial Zone Management Board (IZMB).

## 2. The 4 core stages to completing the Vietnam construction permit process

### Stage 1: Establishing the legal and capacity foundation (pre-dossier)
Before the construction permit (CP) application is even admissible, the investor must secure two main areas of foundation: legal right and technical capacity.
- IRC: The Investment Registration Certificate confirming your legal authority to execute the project.
- Land Use: Valid Land Use Rights, typically demonstrated through a land lease agreement, but crucially, the Land Handover Minute from the competent authority confirming the cleared site is under your control.
- The CP dossier requires a Declaration of Capacity and Experience from the design contractor.

### Stage 2: Mandatory design appraisals (the critical bottlenecks)
- Basic design appraisal (by the Specialized Construction Agency): verifies the design's compliance with local architectural plans, structural integrity, and Vietnamese Technical Regulations (QCVN).
- Fire prevention and fighting (FPP) design approval: The Fire Police Department must issue approval. Review assesses compliance with QCVN 06:2022/BXD.
- Environmental Permit (EP) integration: Under the Law on Environmental Protection 2020, most large manufacturing facilities must secure an Environmental Permit (EP).

### Stage 3: Construction permit application and competent authorities
Essential dossier checklist for industrial projects:
- Application Form
- IRC and ERC
- Legal Land Use Rights Document (including the Land Handover Minute)
- Design Appraisal Results (Basic Design and FPP Approval)
- Environmental Permit (EP)
- Design Contractor Capacity Declaration

The legal processing time for a valid and complete CP dossier is 30 working days.
Realistic total pre-construction approval timeline of 4-6 months.

### Stage 4: Post-permit obligations (before and during commencement)
- Notice of commencement: A written notice of the planned start date must be sent to the local People's Committee and the permit-issuing authority at least three working days prior to commencing work.
- Site management and safety: Ensuring all required safety standards (Law on Occupational Safety and Health) and quality controls are established on site.

## 3. Risk mitigation strategies

### The 3 most common causes of permit delays
1. Design non-compliance with FPP or Vietnamese building codes (TCVN/QCVN).
2. Complexities in site clearance or incomplete land documentation.
3. Poor coordination between multiple regulatory agencies.

### Cases where industrial projects are exempt from permits
- Minor structures: Temporary works used only for the execution of the main construction project.
- Utility infrastructure: Internal technical infrastructure located within the boundaries of the approved main project.
- Internal renovations: Alterations or repairs that do not change the structural load-bearing elements, change the building's function, or affect the fire safety distance.
