How Export Businesses Can Claim and Recover VAT Refunds in Vietnam

Export-oriented businesses in Vietnam frequently accumulate substantial refundable
VAT
balances because qualifying exports are generally subject to a 0 percent VAT rate while domestic procurement, machinery imports, contractor services, and production inputs continue attracting VAT at the standard 10 percent rate.

For foreign-invested manufacturers and trading companies operating procurement-intensive production models, refund recovery can therefore materially affect liquidity management, production scaling, and treasury planning, particularly during periods of manufacturing expansion or export growth.
Which export activities qualify for VAT refunds
Vietnam’s VAT framework generally permits recovery of deductible input VAT connected to qualifying export activities, including export
manufacturing
, contract production, industrial-zone operations, and overseas trading activities. Certain exported services may also qualify where statutory conditions relating to overseas consumption and payment verification are satisfied.
The legal classification of the transaction remains important because different VAT treatments produce different recovery outcomes. Zero-rated export activities generally preserve input VAT deductibility, while VAT-exempt activities may restrict recovery of associated input VAT. Businesses operating both domestic and export activities within the same entity may therefore require allocation methodologies separating recoverable and non-recoverable VAT exposure across operational divisions.
Refund eligibility may also vary depending on how exports are operationally structured. Direct overseas shipment models, export processing enterprise arrangements, on-spot export structures, and exported service transactions can involve different customs, payment, and documentation requirements during refund review procedures.
Vietnam generally permits VAT refunds where accumulated deductible VAT connected to export activities exceeds VND 300 million (US$11,500). Under Vietnam’s VAT regulations and implementing guidance issued by the Ministry of Finance, exporters below this threshold generally carry forward deductible VAT balances into subsequent filing periods rather than applying for immediate reimbursement.
How the VAT refund process works in Vietnam
Once refundable VAT accumulates beyond the statutory threshold, businesses must reconcile VAT declarations, accounting records, customs filings, invoices, contracts, and banking documentation before submitting refund applications to the tax authorities.
Refund applications are generally submitted electronically through Vietnam’s tax administration system together with supporting invoices, customs declarations, banking records, and export documentation. Exporters operating large procurement networks frequently encounter additional clarification requests where invoice timing, customs records, or supplier filings do not reconcile consistently across submissions. As procurement volumes increase, the preparation process itself often becomes a significant internal operational exercise before refund applications can proceed.
After submission, tax authorities review whether declared exports, payment records, customs documentation, and supplier filings align consistently with the refund claim. Refund applications involving large claim values, complex transaction structures, or rapidly expanding production operations may face more extensive verification procedures before approval.
Vietnam applies both pre-refund and post-refund inspection mechanisms depending on the transaction profile and review requirements associated with the refund application. Under pre-refund inspection procedures, tax authorities complete verification reviews before releasing refund amounts. Under post-refund inspection arrangements, refunds may be processed earlier while remaining subject to subsequent audit review. For exporters managing large recurring refund balances, the distinction can materially affect treasury planning and short-term liquidity allocation.
Why are refund claims commonly delayed or rejected
Because refund assessments depend on reconciliation across tax, customs, and banking records, most refund disputes in Vietnam arise from documentary inconsistency rather than direct tax underpayment. Invoice discrepancies, customs mismatches, unsupported export evidence, foreign currency settlement irregularities, and timing differences between declarations can all trigger extended verification procedures during refund review.
Supplier-related verification has also become more relevant within Vietnam’s e-invoicing environment. Since tax authorities can increasingly reconcile invoice data between buyers and suppliers, refund applications may face additional scrutiny where supplier filings, invoice records, or tax reporting histories appear inconsistent.
Verification requirements may increase further where transaction structures create additional complexity. Related-party exports, intermediary trading arrangements, toll manufacturing structures, and atypical cross-border payment flows may attract additional review where tax authorities require further confirmation regarding commercial substance or transaction legitimacy.

Businesses generating large recurring refund claims or operating in sectors subject to greater verification scrutiny may therefore face longer pre-refund review procedures before refund disbursement. In practice, review timelines often depend as much on transaction structure and supplier profile as on the technical completeness of the refund application itself.
VAT refund exposure during manufacturing expansion
The financial significance of VAT recovery typically increases during manufacturing expansion phases. Businesses constructing factories, importing production machinery, onboarding suppliers, or scaling procurement operations may accumulate substantial refundable VAT balances before export revenues stabilize. A manufacturer importing machinery valued at VND 200 billion (US$7.7 million), for example, may generate approximately VND 20 billion (US$770,000) in recoverable VAT before production activity reaches normalized export levels.

This exposure remains commercially significant because manufacturing continues to dominate inbound investment activity in Vietnam. The country attracted more than US$38 billion in foreign direct investment commitments and disbursements in 2025, generating continued growth in machinery imports, industrial construction, contractor services, and procurement-intensive production operations.
For large industrial projects, delayed VAT recovery can affect financing requirements during periods when capital expenditure, procurement commitments, and production scaling occur simultaneously. Refund timing may become particularly relevant where businesses are simultaneously managing machinery imports, supplier localization programs, and export ramp-up schedules within the same investment cycle.
How industry structure influences VAT recovery risk
The operational importance of VAT refunds varies significantly across industries depending on procurement intensity, supplier concentration, and production-cycle structure. Vietnam’s electronics exports exceeded US$130 billion in 2025, while garments and textiles generated approximately US$44 billion in export revenue, and wood and furniture exports exceeded US$16 billion. These sectors typically operate with substantial input procurement requirements relative to output VAT obligations, increasing dependence on efficient refund recovery.
Supplier structure also influences operational exposure differently across sectors. Businesses dependent on fragmented domestic supplier ecosystems may face greater administrative burden during refund preparation, particularly where procurement turnover remains continuous and production cycles are short.
Margin structure creates an additional commercial variable. Industries operating on thinner production margins may experience greater sensitivity to refund timing volatility because delayed recovery can affect inventory financing capacity and procurement flexibility more materially than in slower-cycle or higher-margin sectors.
Digital enforcement and the future of VAT audits
Vietnam’s VAT administration environment is increasingly shaped by integrated digital verification systems.
Mandatory e-invoicing
requirements, customs-data integration, and automated verification processes now allow tax authorities to review refund claims against broader sets of accounting, customs, procurement, and invoice-reporting information.
As refund verification becomes more data-driven, businesses operating multi-entity procurement structures, intermediary trading arrangements, or cross-border manufacturing models may face greater review complexity during refund assessments. Internal coordination between finance, customs, procurement, and operational teams is therefore becoming increasingly important as reporting systems across Vietnam’s tax administration environment become more integrated.
The operational consequences extend beyond
tax compliance
alone. For export-oriented manufacturers operating at scale, refund readiness is increasingly becoming part of broader financial control, procurement management, and manufacturing planning frameworks.
Why VAT refund management now extends beyond tax compliance
For export-oriented businesses operating in Vietnam, VAT refunds increasingly affect treasury management, procurement scalability, financing exposure, and manufacturing execution rather than simply tax compliance outcomes.
