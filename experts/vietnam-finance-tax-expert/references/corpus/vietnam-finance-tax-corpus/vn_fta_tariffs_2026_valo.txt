Understanding the Vietnam Tariff Structure
Vietnam built its tariff system around the international Harmonized System. Every imported product receives a code that sets its duty rate. The system separates importers into three clear categories.
The Three Tariff Tiers
Vietnam applies different rates depending on the origin country and trade relationship. Each tier carries its own qualifying conditions.
Ordinary rates:
These apply to goods from countries without normal trade relations with Vietnam. They are the highest rates in the schedule.
MFN rates:
These apply to World Trade Organization members with normal trade relations. They serve as the default baseline rate.
Special preferential rates:
These apply to goods from FTA partner countries. They are usually the lowest available rates.

How Duties Are Calculated
Vietnam calculates import duty on the CIF value of goods. CIF means Cost, Insurance, and Freight combined. The duty is a percentage of this total value.
VAT then applies on top of the combined CIF plus duty amount. This means you pay VAT on the duty itself. Standard VAT rates for imports are 0%, 5%, or 10%.
Luxury and health-impacting goods carry extra charges. The Special Consumption Tax targets items like alcohol, tobacco, and automobiles. Buyers must factor this into total landed cost.
How HS and HTS Codes Affect Your Vietnam Tariffs 2026
The single most important factor in your duty rate is classification. There is no single Vietnam tariff rate. Your rate depends entirely on your product code and the trade agreement used.
What Is an HS Code?
The Harmonized System code is a global product classification standard. Vietnam customs uses it to assign every import a specific duty rate. The first six digits are universal worldwide.
Vietnam adds further digits for national-level detail. These extra digits refine the product description and rate. Accurate classification is the foundation of compliant importing.
HS Codes Versus HTS Codes
Buyers often confuse these two terms. Both build on the same global framework but serve different markets.
HS code:
The international six-digit standard used by Vietnam and most countries for imports.
HTS code:
The US Harmonized Tariff Schedule code, which extends the HS system to ten digits for American imports.
When you import goods
into
Vietnam, you use HS codes. When you export Vietnamese goods
to
the United States, the buyer uses HTS codes. Both must match the true nature of the product.
Why Classification Errors Cost Money
Misclassification creates real financial and legal risk. Customs may hold your shipment for inspection if documentation does not match. Storage fees and delays follow quickly.
A wrong code can also strip away FTA eligibility. You then pay full MFN rates instead of zero.

The Certificate of Origin Requirement
FTA benefits are never automatic. You must prove your goods qualify under the agreement's rules. The Certificate of Origin is the key document.
This certificate confirms the goods meet Rules of Origin standards. Without it, customs applies the standard MFN rate. With it, qualifying goods can enter at zero percent.

EVFTA: Tariff Elimination With Europe
The EU-Vietnam Free Trade Agreement entered into force in August 2020. It is one of the most ambitious deals Vietnam has ever signed. The agreement reshapes trade between Vietnam and the European Union.

How Much the EVFTA Eliminates
The EVFTA removes tariffs on nearly all goods over a phased schedule. The EVFTA offers Vietnamese exporters tariff elimination on almost 99% of goods traded with the European Union. The phase-out runs up to seven years for the EU and ten years for Vietnam.
By 2026, the vast majority of these tariff lines have already reached zero. This makes Europe a near duty-free market for qualifying Vietnamese goods. The results are visible in surging trade volumes.
The Rules of Origin Catch
EVFTA benefits depend on strict origin compliance. Garments face a "fabric-forward" rule, for example. The fabric must be woven in Vietnam, the EU, or an approved partner country.
This rule prevents simple repackaging of foreign goods. Manufacturers must control their supply chain inputs.

CPTPP: Access Across the Pacific
The Comprehensive and Progressive Agreement for Trans-Pacific Partnership entered into force for Vietnam in January 2019. It links Vietnam to major Pacific Rim economies. These include Japan, Canada, Australia, and Mexico.
CPTPP Tariff Commitments
The CPTPP opens broad market access for Vietnamese goods. CPTPP members will eliminate import duties of 97% to 100% of tariff lines for goods originating from Vietnam. Many goods reached zero duty immediately on entry into force.
Vietnam also committed to reducing nearly all of its own tariff lines. This two-way openness drives investment and competitiveness. The agreement uses three reduction paths.
Immediate elimination:
Certain products reached zero duty the moment the agreement took effect.
Scheduled elimination:
Other tariffs phase out gradually along a fixed annual timeline.
Tariff-rate quotas:
Some sensitive goods receive limited zero-duty volumes before higher rates apply.
EVFTA and CPTPP Compared
Both agreements deliver deep tariff cuts but serve different regions. The EVFTA targets the European Union market. The CPTPP targets Pacific economies.
Buyers should choose the agreement that fits their trade lane. Some goods may qualify under more than one deal.

Recent Updates to Vietnam Tariffs 2026
The trade landscape shifted sharply in early 2026. Buyers exporting Vietnamese goods to the United States saw major changes. These updates affect sourcing decisions worldwide.
The US Court Ruling and Its Impact
A key US legal decision reshaped tariffs on Vietnamese exports. On February 20, 2026, the US Supreme Court ruled that the Trump administration lacked statutory authority under IEEPA to impose broad reciprocal tariffs. US Customs stopped collecting those specific tariffs in late February.
New duties then arrived under different legal authorities. Vietnam is among the biggest beneficiaries, with average US tariffs on its goods falling to about 16 per cent from 21.6 per cent after a 10 per cent Section 122 surcharge took effect on February 24. The situation remains fluid as negotiations continue.
What This Means for Buyers
Tariff risk modeling must now be dynamic rather than static. Rates can change with court rulings and new trade deals. Buyers need real-time data, not last year's assumptions.
Origin rules also face tighter enforcement. Goods must be substantially transformed in Vietnam to claim Vietnamese origin. Simple assembly of foreign parts no longer qualifies.

Frequently Asked Questions About Vietnam Tariffs 2026
1. What is the average import tariff in Vietnam for 2026?
Vietnam's average MFN import duty is around 7.9%. Rates vary widely by product, from 0% on many raw materials to far higher on goods like tobacco and alcohol. Your exact rate depends on your HS code.
2. How can I reduce my Vietnam import duties legally?
Use a Free Trade Agreement such as the EVFTA or CPTPP. You must provide a valid Certificate of Origin proving the goods meet Rules of Origin standards. Qualifying goods can then enter at zero percent.
3. Do the United States and Vietnam have a free trade agreement?
No, the United States and Vietnam do not have a free trade agreement. Vietnamese goods entering the US pay standard MFN rates plus any current surcharges. Vietnam does hold Permanent Normal Trade Relations status with the US.
4. How is import duty calculated in Vietnam?
Duty is calculated as a percentage of the CIF value, meaning Cost, Insurance, and Freight combined. VAT is then applied on the combined CIF plus duty amount. Special Consumption Tax may apply to luxury goods.
5. Why does my HS code matter so much for tariffs?
Your HS code sets your exact duty rate and FTA eligibility. A wrong code can trigger inspections, storage fees, and full MFN rates instead of zero. Accurate classification is essential for compliant, cost-effective importing.
