Results of Economic Concentration Control in Vietnam in the Period of 2020–2024 According to Competition Law

15/07/2025 - Results of Economic Concentration Control in Vietnam in the Period of 2020–2024 According to Competition Law

Economic Concentration (abbreviated  EC), commonly known as the form of Mergers and Acquisitions (M&A), refers to transactions aimed at transferring control over enterprises through capital investment, share acquisition, consolidation, or merger. This is a critical instrument for businesses to restructure, expand market share, optimize resources, and enhance their competitive position.

However, EC may lead to economic power concentration, reducing the number of independent firms in the market and potentially creating dominant or monopolistic entities. Such entities may exploit their market power to the detriment of competitors and consumers. Therefore, competition authorities must exercise control over EC activities to ensure fair competition, prevent anti-competitive or monopolistic behavior, and safeguard consumer interests.

In Vietnam, the mechanism for controlling EC was first introduced in the 2004 Competition Law, which laid the foundation for establishing a competitive market economy. According to Chapter IV of this Law, EC activities such as mergers, consolidations, acquisitions, or joint ventures that significantly increase market power or potentially restrain competition in the Vietnamese market are prohibited. Nonetheless, this first-generation regulatory framework primarily relied on a post-transaction combined market share threshold of 30% and lacked mandatory pre-merger notification requirements or in-depth quantitative assessment tools.

The rapid development of the market and the vibrant wave of domestic and international M&A activities after 2015 exposed shortcomings in the existing legal framework. This prompted the urgent need for reform, culminating in the promulgation of the 2018 Competition Law, which replaced the 2004 Law and introduced a more transparent, modern, and internationally aligned regime.

Legal Framework for Economic Concentration Control under the 2018 Competition Law

The 2018 Competition Law, effective from July 1, 2019, along with Decree No. 35/2020/ND-CP dated March 24, 2020, detailing the implementation of certain provisions of the Law (effective from May 15, 2020), adopted a new approach and addressed key limitations of the previous legal regime. The main provisions governing EC control include:

1. Forms of Economic Concentration: Article 29 of the Law defines EC to include merger, consolidation, acquisition of enterprises, joint ventures, and other forms as prescribed by law.

2. Prohibited Economic Concentration: Article 30 prohibits EC that causes or is likely to cause substantial anti-competitive effects in the Vietnamese market. The assessment of such effects is based on criteria stipulated in Article 31 of the Law and Article 14 of Decree No. 35/2020/ND-CP. Among these, the combined market share in the relevant market is a key determinant.

3. Notification Requirement: Enterprises involved in EC must submit a pre-transaction notification dossier to Viet Nam Competition Commission (VCC) if their transaction meets the notification thresholds under the Law.

4. Notification Thresholds (Article 13, Decree No. 35/2020/ND-CP): A transaction is subject to mandatory notification if any of the following criteria are met: Total assets in Vietnam of the enterprise/group involved: ≥ VND 3,000 billion; Total sales or purchase revenue in Vietnam of the enterprise/group: ≥ VND 3,000 billion; Value of the transaction: ≥ VND 1,000 billion; Combined market share of the participating enterprises in the relevant market: ≥ 20%.

For credit institutions, insurers, and securities companies, specific thresholds are separately prescribed.

5. Review Procedure: Viet Nam Competition Commission (under the Ministry of Industry and Trade) is the competent authority for conducting preliminary reviews (within 30 days) and full assessments (within 90 days, extendable by 60 days) if the transaction qualifies for in-depth review.

From 2020 to early April 2023, the Vietnam Competition and Consumer Authority (VCCA), under the Ministry of Industry and Trade, was responsible for state management in competition matters. Since April 1, 2023, this function has been transferred to the Viet Nam Competition Commission (VCC), established pursuant to Decree No. 03/2023/ND-CP dated February 10, 2023, which prescribes its functions, tasks, powers, and organizational structure.

On May 19, 2023, the Chairman of the VCC issued Decision No. 28/QD-CT promulgating the standard notification form for EC transactions to facilitate enterprise compliance with pre-merger filing obligations.

Under this legal framework, EC transactions that meet or exceed the thresholds—such as a transaction value of VND 1,000 billion or total assets and total revenue of VND 3,000 billion or more in the previous fiscal year—must be notified to the competition authority for review and assessment of potential anti-competitive impacts.

Review and Assessment of Economic Concentration Notifications

Vietnam’s M&A landscape has become increasingly active, as reflected in the steadily growing number of EC notifications submitted to the VCC over the years: from 63 filings in 2020, to 130 filings in 2021 (an increase of over 100%), and 197 filings in 2024 (a more than 200% rise). This growth highlights the expanding scale of the national economy as well as the growing awareness and compliance of enterprises with competition law requirements.

The majority of EC notification dossiers submitted to the VCC surpassed the thresholds related to total revenue and assets in Vietnam in the fiscal year preceding the notification.

Regarding the entities involved in economic concentration: The parties to EC transactions are enterprises of various types, including joint stock companies, limited liability companies, and joint ventures. Each transaction involves at least two enterprises. The number of participating enterprises has increased steadily over the years, from 128 in 2020 to 554 in 2024 (including both foreign and domestic enterprises). Notably, for the first time in 2024, foreign enterprises outnumbered domestic ones in EC transactions, accounting for 52% and 48%, respectively, of the total 554 enterprises involved.

Regarding the geographical location of economic concentration transactions: Economic concentration transactions predominantly took place within Vietnam, accounting for 60–70% of total notified transactions during the period 2020–2024. However, there has been a noticeable upward trend in overseas transactions, with their share increasing from 30% in 2020 to 40% in 2024 of the total number of EC transactions notified.

Regarding forms of economic concentration: According to regulations, economic concentration can take the form of mergers, consolidations, acquisitions, joint ventures, and other forms. In practice, only two (02) pure consolidation transactions were notified to the Viet Nam Competition Commission (VCC) in 2024. Acquisitions have consistently accounted for the vast majority of transactions, representing approximately 80% of all economic concentration notifications during the period 2020–2024.

Statistics on Forms of Economic Concentration (2020–2024)

Source: VCC

Types of Economic Concentration: When classified by type, the majority of notified Economic Concentration (EC) transactions were horizontal transactions i.e., those between enterprises operating in the same relevant market. These accounted for the largest proportion of all reviewed and responded transactions during the period 2020–2024, ranging from 46% to 63%.

The remainder primarily consisted of conglomerate transactions i.e., transactions between enterprises operating in different but related markets which generally outnumbered vertical transactions (those between enterprises operating in upstream and downstream markets or complementary markets). Conglomerate transactions accounted for between 25% and 39% of total notifications during most of the period, except in 2023 when their share dropped to just 17%, making them less prevalent than vertical transactions that year.

Sectors Involved in Economic Concentration Transactions:

Among the EC notification dossiers received and processed during the 2020–2024 period, four sectors consistently recorded the highest number of transactions, accounting for 51% to 73% of the total number of processed cases each year. These sectors are:

(i) Real estate,

(ii) Industry,

(iii) Services, and

(iv) Energy and minerals.

Results of the Assessment of Economic Concentration Notifications: During the 2020–2024 period, among the processed economic concentration (EC) notification dossiers, the majority were reviewed at the preliminary assessment stage, accounting for between 63% and 95% of total processed cases. Cases requiring an official (in-depth) assessment made up only 1% to 4% of all cases during this period, except in 2020, when the proportion of officially assessed cases reached 16%. The remaining cases were either returned or withdrawn by the participating enterprises.

As of the end of 2024, a total of 21 cases were still under review by the Viet Nam Competition Commission, of which two (02) were undergoing official assessment.

Transactions that concluded at the preliminary assessment stage were economic concentration (EC) transactions conducted in accordance with the 2018 Competition Law. In these cases, the participating enterprises held individual or combined market shares below 20%—within the safe threshold specified in Article 14 of Decree No. 35/2020/ND-CP dated March 24, 2020, detailing certain provisions of the Competition Law.

Among these, the majority of EC transactions involved market shares or combined market shares in the relevant market ranging from 0% to 5%, with this category accounting for the largest proportion—reaching up to 81% of all preliminary assessments in 2024.

Handling violations due to failure to notify economic concentration

In 2024, the Viet Nam Competition Commission investigated, handled and resolved complaints about the decision to handle a competition case for 01 case of violation of regulations on economic concentration in the chemical sector as prescribed in Article 44 of the 2018 Competition Law.

On May 13, 2024, the Competition Investigation Agency of the Viet Nam Competition Commission issued Decision No. 14/QD-CT on investigating the competition case code 24-KX-TKT-01 related to violations of regulations on economic concentration against Duc Giang Lao Cai Chemicals Company Limited (enterprise code: 5300299830; head office address: Tang Loong Industrial Park, Tang Loong Town, Bao Thang District, Lao Cai Province, Vietnam) and Phot Phosphate 6 Company Limited (enterprise code: 5300813929; Head office address: Lot B24, B24A, Tang Loong Industrial Area, Tang Loong Town, Bao Thang District, Lao Cai Province, Vietnam).

The investigation results show that the enterprises did not notify the economic concentration for the transaction of Duc Giang Lao Cai Chemicals One Member Co., Ltd. acquiring 100% of shares/charter capital of Phosphorus 6 Joint Stock Company (now Phosphorus 6 One Member Co., Ltd.) according to the provisions of the Competition Law, violating the provisions of Clause 1, Article 44 of the Competition Law.

Pursuant to the provisions of the law on competition and other relevant legal provisions, the Viet Nam Competition Commission has issued a decision to impose administrative sanctions in the field of competition on Duc Giang Lao Cai Chemicals Company Limited with a fine of VND 1,323,982,880 and Phosphorus 6 Company Limited with a fine of VND 100,000,000.

Assessment of Economic Concentration Control from 2020 to 2024

Over the past five years, the enforcement of Economic Concentration (EC) control under the 2018 Competition Law and its implementing regulations has achieved notable results, progressively affirming the role of EC control in ensuring a fair and competitive market environment:

First, the legal framework for EC control has been consolidated in alignment with international standards. The 2018 Competition Law and Decree No. 35/2020/ND-CP have established a comprehensive legal basis using a substantive and effects-based approach to assess the impact of EC on market structure and competition. Notification thresholds based on total assets, revenues, transaction value, and combined market share help delineate the scope of control and reduce legal uncertainty for businesses.

Second, the number of EC notifications submitted to the VCC has increased steadily, from 63 cases in 2020 to 197 cases in 2024 (an average annual increase of nearly 40%). In total, approximately 1,000 enterprises participated annually, with the proportion of foreign enterprises fulfilling notification obligations on the rise—reflecting Vietnam’s growing economic integration. Most filings exceeded the thresholds for revenue or assets, indicating the seriousness and legal compliance of the notifying parties.

Third, the notification and assessment process has been structured into two stages: a preliminary review (30 days) and a full assessment (90 days, extendable by 60 days if needed). Over 90% of cases were resolved at the preliminary stage, demonstrating the efficiency and practicality of case classification. Full assessments were conducted only for complex cases involving large market shares or specific market structures.

Fourth, the VCC’s capacity to analyze markets and identify transaction structures has improved significantly. The VCC now conducts evaluations by EC type (horizontal, vertical, or conglomerate), by transaction form (acquisition, merger, joint venture, etc.), by industry sector (real estate, logistics, energy, ICT, etc.), and by geographic scope (domestic vs. cross-border). This classification not only supports statistical reporting but also facilitates early warning mechanisms in sensitive sectors.

Fifth, the VCC demonstrated its readiness to handle complex, cross-border cases—such as the Maersk deal in logistics, Bunge's acquisition of Viterra Limited in agriculture, or Nvidia’s transaction with VinBrain in AI technology—all of which were assessed rigorously and procedurally, showcasing the Commission’s competence.

Sixth, in complex cases, the VCC actively engaged in consultations with relevant stakeholders, including sectoral regulators, business associations, enterprises, and independent experts, to strengthen its assessment foundation and ensure transparency, fairness, and objectivity.

Seventh, supportive implementation activities—including legal education, market studies, and outreach programs—have enhanced businesses’ legal awareness, encouraged compliance programs, and promoted dialogue between the state and enterprises.

In summary, the control of economic concentration in Vietnam over the period 2020–2024 has made significant progress. The legal framework has been improved in line with international norms, emphasizing the substance-over-form and effect-based assessment approach. The increasing volume of EC notifications, particularly from foreign enterprises, reflects Vietnam’s deepening economic integration. The standardized review process, with most cases resolved in the preliminary stage, has optimized time and resources. The VCC's analytical capacity has been strengthened, enabling it to manage complex, cross-border transactions. Meanwhile, stakeholder consultations and business support initiatives have contributed to effective enforcement and affirmed the pivotal role of EC control in maintaining market fairness.
