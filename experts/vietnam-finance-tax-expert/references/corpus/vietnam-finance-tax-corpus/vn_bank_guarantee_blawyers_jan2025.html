Update on new regulations in January 2025

Our Newsletter #30 would lead you on the following:

LEGAL UPDATE

I. Operation of the competitive wholesale electricity market

On 10 October 2024, the Ministry of Industry and Trade issued Circular No. 21/2024/TT-BCT (“Circular 21”) regulating the operation of the competitive wholesale electricity market with some notable points as follows:

1. Subjects of application

The Circular 21 applies to units participating in the competitive wholesale electricity market (“Electricity Market”) including:

  1. Wholesale electricity purchasing unit is a power unit with the function of purchasing wholesale electricity on the spot electricity market (at delivery points between the transmission grid and the distribution grid and at delivery points with power plants on the distribution grid);
  2. Electricity generation unit is a unit that owns one or more power plants participating in the electricity market and signs electricity purchase contracts for these power plants with the electricity purchasing unit;
  3. Electricity system and market operating unit (National Power System and Market Operator Company – NSMO);
  4. Electricity transmission unit is an electricity unit licensed to operate in the field of electricity transmission, responsible for managing and operating the national electricity transmission grid; and
  5. Vietnam Electricity Group.
2. Regulations on registration to participate in the Electricity Market

2.1. Responsibility of electricity generation units to participate in the Electricity Market

  1. The electricity generation units that are obligated to participate in the Electricity Market include:
    1. Power plants with a license to operate in the electricity generation sector, with an installed capacity of more than 30 MW connected to the national electricity system;
    2. Hydropower plants with a capacity of 10 MW or more when the electricity purchase agreement under avoided cost expires (including cascade plants);
    3. Power plants using renewable energy other than hydropower whose contracts expire under the state’s incentive and preferential price mechanisms with a capacity of 10 MW or more;
    4. BOT power plants whose agreements expire and are transferred to Vietnam; and
    5. Renewable energy power plants as prescribed in Decree No. 80/2024/ND-CP regulating the mechanism for direct electricity purchase and sale between renewable energy power generators and large electricity users through the national grid.
  2. The electricity generation units that are entitled to choose to participate in the Electricity Market include:
    1. Power plants with an installed capacity of up to 30 MW connected to the grid with a voltage of 110 kV or higher;
    2. Power plants using renewable energy other than hydropower with an installed capacity of 10 MW or higher, power plants located in industrial zones that only sell a portion of their electricity output to the national electricity system.
  3. The electricity generation units that participate indirectly in the Electricity Market include:
    1. BOT power plants with valid agreements;
    2. Power plants using renewable energy other than hydropower, except for the case in section 2.1.(ii);
    3. Thermal power plants with obligations to maximize the use of gas fuel sources to ensure national interest and are permitted by competent state agencies to indirectly participate in the electricity market;
    4. Power plants in industrial zones that only sell part or no electricity output to the national electricity system, except for the case in section 2.1.(ii);
    5. Multi-purpose strategic hydropower plants;
    6. Imported electricity sources;\Plants providing ancillary services that must generate and start up quickly according to the annually published list; and
    7. Plants operating under the avoided cost mechanism with valid agreements.
  4. The electricity generation units that own power plants directly participating in the Electricity Market are responsible for investing in and completing a system of equipment to connect to the electricity market information system (including: Bidding system, dispatch order management system, electricity market payment support system, internal electricity market information network system), SCADA/EMS system, electricity metering system and digital signatures that meet the operational requirements of the Electricity Market and other systems as prescribed.

2.2. Responsibilities of wholesale electricity purchasing units in participating in the Electricity Market

According to Circular 21, wholesale electricity purchasing units have the following responsibilities:

  1. Registering to participate in the Electricity Market in the case of purchasing electricity at metering locations within its scope, including:
    1. Metering locations for the boundary of electricity delivery and receipt between the power plant and the transmission grid;
    2. Metering locations for the boundary of electricity import and export with the transmission grid or distribution grid;
    3. Metering locations for the boundary of electricity delivery and receipt between the transmission grid and the distribution grid;
    4. Metering locations for the boundary of electricity delivery and receipt between the power plant and the distribution grid; and
    5. Metering locations for the boundary of electricity delivery and receipt on the distribution grid between wholesale electricity buyers.
  2. Investing in and completing the equipment system to connect to the Electricity Market information system, electricity metering system, remote metering data collection system at boundary metering locations within the management scope and digital signatures to meet the operational requirements of the Electricity Market and other systems as prescribed.
II. Bank guarantee over off-plan houses

On 25 October 2024, the State Bank of Vietnam issued Circular No. 49/2024/TT-NHNN (“Circular 49”) amending and supplementing Circular No. 11/2022/TT-NHNN (“Circular 11”) regulating bank guarantees with some notable points on the off-plan housing guarantee as follows:

1. Conditions for banks to provide an off-plan housing guarantee

Compared to Circular 11, Circular 49 has adjusted the conditions for banks to provide guarantees for an off-plan housing guarantee with the following new points:

  1. Omitting the regulations on capacity conditions of commercial banks permitted the off-plan housing guarantee. Accordingly, from 10 December 2024, commercial banks will not be subject to capacity conditions when considering and deciding to provide the off-plan housing guarantee;
  2. Allowing both commercial banks and foreign bank branches (“Guarantor Bank”) to provide guarantees for future housing.
2. Conditions for banks to provide an off-plan housing guarantee

Circular 49 maintains several conditions on the investor’s capacity as follows:

  1. The investor must meet the following requirements:
    1. Having full civil legal capacity and civil conduct capacity as prescribed by law;
    2. The guaranteed obligation is a legitimate financial obligation; and
    3. The credit institution or foreign bank branch that issues the guarantee assesses that the investor has the ability to repay the amount that the credit institution or foreign bank branch must pay on its behalf when performing the guarantee obligation.
      • Of note: in cases in which the commercial bank or foreign bank branch guarantees for the investor based on a counter-guarantee, it is not necessary to meet these conditions.
  2. Additionally, compared to Circular 11, Circular 49 has specified the regulations on the conditions of the investor’s investment project. In particular, the investor has received a document from the provincial-level state management agency for real estate business responding to the investor about housing that meets the conditions for sale or lease-purchase.
3. Procedure for implementing an off-plan housing guarantee

Circular 49 has adjusted the regulations on the procedure for implementing an off-plan housing guarantee between the Guarantor Bank and the investor, specifically as follows:

Step 1: Based on the request of the investor or the counterpart guarantor, the Guarantor Bank considers, appraises and decides to issue a guarantee to the investor.

Step 2: The Guarantor Bank and the investor proceed to conclude a guaranteed issuance agreement instead of a future housing guarantee agreement according to Circular 11. Accordingly, the guaranteed agreement is stipulated as follows:

  1. The guaranteed agreement is an agreement between the guarantor or the counter-guarantor or the guaranteed confirmation party with the customer and other related parties (if any) on the issuance of bank guarantees, counter-guarantees, and guarantee confirmations for customers;
  2. The guaranteed agreement is effective from the time of signing until the guaranteed obligations of all letters of guarantee for the buyer expire and all obligations of the investor to the guarantor under the guaranteed agreement have been completed (except in cases where the parties agree to terminate the guarantee agreement before the deadline); and
  3. The guaranteed agreement must have full contents according to regulations.

Step 3: The Guarantor Bank releases a written commitment to issue a letter of guarantee to the investor so that the investor will send its copy to the buyer when signing the housing purchase or lease-purchase contract. Accordingly, the written commitment to issue a letter of guarantee is stipulated as follows:

  1. Effective from the time of signing until the completion of the issuance of the letter of guarantee before the deadline for handing over and receiving the house as committed in the housing purchase or lease-purchase contract or when the guaranteed agreement expires (whichever occurs first);
  2. The written commitment to issue a letter of guarantee must have the following full contents:
    1. Information about the guarantor and the investor;
    2. Its validity;
    3. Information about the guaranteed project or the part of the project guaranteed (if only a part of the project is guaranteed);
    4. Commitment of the guarantor to issue a letter of guarantee to all buyers who choose to have a guarantee in the project of the investor approved by the guarantor to guarantee for the investor;
    5. Financial obligations of the investor; and
    6. The buyer’s documents sent to the guarantor requesting the performance of the guarantee obligation must be accompanied by a letter of guarantee issued by a commercial bank or foreign bank branch to the buyer.

Step 4: The Guarantor Bank issues a letter of guarantee and sends it to the investor to provide the letter of guarantee to the buyer.

III. Fire prevention and fighting activities

On 31 October 2024, the Ministry of Public Security issued Circular No. 55/2024/TT-BCA (“Circular 55”) amending and supplementing several documents on fire prevention and fighting activities, including Circular No. 06/2022/TT-BCA (“Circular 06”) regulating the process of performing fire prevention, fighting, rescue and salvage tasks in the People’s Public Security with the following notable points:

1. Process of appraisal for fire safety

Compared to Circular 06, Circular 55 has amendments on the working process of officers assigned to handle applications for appraisal for fire safety, specifically as follows:

  1. Preparing a draft Certificate of fire safety design appraisal or fire safety design appraisal document, a written notification of fee for safety design appraisal (if any), and report to their supervisory leaders or headers to submit to the competent person directly in charge of management for approval and signature if the documents meet the requirements;
    • Cases of rejection of application: Drafting a written response stating the reason, and reporting to their commanding officers to submit to the competent person directly in charge of management for approval and signature;
  2. After the certificate of fire safety design appraisal or fire safety design appraisal document has been approved and signed, proceed to issue a document number, affix seal to the document in accordance with law, affix the seal of fire safety design appraisal on the application according to the form; and
  3. Handing over the certificate of fire safety design appraisal or fire safety design appraisal document along with the application bearing the seal of “Fire prevention and fighting design approved” and written notification of fee for safety design appraisal (if any) or the written response accompanying the previously submitted application to the Document response division.
2. Procedure for issuance of practitioner certificate in fire prevention and fighting consultancy

Circular 55 has amendments and supplements compared to Circular 06 on the working process of officers and soldiers assigned to handle the application for issuance of practitioner certificate in fire prevention and fighting consultancy according to the following steps:

  1. Preparing a draft practitioner certificate for fire prevention and fighting consultancy according to the form, reporting to their commanding officers to submit to the competent person directly in charge of management for approval and signature if the dossier meets the requirements;
    • Cases of rejection of application: Drafting a written response stating the reason, reporting to their commanding officers to submit to the competent person directly in charge of management for approval and signature;
  2. After the practitioner certificate in fire prevention and fighting consultancy or the written response has been approved and signed, proceed to issue a document number, affix seal to the document in accordance with law; and
  3. Handing over the practitioner certificate in fire prevention and fighting consultancy or the written response to Document response divisions.
3. Procedures for appraisal of fire safety commissioning

Circular 55 has amendments compared to Circular 06 on the working process of officers assigned to handle the application for appraisal of fire safety commissioning, specifically as follows:

  1. Proposing the content, time, composition of the inspection team, drafting plan for inspection of fire safety commissioning results, drafting the written notification for the investor, vehicle owner and related units; reporting to their commanding officers to submit to the competent person directly in charge of management; and notify the investor, vehicle owner, and relevant agencies and units for approval and signature;
  2. During the inspection of fire safety commissioning results, officers carry out the following tasks: Introducing the inspection team members, informing about the content and inspection plan related to fire safety work of works and motor vehicles with special requirements for fire safety; conducting the inspection of fire safety commissioning results and approving the inspection content;
  3. Based on the inspection report on commissioning results, drafting a written approval odd fire safety commissioning, reporting to their commanding officers to submit to the competent person directly in charge of management for approval and signature in the case of meeting the requirements;
    • If the requirements are not met, drafting a written response stating the reasons, and reporting to their commanding officers to submit to the competent person directly in charge of management for approval and signature;
  4. After the certificate of fire safety appraisal or fire safety design appraisal documents have been approved and signed, proceeding to issue a document number, affix seal to the document in accordance with law; and
  5. Handing over the document approving the fire safety commissioning results or written response to Document response divisions.
