ANTI-CORRUPTION LAW No. 36/2018/QH14
Passed: November 20, 2018, 14th National Assembly, 6th session
Effective: July 1, 2019
Replaces: Anti-Corruption Law No. 55/2005/QH11 and amendments (No. 01/2007/QH12, No. 27/2012/QH13)

Chapter I: GENERAL PROVISIONS

Article 1. Scope of regulation
This Law prescribes the prevention and detection of corruption; and handling of corruption and acts of violation of the anti-corruption law.

Article 2. Acts of corruption
1. Acts of corruption committed by persons with positions and powers in agencies, organizations and units in the state sector include:
a/ Embezzling assets;
b/ Taking bribes;
c/ Abusing positions and powers to appropriate assets;
d/ Abusing positions and powers while performing tasks or public duties for personal gain;
dd/ Misusing powers while performing tasks or public duties for personal gain;
e/ Abusing positions and powers to influence others for personal gain;
g/ Committing fraud in work for personal gain;
h/ Giving bribes or brokering bribery for settlement of affairs of agencies, organizations, units or localities for personal gain;
i/ Abusing positions and powers to illegally use public assets for personal gain;
k/ Committing harassment for personal gain;
l/ Failing to perform or to properly or fully perform tasks or public duties for personal gain;
m/ Abusing positions and powers to cover persons who commit law-breaking acts for personal gain; illegally obstructing or intervening into the supervision, examination, inspection, audit, investigation, prosecution, trial or judgment execution for personal gain.

2. Acts of corruption committed by persons with positions and powers in enterprises or organizations in the non-state sector include:
a/ Embezzling assets;
b/ Taking bribes;
c/ Giving bribes or brokering bribery for settlement of affairs of enterprises or organizations for personal gain.

Article 3. Interpretation of terms
Key definitions:
- Corruption: an act committed by a person with positions and powers who abuses such positions and powers for personal gain.
- Person with positions and powers: appointed, elected, recruited or contract worker with assigned duties and powers.
- Corruptly-acquired asset: asset obtained through corrupt activities.
- Harassment: authoritative or magisterial act causing difficulties.
- Personal gain: benefit obtained through abuse of position/power.
- Conflict of interest: situation where interests of person or relatives influence duty performance.
- State sector agencies: state agencies, political organizations, armed forces units, public service agencies, state-owned enterprises.
- Non-state sector: enterprises/organizations not in state sector.

Article 4. Responsibilities for anti-corruption
- State organizations: implement prevention measures, discover and handle corruption, protect informers.
- Non-state organizations: implement prevention measures, discover and notify competent authorities.

Article 8. Prohibited acts
1. All corruption acts defined in Article 2.
2. Threatening, retaliating against or disclosing information about informers.
3. Abuse of anti-corruption notifications to slander others.
4. Concealing corruption; illegally obstructing anti-corruption discovery and handling.

Chapter II: PREVENTION OF CORRUPTION IN STATE ORGANIZATIONS

Section 1: Publicity and Transparency (Arts 9-17)
- Organizations must ensure publicity of organizational/operational information (except state/commercial secrets).
- Must disclose: policy implementation, public finance management, human resource management, code of conduct.
- 8 forms of disclosure: meetings, displays, written notices, publications, media, websites, press conferences, on-demand provision.
- Information request right: 10-day response deadline.
- Accountability: must explain decisions/actions to affected parties.
- Annual anti-corruption report: government reports to National Assembly.

Section 2: Norms, Standards and Regimes (Arts 18-19)
- State agencies must establish, promulgate and apply norms, standards and regimes.
- Violators must compensate for damage.

Section 3: Code of Conduct (Arts 20-23)
- Prohibited acts for office holders:
  - Causing harassment
  - Establishing/managing private enterprises (except as permitted by law)
  - Providing consulting on state secrets/managed matters
  - Taking management positions in previously managed fields within specified period
  - Illegal use of organizational information
- Relative avoidance: spouses, parents, children, siblings cannot hold key positions (personnel, accounting, cashier).
- Gift rules: no giving/receiving gifts related to official duties.
- Conflict of interest control: report, supervise, suspend, or transfer.

Section 4: Job Rotation (Arts 24-26)
- Positions involving personnel, public finance, public assets, public investment must be rotated every 2-5 years.
- Annual rotation plan must be published.

Section 5: Administrative Reform & Technology (Arts 27-29)
- Simplify procedures, reduce face-to-face requirements.
- Promote national databases and IT systems.
- Non-cash payment: mandatory for high-value transactions, salaries, bonuses.

Section 6: Asset and Income Control (Arts 30-54)
- Control agencies: Government Inspectorate (central level), provincial inspectorates (local level).
- Persons required to declare: officials, military/police officers, deputy managers+ in public institutions/SOEs, election candidates.
- Declaration content: land rights, houses, assets ≥50 million VND, overseas assets, total income.
- Declaration timing: initial, supplementary (annual change ≥300 million VND), annual, pre-appointment.
- Verification triggers: false declaration signs, unexplained increase ≥300 million VND, whistleblower reports, random selection, competent authority requests.
- Verification procedure: 45-day report deadline (90 days for complex cases), 10-day conclusion deadline (20 days complex).
- Penalties for false declarations: removal from candidate lists, cancellation of appointments, disciplinary action.
- National asset/income database: managed by Government Inspectorate.

Chapter III: DISCOVERY OF CORRUPTION (Arts 55-69)
- Self-inspection by organizations.
- Inspection/audit by Government Inspectorate and State Audit.
- Whistleblower protection and reward.
- 15-day processing deadline for corruption notifications (30 days for complex cases).

Chapter IV: RESPONSIBILITY OF ORGANIZATION HEADS (Arts 70-73)
- Heads bear personal responsibility for corruption by managed personnel.
- Deputy heads bear primary responsibility; heads bear joint responsibility.
- Exemption: impossible to know or preventive measures taken.
- Mitigation: proactive resignation before discovery.
- Aggravation: failure to detect, report or handle.

Chapter V: SOCIAL RESPONSIBILITY (Arts 74-77)
- Vietnam Fatherland Front: propaganda, monitoring, suggestions.
- News agencies: request corruption information from competent authorities.
- Enterprises/business associations: propaganda, prevention measures, suggestions.
- Citizens: participate through people's inspection committees.

Chapter VI: ANTI-CORRUPTION IN NON-STATE SECTOR (Arts 78-82)
- Encourage professional/business conduct codes.
- Enterprises must establish conduct codes and internal control mechanisms.
- Listed companies, credit institutions must apply transparency, disclosure, conflict of interest control provisions.

Chapter VII-VIII: HANDLING OF CORRUPTION (Arts 83-95)
- Specialized anti-corruption units in Government Inspectorate, Ministry of Public Security, Supreme People's Procuracy.
- Handling principles: strict handling regardless of position; criminal liability for serious cases.
- Penalties: disciplinary, administrative, or criminal depending on severity.
- Asset recovery: corruptly-acquired assets must be recovered and returned or confiscated.
- Damages compensation: corrupt persons must compensate for damages caused.
- Voluntary confession before discovery may result in leniency or exemption.

Chapter IX: INTERNATIONAL COOPERATION (Arts 89-91)
- Vietnam implements signed anti-corruption treaties.
- Supreme People's Procuracy serves as central authority for international asset recovery cooperation.
- Cooperation includes asset seizure, account freezing, confiscation, and return to lawful owners.

Implementation: Effective July 1, 2019. Government details implementation.
