SECURED ASSETS UNDER VIETNAM'S LAW
Source: Vietnam Law Magazine - vietnamlawmagazine.vn/secured-assets-under-vietnams-law-27584.html

1. GENERAL REQUIREMENTS ON COLLATERAL (Civil Code Article 295)

Secured assets (collateral) are property that securing parties use to secure the performance of a civil obligation toward the secured party. Collateral includes all kinds of assets specified in Article 105 of the 2015 Civil Code that may be tangible property or intangible property, immovable property or movable property, existing property, or after-acquired property.

Collateral must meet the general conditions in Article 295:
- Must be owned by the debtor (exceptions: retention of title and lien on property)
- May be generally describable but must be identifiable
- May be existing property or future property
- Value may be higher than, equal to, or lower than the secured obligation value

2. RETENTION OF TITLE EXCEPTION
Under Article 331.1, the purchaser is not yet the owner of the property until payment obligation is fulfilled. Sellers may use property subject to retention of title as collateral, but purchasers cannot.

Bank risk: If bank accepts collateral subject to retention of title, the secured transaction between bank and seller will terminate under Article 422.5 when purchaser obtains ownership.

3. CIRCULATING ASSETS
A circulating asset is a movable asset in the production or business process used for exchange, sale, purchase or lease. Banks often have difficulty weighing/measuring all quantity of circulating goods, especially agricultural products. Loan officers may face prosecution if inventory records differ from collateral documentation.

4. FUTURE PROPERTY (Article 108.2)
- Property not yet formed
- Property formed but ownership not yet transferred
- Future land-attached property: houses/construction works under construction or not yet approved for use

5. DIFFERENCE BETWEEN PLEDGE AND MORTGAGE COLLATERAL
Only mortgage (charge), not pledge, can be created over:
- Future property (cannot deliver to pledgee)
- Intangible property (IP rights, receivables, shares, account balances)
- Immovable property (no law specifically allows pledge of immovable property per Art. 310.2)
- Circulating goods/inventory (must be sold by company to generate value)

6. SAME PROPERTY SECURING MULTIPLE LOANS AT DIFFERENT BANKS (Article 296)
Conditions:
- Property value greater than total secured obligations at time of establishment
- Must notify subsequent secured parties that property already secures other obligations
- Each security must be established in writing
- Priority order may be limited for subsequent secured parties (Art. 318)

Practical limitation: Land use right certificates are held by first bank, preventing second mortgage registration (Land Law Art. 188.3)

7. PRIORITY ORDER OF CO-SECURED CREDITOR (Article 308)
1. If all security types enforceable against third parties: by time of enforceability
2. Mixed enforceable/non-enforceable: enforceable obligations have priority
3. All non-enforceable: by time of security establishment

8. ENFORCEMENT WHEN ONE OBLIGATION MATURES (Article 296.3)
When one obligation matures requiring collateral enforcement, all other obligations also become due. All secured parties may participate in disposition, with priority determined per above rules.

9. PRACTICAL BANKING CONSIDERATIONS
- Accepting retention of title collateral: risk of termination when buyer pays
- Inventory verification: must thoroughly check quantity and quality
- Value assessment: account for disposal costs and value decline risk
- Repeat mortgages: understand priority order and prior prohibitions
- Immovable property mortgages: certificate held by first bank creates registration barrier
