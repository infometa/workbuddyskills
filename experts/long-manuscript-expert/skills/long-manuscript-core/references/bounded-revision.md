# Bounded Revision

Treat every revision as a scoped, reversible transformation. Preserve the author's meaning and all text outside the authorized scope.

## Establish scope

- If the user names a chapter, paragraph, sentence, quotation, or selection, treat that as confirmed scope.
- If scope is ambiguous, declare the narrowest reasonable scope, provide a clearly labeled preview, and ask one confirmation question.
- If the request implies a whole-document change, describe the affected sections before applying broad edits.
- Never extend a local style request into silent factual, structural, or ideological changes.

## Revision sequence

1. **Scope**: state exactly what may change and what remains locked.
2. **Original anchor**: quote or identify the smallest source fragment needed to locate the change.
3. **Intent**: name the target outcome, such as clarity, compression, continuity, tone, or evidence precision.
4. **Revised text**: provide the complete replacement passage, not scattered suggestions alone.
5. **Change log**: summarize substantive changes and their reasons; omit trivial punctuation noise unless requested.
6. **Integrity check**: verify that names, numbers, terminology, point of view, chronology, and claims still agree with the supplied context.
7. **Rollback basis**: keep the original text in the reply or comparison so the user can reject the revision without relying on host file history.

## Continuation from an anchor

For continuation, the original anchor is the last stable sentence or scene beat. Preserve its open promise and write forward without rewriting the anchor unless the user asks for a transition repair.

When a cross-chapter fact is missing, mark the gap and continue only with material that is supported. A partial manuscript cannot justify a whole-manuscript continuity claim.

## Before and after format

Use a compact comparison when the change is local:

- Scope
- Original
- Revised
- Change log
- Remaining uncertainty
- Single next step

For long sections, quote only enough original text to anchor the change, then provide the full revised section. Do not duplicate private material beyond what the task needs.

## Direct-output minimum

When the user asks for the revision “directly,” shorten the explanation rather than dropping the audit frame. Keep, in this order:

- the authorized scope;
- the smallest original anchor needed to identify or restore the selected passage;
- the complete revised passage;
- a one-line change log;
- one concise next step.

Do not reproduce unrelated surrounding text merely to prove that it stayed unchanged. If privacy or length makes quoting the source undesirable, identify the anchor precisely and say that the original remains the rollback basis in the current exchange.

## Boundaries

- “Rollback” means restoring from the original text preserved in the current exchange; it does not claim an atomic file rollback.
- Do not claim that the revision has overwritten, saved, synchronized, or published a file without a visible successful write receipt.
- Do not convert a user's thesis into an externally verified fact. Preserve it as a thesis or mark it for verification.
