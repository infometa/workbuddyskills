# Quality and Delivery

Use quality review to improve the manuscript, not merely score it. Repair at least one representative high-impact passage when the user asks for finished-draft closure.

## Evidence states

Use exactly one of these states for each quality conclusion:

- `advisory`: the default text-based judgment from current materials.
- `machine_receipt_present`: a machine check actually ran, and the result identifies its tool, scope, and outcome.
- `human_review_pending`: final publication, high-risk claims, rights, or subjective author decisions still require a person.

Never report a machine pass without an actual receipt. A receipt proves only the checks and manuscript scope it names; it does not prove factual truth, rights clearance, or publication approval.

## Finished-draft review

Check the draft in this order:

1. **Purpose and coverage**: the opening promise, chapter responsibilities, critical user requirements, and conclusion agree.
2. **Structure**: headings, ordering, transitions, and section boundaries support the intended reading path.
3. **Continuity**: terms, names, chronology, numbers, point of view, and claims remain consistent within the supplied material.
4. **Readability**: remove repetition, empty abstraction, abrupt jumps, excessive templates, and monotonous sentence patterns.
5. **Evidence**: mark unsupported, stale, or high-risk claims and distinguish user-provided sources from inference.
6. **Delivery readiness**: identify missing title matter, summary, contents, figure references, citations, appendices, or format decisions.

## Required result

Return:

- an overall judgment with its evidence state;
- the highest-priority problems and their manuscript anchors;
- at least one directly usable repaired passage;
- unresolved factual, rights, or human-review risks;
- a delivery checklist tailored to the requested form;
- one recommended next step.

Do not let a high style score hide missing facts or requirements. If only part of the manuscript was supplied, limit the review claim to that part.

## Delivery language

Say “prepared for review” when the content appears ready at the advisory level. Say a file was saved, exported, rendered, or uploaded only when a visible execution receipt proves that action.

Formatting guidance may specify headings, page elements, figure placement, and export checks, but it must not imply that a host application has already applied them.
