MALAYSIA INDUSTRIAL RELATIONS ACT 1967 (Act 177)
Comprehensive Summary for HR & Employer Compliance
================================================================================
Source: MIDA.gov.my, payroll.my, Mahkamah Perusahaan Malaysia
Last Updated: 2024-09-15 (2024 Amendments)
================================================================================

1. OVERVIEW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Industrial Relations Act 1967 (IRA) is the cornerstone legislation governing
employer-employee relations in Malaysia. It is enforced by the Department of
Industrial Relations Malaysia (DIRM). The Act promotes industrial harmony and
regulates relations between employers, workmen, and trade unions.

Key objectives:
- Promote and maintain industrial harmony
- Regulate relations between employers and workmen and their trade unions
- Prevent and settle trade disputes
- Provide a legal framework for collective bargaining
- Establish the Industrial Court

2. SCOPE OF THE ACT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The IRA regulates:
i.  Claims for recognition and scope of representation of trade unions
ii. Facilitation of collective bargaining and conclusion of collective agreements
iii. Prevention and settlement of trade disputes via Minister and Industrial Court
iv. Industrial action: pickets, strikes, and lockouts
v.  Representations for reinstatement claims by workmen (Section 20)
vi. Operation of the Industrial Court
vii. Investigative powers of DIRM officers

3. TRADE DISPUTE RESOLUTION PROCESS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The IRA establishes a structured dispute resolution mechanism:

3.1 Director General for Industrial Relations (DGIR)
Under the 2024 amendments (effective 15 September 2024), significant changes
were made to streamline the process:
- Responsibility for referring unfair dismissal cases shifted from the Minister
  to the Director General of Industrial Relations
- DGIR now receives complaints, determines which proceed to Industrial Court
- DGIR decides which disputes can be resolved quickly
- DGIR presents awards to claimants if a matter is unlikely to settle

3.2 Conciliation Process
- DGIR may conciliate trade disputes
- If conciliation fails, DGIR refers the matter to the Minister
- Minister may then refer to the Industrial Court

3.3 Industrial Court
- The Court hears and adjudicates trade disputes
- Has power to make awards that are final and binding
- Awards may include reinstatement, compensation, back wages
- Non-compliance with awards is a criminal offence

4. UNION RECOGNITION AND COLLECTIVE BARGAINING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.1 Recognition Claims
- A trade union may serve a claim for recognition on an employer
- Employer must respond within 21 days
- If not recognized, union reports to DGIR
- DGIR conducts a membership check/secret ballot
- DGIR decides on recognition

4.2 Collective Bargaining (2024 Amendments)
- Clearer collective bargaining timeline established
- Process can move to Industrial Court if deadlock occurs
- Descriptions of situations moving bargaining to Industrial Court specified
- DGIR will no longer refer disputes on "lack of competence" of a trade union

4.3 Collective Agreements
- Must be in writing, signed by both parties
- Cover terms and conditions of employment
- Valid for minimum 3 years
- Must be deposited with Industrial Court for cognizance

5. INDUSTRIAL ACTION (Strikes, Lockouts, Pickets)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.1 Conditions for Legal Strike/Lockout
- Must relate to a registered trade dispute
- Secret ballot required (at least 2/3 majority)
- Notice period to employer/DGIR required
- Must not be in breach of collective agreement
- Peaceful picketing permitted at or near the workplace

5.2 Prohibited Strikes
- During conciliation proceedings
- After a dispute has been referred to Industrial Court
- On matters covered by a collective agreement
- In essential services without proper notice (7-21 days required)

5.3 Picketing
- Allowed peacefully at or near the workplace
- Must not obstruct ingress/egress
- Must not intimidate non-striking workers
- Must not damage property

6. SECTION 20 — UNFAIR DISMISSAL (REINSTATEMENT CLAIMS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This is the most commonly invoked section by employees:

6.1 Who Can File
- Any workman who considers they have been dismissed without just cause or excuse
- Must file within 60 days of dismissal

6.2 Process Under 2024 Amendments
- Complaint filed with DGIR (not Minister)
- DGIR attempts conciliation between parties
- If no settlement, DGIR refers to Industrial Court
- Court hears evidence and determines:
  a) Whether dismissal was with just cause or excuse
  b) Appropriate remedy: reinstatement OR compensation

6.3 Remedies
- Reinstatement to former position (primary remedy)
- Back wages from dismissal date to reinstatement (max 24 months)
- Compensation in lieu of reinstatement:
  - 1 month's wages per year of service (if reinstatement not ordered)
- Court may also order continuation of benefits

7. ROLE OF THE MINISTER OF HUMAN RESOURCES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- May refer trade disputes to Industrial Court
- May intervene in disputes affecting national interest
- May prohibit strikes/lockouts in essential services
- May make regulations under the Act

8. NON-UNIONISED ESTABLISHMENTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
In non-unionised workplaces:
- Employee seeks redress from supervisor/employer directly
- Employee may lodge complaint with Ministry of Human Resources (MOHR)
- MOHR conducts investigation
- May refer to Industrial Court if warranted

9. DIRM ADVISORY SERVICES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Department of Industrial Relations provides:
- Advisory services on employment relations
- Available through branch offices nationwide
- Guidance on compliance with IRA provisions

10. ENFORCEMENT AND PENALTIES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Non-compliance with Industrial Court awards: criminal offence
- Unlawful strikes/lockouts: penalties apply
- Victimisation of employees for union activity: prohibited
- Interference with trade union rights: prohibited

11. 2024 AMENDMENTS — KEY CHANGES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Effective: 15 September 2024

Major changes:
1. DGIR takes over referral power from Minister for unfair dismissal cases
2. Streamlined dispute resolution to reduce Industrial Court backlog
3. Concrete collective bargaining timelines introduced
4. Alignment with International Labour Standards (ILO)
5. Enhanced DGIR powers: receive complaints, determine which proceed to court,
   decide quick resolutions, present awards directly

12. PRACTICAL IMPLICATIONS FOR HR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- DOCUMENT all disciplinary actions and terminations thoroughly
- Ensure proper procedures before dismissal (domestic inquiry recommended)
- Respond to union recognition claims within 21 days
- Comply with collective agreement terms strictly
- Seek DIRM advisory if unsure about any IR matter
- Maintain records of all employment decisions
