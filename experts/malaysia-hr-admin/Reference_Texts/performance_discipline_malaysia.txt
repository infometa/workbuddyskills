# Malaysia Performance Management & Discipline
## Source: Employment Act 1955 (Act 265); Industrial Relations Act 1967 (Act 177); Industrial Court awards and landmark cases; HRDF best practices
## Validity: 2026

---

## 1. PERFORMANCE MANAGEMENT FRAMEWORK

### Legal Basis
- There is NO statutory requirement for performance appraisal in Malaysian law
- However: termination based on poor performance MUST be substantiated with documented evidence
- Industrial Court standard: employer must prove employee was given fair opportunity to improve AND was warned of consequences

### Recommended Appraisal Cycle
- Mid-year review (June): informal, coaching-focused
- Year-end review (Dec): formal rating, linked to bonus/increment
- Probation review: at 3 months and 6 months (before confirmation)

### KPI Setting — SMART Framework
- Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound
- Weighted: typically 70% quantitative (KPIs) + 30% qualitative (competencies/behaviours)
- Must be documented and acknowledged by employee (signed copy)

---

## 2. PERFORMANCE IMPROVEMENT PLAN (PIP)

### When to Issue
- Consistently below expectations for 2+ consecutive appraisal periods
- Serious performance gap identified mid-cycle (not wait until year-end)
- After verbal coaching and informal feedback have failed

### PIP Content (Must Include)
1. Specific areas of underperformance (with examples and dates)
2. Clear, measurable improvement targets (with timeline — typically 30/60/90 days)
3. Support/resources/training the company will provide
4. Regular review schedule (e.g., weekly check-ins)
5. Consequence of failure to improve (up to and including termination)
6. Employee's right to bring a representative to PIP meetings

### PIP Timeline
| Phase | Duration | Action |
|-------|----------|--------|
| Issue PIP | Day 1 | PIP letter signed by both parties |
| Check-in 1 | Week 1-2 | Review progress, address obstacles |
| Check-in 2 | Week 3-4 | Mid-point assessment |
| Final review | Day 60/90 | Decision: pass → close; fail → proceed to next step |

### After Failed PIP
- Issue "show cause" letter (why contract should not be terminated)
- Employee responds in writing (typically 7-14 days)
- If response unsatisfactory: DI or retrenchment (see retrenchment_termination_guide.txt)
- If DI finds against employee: termination with notice/pay in lieu
- If employee resigns during PIP: accept resignation; no constructive dismissal risk

## 3. DISCIPLINARY PROCEDURE

### Progressive Discipline Ladder
```
Verbal Warning → First Written Warning → Second Written Warning → Show Cause → Domestic Inquiry (DI) → Punishment
```

### Step 1: Verbal Warning
- Informal discussion; documented in supervisor's file
- Notify employee this is a verbal warning
- Set improvement expectations and review date

### Step 2: First Written Warning
- Formal letter detailing misconduct/gap, date(s), company rule violated
- Warning that repeated behaviour may lead to further disciplinary action
- Employee signs acknowledgement (not admission)
- Valid for: typically 6-12 months (then expires)

### Step 3: Second Written Warning (Final Warning)
- Same format; clearly states "FINAL WARNING"
- Consequence: any further instance = DI

### Step 4: Show Cause Letter
- Formal allegation letter with full particulars (who, what, when, where)
- Employee given 7-14 days to respond in writing
- Employee may bring representative (colleague or union official — NOT external lawyer)
- Allegation must be specific: "On [date] at [time], you did [specific action] which violated [rule]"

### Step 5: Domestic Inquiry (DI)
- See domestic_inquiry_procedure.txt for full details
- Panel: 3 persons (ideally: Chair + HR + Department Rep; NOT the accusing officer)
- Employee rights: present case, call witnesses, cross-examine, represented by colleague/union
- Finding: guilty / not guilty
- If guilty: punishment (warning, fine, demotion, suspension without pay [max 2 weeks], or dismissal)
- Must produce written DI report with findings of fact and reasons

### Step 6: Punishment
- Proportionality: punishment must fit the offence (Industrial Court principle)
- Minor misconduct (lateness): warning → fine → suspension
- Major misconduct (theft, fraud, fighting, insubordination): dismissal
- All punishments must be documented

---

## 4. MISCONDUCT CATEGORIES

### Minor Misconduct (first-time)
- Occasional lateness (not habitual)
- Minor errors in work
- Minor breach of dress code
- Minor insubordination (e.g., failure to follow reasonable instruction)

### Major Misconduct (may warrant immediate dismissal)
- Theft, fraud, dishonesty
- Fighting / physical violence at workplace
- Gross insubordination
- Sexual harassment (see anti_sexual_harassment_act_2022.txt)
- Wilful damage to company property
- Criminal conviction affecting employment
- Unauthorised absence >2 consecutive days (with intention to abandon)
- Breach of confidentiality / PDPA / trade secrets
- Substance abuse at workplace
- Sleeping on duty (in safety-critical roles)

---

## 5. SUSPENSION PENDING INQUIRY

- EA 1955 S14(2): employer may suspend employee for max 2 weeks
- During suspension: pay must NOT be less than HALF the wages
- If DI finds employee not guilty: full back-pay for suspended period
- Suspension is NOT punishment — it's to facilitate investigation (remove employee from scene)
- If investigation exceeds 2 weeks: employer may extend with employee's consent OR pay full wages

---

## 6. TERMINATION FOR POOR PERFORMANCE — INDUSTRIAL COURT STANDARD

The Industrial Court requires employer to prove (Ireka Construction case):
1. Employee was informed of the required standard
2. Employee was informed of the shortfall
3. Employee was given reasonable time and support to improve
4. Employee was warned of consequences
5. Employee failed to improve

Without all 5 → unfair dismissal → reinstatement or compensation (typically 1-2 months' salary per year of service, 24 months cap)

---

## 7. CONSTRUCTIVE DISMISSAL RISK

Constructive dismissal occurs when employer's conduct fundamentally breaches the employment contract, leaving employee no choice but to resign. Triggers include:
- Unilateral demotion / significant reduction in pay
- Unilateral relocation without contractual right
- Harassment / hostile work environment not addressed by employer
- Withdrawal of essential tools/resources to perform job

### Mitigations
- All performance/disciplinary actions must follow documented process
- Never verbally berate or humiliate employee (creates hostile environment claim)
- Accept resignation without coercion; do not pressure employee to resign
- Get legal advice before any unilateral contract change

---

## 8. DOCUMENTATION REQUIREMENTS

Every disciplinary step MUST be documented with:
- Date, parties present, what was discussed
- Employee acknowledgement (or "refused to sign" noted with witness)
- Copies kept in employee's personal file (separate from normal HR file)
- Secured access (HR Manager + CEO only)

### Document Retention
- Disciplinary records: 7 years (after employment ends)
- DI transcripts and reports: permanently (Industrial Court statute of limitations: 60 days from dismissal to file claim, but claims can be refiled)
