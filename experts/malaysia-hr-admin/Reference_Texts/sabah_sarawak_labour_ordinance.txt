MALAYSIA SABAH & SARAWAK LABOUR ORDINANCE
Comprehensive Summary for HR & Employer Compliance
================================================================================
Source: Sabah Labour Ordinance (Chapter 67), Sarawak Labour Ordinance (Chapter 76),
Skrine Advocates, Malaysia HR Forum, MyWave
2025 Amendments effective: Harmonisation with Employment Act 1955
================================================================================

1. OVERVIEW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
East Malaysia operates under SEPARATE labour legislation:
- Peninsular Malaysia: Employment Act 1955 (Act 265)
- Sabah: Sabah Labour Ordinance (Sabah Cap. 67)
- Sarawak: Sarawak Labour Ordinance (Sarawak Cap. 76)

Historically, these Ordinances provided LOWER protections than the Employment
Act. The 2025 amendments harmonise East Malaysian labour laws with the
Employment Act 1955 standards introduced in 2023.

2. KEY 2025 AMENDMENTS — WHAT CHANGED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The amendments to both Sabah and Sarawak Labour Ordinances largely standardise
provisions with the Employment Act 1955:

2.1 Coverage
- PREVIOUSLY: Only employees earning RM 2,000/month or below
- NOW: ALL employees regardless of salary (except certain exempted categories)
- This is the SAME as Employment Act 1955 post-2023 amendment

2.2 Working Hours
- Standard hours: 45 hours/week (aligned with Peninsular Malaysia)
- Overtime: 1.5x for standard OT, 2x for rest days, 3x for public holidays
- Maximum 8 hours/day, 45 hours/week

2.3 Maternity Leave
- Increased to 98 days (same as Peninsular)
- Paternity leave: 7 working days per birth (same as Peninsular)

2.4 Sexual Harassment Protection
- Employer now MUST establish formal complaint procedures
- Display anti-sexual harassment notice in workplace
- Aligned with Anti-Sexual Harassment Act 2022

2.5 Flexible Working Arrangements (FWA)
- Employee right to APPLY
- Employer obligation to respond in 60 days
- Aligned with EA 1955 Section 60P/60Q

3. REMAINING DIFFERENCES FROM PENINSULAR MALAYSIA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Despite harmonisation, some DIFFERENCES remain:

3.1 State-Specific Administrative Structures
- Each state has own Labour Department (JTK Sabah / JTK Sarawak)
- Slightly different administrative procedures
- Local Director of Labour has enforcement powers

3.2 Minimum Wages
- National Minimum Wage Order applies uniformly (RM 1,700/month)
- Sabah and Sarawak NOT exempt from national rate

3.3 Local Employment Preferences
- Sabah and Sarawak may impose LOCAL HIRING PREFERENCES
- "Sabahan First" / "Sarawakian First" policies for certain roles
- Immigration/work pass approvals may reflect local preference

4. EMPLOYER OBLIGATIONS — EAST MALAYSIA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Employers operating in Sabah/Sarawak MUST:

- Apply Sabah/Sarawak Labour Ordinance (NOT Employment Act 1955)
- Register with STATE Labour Department (JTK Sabah/JTK Sarawak)
- Issue employment contracts with SLO-compliant terms
- Maintain wage records per state requirements
- Display labour notices in Bahasa Malaysia and local languages if applicable
- Comply with state-specific public holidays
- Report to LOCAL labour office for any disputes

5. COMMON PITFALLS FOR PENINSULAR EMPLOYERS EXPANDING EAST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Using Peninsular employment contract template in Sabah/Sarawak → NON-COMPLIANT
- Not registering with state Labour Department
- Assuming Employment Act 1955 applies (it does NOT in Sabah/Sarawak)
- Ignoring local hiring preferences (may affect work pass renewals)
- Different public holiday schedule (each state has own holidays)

6. SARAWAK FOREIGN WORKERS TRANSFORMATION APPROACH (FWTA)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Sarawak has an ADDITIONAL RM 1,854 FWTA fee per foreign worker ON TOP of the
basic levy. This is UNIQUE to Sarawak and does not apply in Sabah or Peninsular.
