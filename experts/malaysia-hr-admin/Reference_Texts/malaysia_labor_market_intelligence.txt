1. DOSM — MALAYSIA LABOUR MARKET REVIEW Q4 2025
================================================================================
Source: Department of Statistics Malaysia (DOSM)
Release: February 2026 (Q4 2025 data)

Key Indicators:
- Labour Force Participation Rate (LFPR): 70.9%
- Total Labour Force: ~17.2 million
- Employed Persons: ~16.6 million
- Unemployment Rate: 3.2% (stable, near full employment)
- Labour Demand: 9.16 million jobs (all sectors)

Employment by Sector (Q3 2025):
- Services: 64.5% of total employment (dominant)
- Manufacturing: 16.8%
- Construction: 9.4%

2. TALENTCORP — AI/DIGITAL/GREEN ECONOMY WORKFORCE IMPACT STUDY (2024)
================================================================================
Source: Talent Corporation Malaysia Berhad (Ministry of Human Resources)
Published: November 18, 2024
Scope: 889 roles across 10 key sectors

KEY FINDING: 620,000 employees will be HIGHLY IMPACTED over 3-5 years by AI,
digitalisation, and green economy trends — facing potential job displacement
or convergence.

10 Sectors Studied (collectively RM 933B / 60% of GDP):
1. Aerospace
2. Chemicals
3. Electrical & Electronics (E&E)
4. Energy and Power
5. Food Manufacturing and Service
6. Global Business Services (GBS)
7. Information and Communication Technologies (ICT)
8. Medical Devices
9. Pharmaceutical Manufacturing
10. Wholesale and Retail Trade

Impact by Sector:
- HIGHEST impact (by headcount): Wholesale & Retail Trade, Food Manufacturing
  (large employee bases → large absolute numbers affected)
- HIGHEST impact (by %): Global Business Services — 32% of employees highly
  impacted (routine/transactional tasks easily automated)
- LOWEST impact: Aerospace (already integrated AI/digital), Energy & Power
  (heavily regulated, requires human oversight)
- ICT, Aerospace, E&E: <10% of roles highly impacted at risk (specialized,
  human-centric skills)

Role Classification (out of 889 roles):
- 84 roles (~10%) classified as HIGHLY IMPACTED
- 18 of those in Global Business Services alone
- 60 EMERGING ROLES identified: AI Engineers, Sustainability Specialists,
  Bio-Process Engineers, Green Energy Technicians, etc.
- Energy & Power: ZERO highly impacted roles at risk (regulatory safety net)

Government Response:
- MYR 3 billion (USD 670M) workforce fund through KESUMA
- MyMAHIR digital platform: industry trends, emerging jobs, skills taxonomy,
  career pathways, training programs
- Akademi KESUMA (launching 2025): comprehensive skill development
- Phase 2 (2025): expanded to 12 additional sectors

Source: https://www.prnewswire.com/apac/news-releases/talentcorp-launches-landmark-study-on-workforce-impact-of-ai-digitalisation-and-green-economy-302308514.html
Source: https://technode.global/2024/11/20/620000-employees-in-malaysia-will-be-affected-by-the-influence-of-ai-digital-and-green-economy-trends-official-study-shows/

3. MEF — SALARY SURVEY 2025/2026 (ACTUAL & FORECAST)
================================================================================
Source: Malaysian Employers Federation (MEF)
Published: December 2025
Sample: 266 companies, 13,082 Executives + 27,017 Non-Executives

2025 ACTUAL Performance:
- Salary Increase (Executives): 5.04%
- Salary Increase (Non-Executives): 4.85%
- ~90% of companies provided salary increases to BOTH executives and non-executives
- Average Bonus (Executives): 2.19 months
- Average Bonus (Non-Executives): 2.04 months

2026 FORECAST:
- Salary Increase (Executives): 5.33% (ACCELERATING)
- Salary Increase (Non-Executives): 5.01% (ACCELERATING)
- Bonus (Executives): 2.16 months
- Bonus (Non-Executives): 2.02 months

Key Insight for Employers:
- Wage growth ACCELERATING — budgets must account for 5%+ annual increases
- Bonus expectations ~2 months — factor into total cost of employment
- Companies NOT giving increases risk losing talent in a tight labour market

4. KRI — SHIFTING TIDES: CAREER PROGRESSION OF MALAYSIAN WORKERS
================================================================================
Source: Khazanah Research Institute (KRI)
Published: 2025

Key Findings:
- DECADE-LONG WAGE STAGNATION: Entry-level salaries have barely kept pace
  with inflation over 10+ years
- SKILLS MISMATCH: Graduates entering jobs below qualification level
- YOUTH DEBT: Stagnant wages + high living costs → youth increasingly
  reliant on debt upon entering workforce
- Graduate Employability: High but UNDEREMPLOYMENT is the real problem
  (working below qualification level)
- Career Progression: Slow; many workers stay in same wage bracket for
  5+ years without significant advancement

Implication for Employers:
- Competitive salaries above RM 3,000/month needed to attract graduates
- Career progression pathways are ESSENTIAL retention tools
- Training and upskilling = competitive advantage in talent market

Source: https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/684b55df28cddcbe52b406f2/68a7ba7a584b1fb875c3a5a2_KRI%20Booklet%20-%20Shifting%20Tides.pdf

7. PRACTICAL IMPLICATIONS FOR EMPLOYERS HIRING IN MALAYSIA
================================================================================

Based on synthesis of ALL above authoritative sources:

[1] BUDGET FOR 5%+ ANNUAL WAGE GROWTH
MEF data shows salaries rising 5%+ per year. Budget must factor this in for
multi-year headcount planning.

[2] EXPECT ~2 MONTHS BONUS
Standard practice per MEF survey. Include in total cost calculations.

[3] COMPETITIVE ENTRY SALARY = RM 2,800-3,500 FOR GRADUATES
Per KRI Shifting Tides + DOSM data. Below RM 2,800 will struggle to attract.

[4] TECH/DIGITAL ROLES COMMAND 15-30% PREMIUM
Per TalentCorp Impact Study — AI/digital skills scarce, 620K workers need
reskilling. Employers competing for the same talent pool.

[5] SKILLS MISMATCH IS THE #1 HIRING CHALLENGE
Not unemployment (3.2% is low) — the issue is FINDING people with RIGHT skills.
Invest in training pipelines, don't just post job ads.

[6] KL PREMIUM ~50% ABOVE NATIONAL MEDIAN
National median: RM 2,793; KL median: ~RM 4,200. Budget accordingly for
KL-based roles.

[7] GBS/GLOBAL BUSINESS SERVICES = HIGHEST AUTOMATION RISK
If hiring for routine transactional roles (data entry, basic processing),
expect rapid change — plan for upskilling to higher-value functions.
