# Egypt Economic and Political Risk Assessment 2025
# Source: Middle East Observer, IMF, CBE, EBRD
# Retrieved: 2026-07-09

## 2025: Egypt's Shift from Crisis to Stability

Egypt ended 2025 with renewed international confidence after the International Monetary Fund approved the fifth and sixth reviews of its economic reform programme, unlocking $2.5 billion in fresh financing.

### IMF Programme Progress
- Egypt exceeded around 90 percent of key IMF targets
- Key areas: primary fiscal surplus, tax revenues, debt management, social protection
- 12 of 14 structural reforms completed
- Reforms span: energy efficiency, tax and customs administration, fiscal transparency, expanded social safety nets

### Macroeconomic Conditions
- Economic growth rebounded to 4.4 percent in FY 2024/25 (up from 2.4 percent a year earlier)
- Growth accelerated to 5.3 percent year-on-year in Q1 FY 2025/26
- Growth drivers: non-oil manufacturing, transport, finance, tourism
- Current account deficit narrowed
- Foreign reserves climbed to $56.9 billion
- Strong remittances, tourism receipts, and rising non-oil exports supported external balances

### Fiscal Discipline
- Primary surplus of 3.5 percent of GDP in FY 2024/25
- Tax revenues surged more than 30 percent following reforms that broadened the base and improved compliance
- Central Bank of Egypt maintained cautious monetary stance
- Inflation retreated sharply from earlier peaks

### Government Priorities for 2026
- Higher primary surpluses
- Further tax reform
- Accelerated exports
- Deeper private-sector participation
- Continued expansion of social programmes (Takaful and Karama)

### IMF Caution
- Still-modest tax-to-GDP ratio
- Need to curb state's footprint in the economy
- Stabilization gains provide platform for more competitive, private-sector-led growth model

## Key Risks

### Political and Social Risks
- Regional geopolitical tensions (Middle East, Red Sea security)
- Social pressures from inflation and subsidy reforms
- Need to balance fiscal consolidation with social protection

### Economic Risks
- Foreign exchange volatility and reserve adequacy
- Debt sustainability concerns
- State footprint crowding out private sector
- Modest tax-to-GDP ratio limiting fiscal space

### External Risks
- Global interest rate environment
- Commodity price shocks
- Tourism and Suez Canal revenue vulnerability to regional instability
- Remittance flows dependent on Gulf economies

## Sovereign Rating
- Fitch affirmed Egypt at 'B' with Stable Outlook (October 2025)
- Rating supported by large economy, improving growth momentum, steady external buffers
- Constrained by high government debt, foreign-currency liquidity risks, and geopolitical risks

## Red Sea / Suez Canal Impact
- Suez Canal revenues dropped 45.5% to $3.6 billion in FY 2024/2025
- Net tonnage dropped 55.1% to 482.8 million tons
- Transiting ships decreased 38.5% to around 12,400 vessels
- Regional conflict and Houthi attacks in Bab al-Mandab significantly disrupted traffic
- Recovery depends on regional security stabilization

## Investment Implications

Positive signals:
- Firming macroeconomic foundations
- Improved policy credibility
- Reform agenda translating into growth
- Large domestic market and strategic location
- Active IMF programme providing external anchor

Cautious considerations:
- Regional security uncertainties
- Foreign exchange constraints
- State dominance in some sectors
- Implementation risks on structural reforms
- Need for continued private-sector opening

## Recommended Monitoring
- IMF programme reviews and disbursements
- CBE foreign reserve levels
- EGP exchange rate dynamics
- Inflation trajectory
- Primary fiscal surplus
- Tourism and Suez Canal receipts
- Regional security developments
- Progress on state-owned enterprise reform
