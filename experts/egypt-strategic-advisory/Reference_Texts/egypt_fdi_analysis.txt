### 2024 FDI Surge
- Egypt FDI reached $46.1 billion in 2024, up from $9.8 billion in 2023
- Main driver: $35 billion Ras El Hekma deal with Abu Dhabi sovereign wealth fund ADQ (February 2024)
- Egypt remained Africa's largest FDI recipient

### FDI by Source Country (2023/24 fiscal year)
- United Arab Emirates: 68.7% (dominant source)
- United States: 5.3%
- United Kingdom: 5.2%
- Italy: 3.7%

### FDI by Sector (December 2024 - GAFI)
- Industry sector: EGP 9,674.6 million (40.1% of total investments)
- Services sector: second highest inflows

## FDI Attractiveness Factors

1. Strategic geographical location at crossroads of Europe, Asia, and Africa
2. Proximity to Suez Canal (logistics and trade hub)
3. Low-cost and relatively qualified labor force
4. Large domestic market (population over 100 million)
5. High tourism potential
6. Significant energy resources, especially natural gas
7. Public works policy creating investment opportunities
8. Diversified economy (energy, tourism, Suez Canal revenues, industry)
9. Government reform willingness and improving business climate

## Market Disadvantages and Challenges

1. Political tensions creating uncertainty
2. Regional security situation
3. Fragile banking system
4. High poverty and persistent unemployment
5. Insufficient infrastructure
6. Large public sector and excessive bureaucracy
7. Shortage of skilled labor
8. Complicated customs procedures and non-tariff trade barriers
9. Bureaucracy and lack of transparency
10. Inconsistent law enforcement
11. Foreign exchange access difficulties for profit repatriation and imports
12. Corruption concerns
13. Intellectual property protection weaknesses
14. Sectoral restrictions (joint ventures required in oil & gas and real estate)
15. Import law requiring import companies to be 51% Egyptian-owned

## Investment Support Policies and Incentives

### Institutional Reforms
- GAFI established in 2004 to attract investment
- Average tariffs reduced by 35%
- Customs procedures simplified
- IMF-supported economic reforms (VAT, fuel and electricity subsidy reductions)

### Legal Framework
- New Investment Law (2017): performance requirements for labor-intensive and geographically targeted projects
- New Company Law and Bankruptcy Law (2018)
- New Customs Law (2020): 2% unified preferential tariff on machinery, equipment, and apparatus for establishment under Investment Law
- Green Hydrogen Law (Law No. 2 of 2024): incentives for green hydrogen projects including desalination and hydrogen facilities
  - Projects must complete within 5 years (expansions within 7 years)
  - Requirements: 70% external financing (FDI), at least 20% local inputs, contribution to local development
  - Incentives: 33-55% income tax cash investment deduction, VAT exemption on exports and equipment/raw materials, property tax reductions, "Golden License" for approved projects

### Special Economic Zones
- More liberal business regulations
- Higher administrative efficiency
- Tax incentives
- Simplified registration and customs procedures

### Investment Protection
- Bilateral investment treaties with over 100 countries
- Coverage: most EU countries, United States, African, Middle Eastern, Asian countries, Mercosur countries

## Strategic Implications for Investors

Opportunities:
- Regional hub for manufacturing and logistics
- Green energy transition (solar, wind, green hydrogen)
- Digital economy and fintech
- Tourism and real estate development
- Infrastructure and mega projects
- Export platform to EU, Africa, Arab markets via FTAs

Risks to manage:
- Foreign exchange availability and repatriation
- Bureaucratic and regulatory complexity
- Regional security dynamics
- Skills gaps in specialized fields
- Corruption and IP protection
