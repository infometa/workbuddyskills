# Suez Canal Economic Impact and Revenue Analysis
# Source: Central Bank of Egypt, Amwal Al Ghad, AGBI, Ecomnewsmed
# Retrieved: 2026-07-09

## Overview

The Suez Canal is one of Egypt's most critical economic assets, serving as a vital artery for global commerce and maritime operations. It connects the Mediterranean Sea to the Red Sea, providing the shortest maritime route between Europe and Asia.

## Economic Significance

- Accounts for approximately 8% of world trade
- Red Sea corridor accounts for about 12-15% of global trade
- Nearly 30% of container traffic passes through the region
- Suez Canal revenues are Egypt's third largest financial source
- Shipping from Egypt is 7 days less to the USA than from China
- 50% cheaper shipping costs compared to the UAE

## Revenue Performance

### FY 2024/2025
- Suez Canal revenues: $3.6 billion
- Year-on-year decline: 45.5% (from $6.6 billion in FY 2023/2024)
- Net tonnage: 482.8 million tons (down 55.1%)
- Transiting ships: approximately 12,400 vessels (down 38.5%)

### Second Half FY 2024/2025 (January-June 2025)
- Suez Canal receipts: approximately $1.80 billion
- Slight decline of 1.4% compared to $1.83 billion in same period of 2024
- Indicates some stabilization in H2 despite H1 disruption

### Calendar Year 2024
- Some reports indicated up to 62% year-on-year revenue drop in 2024
- Number of ships using the waterway dropped approximately 50% in 2024

## Causes of Revenue Decline

### Regional Conflicts
- Houthi attacks on vessels through Bab al-Mandab Strait
- Red Sea security crisis
- Shipping companies rerouting around Cape of Good Hope
- Increased insurance costs and risk premiums

### Global Shipping Disruptions
- Container ships avoiding Red Sea route
- Alternative routes adding time and cost
- Decreased vessel traffic through Suez Canal

## Recovery Signs (Late 2025 - Early 2026)

### Q4 2025
- Suez Canal revenues rose by 24.5% in Q4 2025
- Vessel traffic increased by 9%
- Indicates gradual recovery as global shipping returns

### Regional Revenue Growth
- SCZone revenues jumped 38% year-on-year despite traffic downturn
- Growth driven by industrial zone development and investment deals
- Diversification reducing pure transit dependency

## Strategic Implications

### Economic Vulnerability
- Heavy reliance on Suez Canal revenues exposes Egypt to regional geopolitical shocks
- Revenue volatility affects foreign reserves and fiscal position
- Need for economic diversification

### Investment Opportunities
- SCZone development creating manufacturing and logistics hubs
- Targeted sectors: textiles, automotive, petrochemicals, data centers, solar PV, pharmaceuticals
- One-stop-shop services: company incorporation within 48 hours
- Integrated infrastructure: roads, railways, tunnels, power, water desalination

### Competitive Advantages
- Strategic location on main Europe-Asia trade route
- Access to 2 billion consumers through Egypt's FTA network
- World-class port infrastructure
- Lower shipping times and costs compared to alternatives

## Risk Factors

- Regional security instability
- Continued Houthi attacks or new conflict escalations
- Global trade slowdown
- Alternative route competition
- Energy transition affecting oil and LNG shipping volumes

## Long-term Outlook

- Recovery depends on regional security stabilization
- SCZone development can offset pure transit revenue volatility
- Expansion of industrial zones and logistics facilities
- Integration with Egypt Vision 2030 infrastructure plans
- Potential for green energy and manufacturing hubs along the canal corridor
