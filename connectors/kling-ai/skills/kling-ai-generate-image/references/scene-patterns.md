# Image scene patterns

Select the smallest pattern that matches the user's destination. User-supplied facts and brand assets override every suggestion.

## Product and commerce

- **Catalog/studio:** faithful product geometry, neutral or brand-color sweep, controlled softbox reflections, grounded shadow, clean margins, no decorative clutter.
- **Lifestyle:** show the product being used in a believable environment; make hands, scale, and interaction physically plausible.
- **Close-up demonstration:** macro or tight crop around texture, mechanism, application, or material finish; keep enough context to identify the product.
- **Hero banner:** wide composition, product on one power third, deliberate negative space for real layout text, strong foreground/background separation.
- **Marketplace secondary image:** one benefit or use case per frame; avoid unverified badges, measurements, claims, or fake UI.
- **Conceptual product:** surreal suspension, liquid/splash, sculptural set, miniature world, or CGI-like material treatment while preserving the real product and label.
- **Restyle:** lock subject geometry and requested copy; change only season, environment, palette, lighting, or styling.

## Advertising and social

- **Static ad:** one hook, one subject, one visual proof, and clear copy-safe space. Avoid making the image itself carry long marketing copy.
- **UGC still:** casual camera height, believable home/street setting, natural imperfection, authentic handling; do not fabricate testimonials.
- **Social carousel:** define a shared palette, lens, lighting, margins, and product scale, then give each slide one distinct role.
- **Pinterest/editorial pin:** vertical storytelling, tactile detail, layered depth, aspirational but plausible styling.
- **Campaign key visual:** iconic silhouette, memorable color mechanism, one dominant visual metaphor, simplified background.

## People and identity

- **Portrait/editorial:** specify framing, expression, gaze, wardrobe, background, key/fill/rim lighting, and texture. Keep skin and facial structure natural unless stylization is requested.
- **Reference identity:** identify each reference by role and order. Lock bone structure, skin tone, hairline, distinctive features, and age presentation without beautifying or averaging.
- **Fashion/try-on:** preserve garment cut, material, pattern, fastening, logo placement, and fit; use plausible anatomy and fabric tension.

## Covers and thumbnails

- **YouTube 16:9:** one dominant face/object, strong information gap, bright subject separation, minimal clutter, readable at small size, truthful to the video.
- **Shorts/story 9:16:** keep faces and key action in the upper two-thirds; reserve lower safe area for platform UI.
- **Poster:** one visual thesis, deliberate hierarchy, clean copy area, recognizable silhouette, controlled palette.
- Do not copy a reference thumbnail's exact identity or composition. Extract only generic energy, framing, palette, and layout logic.

## Concept selection

When the user requests ideas, propose three materially different routes, for example:

1. Product truth — literal demonstration or faithful hero shot.
2. Human outcome — lifestyle moment showing use and emotion.
3. Visual metaphor — symbolic, surreal, or scale-based concept.

Label each route with intended use, focal subject, composition, palette, and why it communicates quickly.
